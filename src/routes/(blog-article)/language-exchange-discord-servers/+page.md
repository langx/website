Discord is one of the best free places to practice speaking, because many servers have voice channels you can drop into at any hour. It is also noisy, and server lists are full of abandoned or mislabeled communities. This guide lists the servers we could verify, explains how to evaluate any server in five minutes, and covers the etiquette and safety basics. For the bigger picture on where to find partners, see [how to find a language exchange partner](/how-to-find-a-language-exchange-partner).

A few notes on the list:

- **Language-pair servers** (Spanish-English, English-Japanese, Korean-English) are the classic language exchange setup: half the members are native speakers of one language, half of the other, so everyone has something to offer.
- **Multi-language servers** such as r/Language_Exchange's server and Language Sloth are good if you learn a less common language, or more than one.
- **Learning-focused servers** like the French and German ones lean toward study and discussion, with native speakers who help, rather than a strict 50/50 exchange. They are still excellent for asking questions and joining voice chats.

Servers change names, merge and close. If an invite stops working, search for the server's name in Discord discovery rather than trusting an old link on a blog, including this one.

## How to find more language exchange Discord servers

1. **Use Discord's server discovery.** In the Discord app, open Discover and search for your target language or "language exchange." Discovery only lists community servers that meet Discord's requirements, which filters out some of the junk.
2. **Check a server list.** [Disboard](https://disboard.org) lets you search by tags such as "language-exchange," "spanish" or "korean." Listings are written by server owners, so treat them as advertising.
3. **Look at the communities you already use.** Subreddits for specific languages, and teachers or YouTubers you follow, often have official servers linked in their sidebars or descriptions. An official link is more trustworthy than a random list.

## Discord etiquette for language exchange

### Read the rules and pick your roles

Most servers ask you to accept the rules and choose roles for your native language, the languages you are learning and often your level. Do this first; it unlocks channels and tells people how to talk to you.

### Lurk, then introduce yourself

Spend a day reading. Then post a short introduction in the right channel: your languages and levels, your time zone, what you want to practice, and one or two interests. Write part of it in your target language. People are far more likely to invite you to voice after they have seen you around.

### Use the right channel for the right language

Language-pair servers usually split channels by language. Writing English in a Spanish-only channel is the fastest way to annoy the regulars.

### Ask before you correct

Some people want every mistake fixed; others just want to chat. Offer, don't impose. When you do correct, rewrite the sentence rather than just saying "wrong." In a one-to-one app you can simply hold a message to correct it, but on Discord you will usually quote the message and reply with the fixed version.

### Voice channel manners

- Join muted, listen for a minute, then say hello.
- Use push-to-talk if you have background noise.
- Keep turns short and ask questions so others get a chance to speak.
- In exchange channels, switch languages when the channel's rules say to, so both sides get practice.
- Leave a quick "thanks, bye" instead of vanishing mid-conversation.

If voice chats with strangers make you nervous, start in text, then join a voice channel with people you have already chatted with. ## Staying safe on language exchange servers

Most people on these servers are there to learn. A few are not, and Discord's open structure makes it easy for them to message you.

- **Limit direct messages.** Discord lets you turn off direct messages from server members, for all servers or per server, in your privacy settings. Genuine partners can ask in a public channel first.
- **Be wary of instant DMs.** Someone who messages you privately seconds after you join, without ever posting publicly, is a red flag.
- **Never click unexpected links** or "verify your account" prompts from other members. Account-stealing scams are common on Discord.
- **Report and block.** Server moderators can act on reports; use them.
- **Keep money out of it.** No legitimate language partner asks for money, gift cards or crypto.

<Callout type="warning">
If someone moves from language practice to romance, money or pressure to switch apps, stop replying and report them to the server's moderators. It is not rude; it is how good servers stay good.
</Callout>

## FAQ

### What is the best Discord server for language exchange?

It depends on your language pair. For Spanish and English, the Spanish-English Learning Server is one of the largest; for Japanese, the English-Japanese Language Exchange; for Korean, 한E (HanE). For many languages at once, try r/Language_Exchange's official server or Language Sloth.

### Are language exchange Discord servers free?

Yes. Joining and using the servers in this guide is free. Some servers offer optional perks for supporters, but you never need them to practice.

### How do I find a language exchange partner on Discord?

Choose your language roles, introduce yourself in the introductions channel with your languages, level, time zone and interests, and join voice channels at busy times. When you click with someone, ask if they want to practice regularly.

### Is Discord good for speaking practice?

Yes, it is one of the best free options, because active servers have voice channels open around the clock. Join muted, listen first, and start with short, simple contributions.

### Is the LangX Discord a language exchange server?

No. It is the community server for the LangX project, for feedback, updates and open source contributors. Language exchange happens in the LangX app.
