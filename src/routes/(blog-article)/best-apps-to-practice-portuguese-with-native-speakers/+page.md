## Brazilian or European Portuguese?

Brazil is home to the large majority of the world's Portuguese speakers, so most partners you meet on any app will be Brazilian. Portugal, Angola, Mozambique, Cape Verde and other countries speak Portuguese too. Speakers understand each other, but here is what you will notice:

- **Pronunciation.** European Portuguese swallows many unstressed vowels, so it sounds faster and more closed; Brazilian Portuguese keeps vowels open and is usually easier for beginners to follow.
- **"You".** In Brazil, **você** is the everyday "you" in most regions. In Portugal, **tu** is normal between friends, and você is used more carefully.
- **Continuous tense.** Brazil says *estou falando* ("I am speaking"); Portugal says *estou a falar*.
- **Everyday words.** Bus is *ônibus* in Brazil and *autocarro* in Portugal; train is *trem* or *comboio*; breakfast is *café da manhã* or *pequeno-almoço*; a cell phone is a *celular* or a *telemóvel*.

**How to choose:** pick the variety of the place you will actually use it. Moving to Lisbon or Porto? European, and look for Portuguese partners from the start, since they are fewer. Brazilian friends, family, music or work? Brazilian. No preference? Brazilian is easier to find partners and material for, and switching later is very doable.

## How to pick an app to practice Portuguese

**Filter by country.** Because most Portuguese speakers on exchange apps are Brazilian, learners of European Portuguese need an app with a country filter, or a tutor they can choose by country.

**Match the format to your level.** Text and voice messages give beginners time to think. Once you can write simple messages, live calls train you for the speed of real speech, which matters a lot for European Portuguese.

**Be ready to give back.** Many Brazilians and Portuguese are keen to practice English, and plenty learn Spanish, French or German too, so partners usually come quickly.

## Language exchange apps for Portuguese

### HelloTalk

[HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com) is the largest exchange community, and Portuguese speakers, mostly Brazilian, are easy to find. Its **Moments** feed lets you post a few sentences and get corrections from several native speakers, which is a quick way to learn how Brazilians actually phrase things.

- **Best for:** finding partners fast, and live group audio in Voicerooms.
- **Keep in mind:** the free tier has ads and daily caps on translation and some tools; VIP removes them.

### Lingbe

[Lingbe](https://www.lingbe.com), from a company in Madrid, lists Portuguese among its languages. Instead of chat, you press a button and are connected to a live voice call with someone who speaks it. You earn practice time by helping others with your language. It is a good way to get over the fear of speaking. Compare: [LangX vs Lingbe](/open-source-alternative-to-lingbe).

## Portuguese-specific resources worth adding

**[Practice Portuguese](https://www.practiceportuguese.com)** has focused only on European Portuguese since 2012, on the web and in apps for iOS and Android. It has structured units from A1 to B2, short audio and video clips of native speakers, podcast episodes with transcripts, and spaced-repetition review. Much of its audio and video is free; the full course is Premium. If you are learning the Portuguese of Portugal, it fills the gap that Brazil-heavy apps leave.

**Easy Portuguese**, part of the Easy Languages network on YouTube, films street interviews in Brazil with subtitles in Portuguese and English, plus slower "super easy" episodes for beginners.

**Frequency lists and tests.** Our [most common Portuguese words](/tools/most-common-words/portuguese) is drawn from Brazilian and European subtitles alike, so it covers the core both share. Check your level with the [Portuguese vocabulary test](/tools/vocabulary-test/portuguese).

**If you already speak Spanish.** You will understand a lot of written Portuguese from day one, but speaking is harder, and false friends are everywhere: *esquisito* means "strange", not "exquisite"; *polvo* means "octopus", not "dust"; and *embaraçada* means "embarrassed", while Spanish *embarazada* means "pregnant". ## Your first message in Portuguese

Keep it short, friendly and specific to their profile. In Brazilian Portuguese:

> Oi, Ana! Tudo bem? Meu nome é Sam e sou do Canadá. Estou aprendendo português e vi que você está aprendendo inglês. Vamos nos ajudar? Eu te ajudo com o inglês e você corrige o meu português.

And to a partner in Portugal:

> Olá, Ana! Tudo bem? Chamo-me Sam e sou do Canadá. Estou a aprender português e vi que estás a aprender inglês. Eu ajudo-te com o inglês e tu corriges o meu português?

In English: "Hi Ana! How are you? My name is Sam and I'm from Canada. I'm learning Portuguese and I saw you're learning English. I help you with English and you correct my Portuguese?" Notice *aprendendo* against *a aprender*, *você* against *tu*, and where the pronoun goes: *te ajudo* in Brazil, *ajudo-te* in Portugal.

Three more phrases you will use every week, the same in both varieties:

- **Como se diz... em português?** How do you say... in Portuguese?
- **Isso soa natural?** Does that sound natural?
- **Mais devagar, por favor.** More slowly, please.

## Tips for Portuguese learners on exchange apps

**Tell partners which variety you want.** Put "learning Brazilian Portuguese" or "learning European Portuguese" in your profile. Partners will correct you toward their own variety otherwise, and a correction from the other side of the Atlantic may not be a mistake at all.

**Practice the nasal sounds out loud.** Words like *pão* (bread), *mãe* (mother) and *não* (no) have nasal vowels English does not. Send voice messages and ask your partner to record the same words back.

**Set up a Portuguese keyboard.** Typing ã, õ, ç, é and ê properly takes seconds, and the marks matter: *avó* is grandmother, *avô* is grandfather.

**Ask for corrections on the tricky bits.** Ser versus estar, the personal infinitive, and where object pronouns go are classic learner mistakes. Name them and partners will watch for them.

## FAQ

### What is the best app to practice Portuguese with native speakers?

HelloTalk and Tandem have the most Portuguese speakers, most of them Brazilian. LangX suits learners who want unlimited corrections and no ads, and Lingbe is good for instant voice calls. For European Portuguese, pair a tutor from Portugal with Practice Portuguese.

### Should I learn Brazilian or European Portuguese?

Learn the one you will use. If you have no preference, Brazilian Portuguese is easier to find partners and material for. Speakers of both understand each other, so nothing you learn is wasted.

### How do I find European Portuguese speakers on exchange apps?

Use a country filter to show partners in Portugal, say in your profile that you are learning European Portuguese, or book a tutor from Portugal on italki or Preply.

### Can I practice Portuguese speaking for free?

Yes. HelloTalk and Tandem include free calls with partners, and Lingbe gives some free practice time for live calls. LangX supports voice messages but not live calls.
