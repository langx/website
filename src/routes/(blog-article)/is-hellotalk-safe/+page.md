HelloTalk is reasonably safe for language learning if you use the same care you would on any app that connects you with strangers: it has in-app reporting and blocking, a moderation team, limits on how many new people you can message, and separate spaces for users under 18. The real risks are the ones HelloTalk itself warns about, namely scammers and people looking for dating rather than a language exchange, plus a privacy trade-off worth understanding: the free app is ad-supported and the company is based in Hong Kong.

Sources: HelloTalk's help center, terms and privacy policy at the time of writing (September 2026), plus public user reviews. We make a different app, LangX, and mention it once at the end.

## HelloTalk's safety features at a glance

| Safety area | What HelloTalk says it does |
| --- | --- |
| Signing up | Instant sign-up; in some registration scenarios it may ask for video verification to prevent fake accounts and impersonation |
| Age rules | Children under 12 are not allowed; under-18s need a parent's or guardian's approval |

| Calls | HelloTalk says it does not monitor, record or broadcast voice or video calls |

*Table: HelloTalk's official safety features, from its FAQ, terms and privacy policy (September 2026).*

## How reporting and blocking work on HelloTalk

To **report** someone, open the chat settings (the gear icon at the top right of a chat) or the **...** menu on their profile, choose **Report**, pick a reason and add details. HelloTalk asks you to attach the conversation history as evidence. According to its FAQ, the Community Safety Team reviews each report and warns or bans the account if the person broke the guidelines, and the person is not told that it was you. **Block** sits in the same menus. Blocking and reporting are separate, so for a scammer or harasser, do both.

HelloTalk's guidelines ban harassment and stalking, sexual content, phishing links, spam, fraud, using other people's photos and multiple accounts used to harass. On Moments they also ban sharing IDs or QR codes for other apps, and posts on politically or religiously sensitive issues.

## Is HelloTalk safe for teenagers?

HelloTalk's rules are in the table above: no children under 12, parental approval under 18, and under-18s kept to other minors across search, Moments, comments, livestreams and Voicerooms. Its terms also forbid soliciting personal information from anyone under 18. All of this depends on people entering their real age, so if you are a parent, set up the profile together and go through the tips below.

## Common problems HelloTalk users report

Most people on HelloTalk are there to learn, and many describe genuinely useful exchanges. Three problems still come up repeatedly; HelloTalk acknowledges the first two itself.

### Scams

HelloTalk's own safety FAQ describes three scam patterns it sees:

- **Romance scams:** a fake profile, often presented as a middle-aged man from a Western country working abroad, builds a relationship quickly and then needs money for an emergency, customs fees, a visa or medical bills, while avoiding video calls.
- **Investment or currency scams:** a fake profile, often presented as a young woman running a business, pushes cryptocurrency or stock investments and refuses video verification.
- **"Butchering pig" scams:** a friendly, successful-seeming person builds trust, moves the chat to WhatsApp or WeChat, and eventually steers you to a fake investment or gambling platform.

HelloTalk's advice is simple: never send money or personal details, keep conversations on HelloTalk, and report anyone you suspect.

### Dating-style messages

HelloTalk is a language exchange app, but it is also a large open social network with instant sign-up, and some members use it to flirt or look for dates. Public reviews, for example on Trustpilot, include complaints about people who ignore boundaries, ask personal questions and request photos early. Sexual content and harassment are against HelloTalk's rules, so reporting is the right response, and blocking stops the messages immediately.

### Account bans and content rules

The other recurring complaint in public reviews is the opposite problem: users who say their account was restricted or banned without a clear explanation, sometimes after posting about political topics. HelloTalk's Moments rules do prohibit "politically or religiously sensitive issues", so if you want to discuss politics, one-to-one chat with a partner you trust is the safer place for it.

## What HelloTalk's privacy policy says

HelloTalk's privacy policy (last updated 11 September 2026 at the time of writing) is detailed. The highlights:

- **Who runs it:** the policy names Global Connection Technologies Limited, with an address in Hong Kong, and describes HelloTalk as a team based in Hong Kong and Shenzhen, China. The terms of service are governed by Hong Kong law.
- **Where data goes:** HelloTalk says it is a global platform and that data is transferred to countries outside your home country. It does not name specific server locations in the policy.
- **What it collects:** account details (such as email, gender, age, nationality and languages), location data including GPS and IP address when permitted, device identifiers including advertising IDs such as IDFA and GAID, and, when you use video verification at sign-up, the video, which it says it keeps for no longer than 180 days.
- **Messages:** HelloTalk says messages are deleted from its servers once delivered, and undelivered messages are kept encrypted for up to 30 days. It does not describe chats as end-to-end encrypted; it says transmissions are secured with SSL/TLS.
- **Calls:** it says it will not monitor, record or broadcast voice or video chats.
- **Advertising:** to personalize ads it may share information such as interests, age and device type with ad networks it names, including AdMob by Google, Meta's Audience Network, TopOn and Pangle. You can turn off personalized ads in **Settings → Privacy → Personalized Ads**. HelloTalk VIP removes ads altogether; see [Is HelloTalk free?](/is-hellotalk-free)
- **Your rights:** you can delete your data and your account in the app, and contact HelloTalk about your data at the addresses listed in the policy.

In practice: turn off personalized ads if you do not want them, grant location only if you use location features, and treat anything on Moments as public.

## How to stay safe on HelloTalk

1. **Keep it about language.** Someone who never uses their target language or asks about yours may be after something else.
2. **Stay in the app** until you know someone well. Pressure to move to WhatsApp, Telegram or WeChat early is a common first step in a scam.
3. **Never send money, gift cards or crypto**, and never "invest" anywhere a chat partner suggests.
4. **Keep personal details private:** address, workplace, ID documents, finances.
5. **Use a video call** before trusting someone new. Scammers often avoid video.
6. **Report and block freely.** It is anonymous and protects the next person too.

## FAQ

### Is HelloTalk safe to use?

For most people, yes, with sensible precautions. HelloTalk has reporting, blocking, a moderation team and separate spaces for minors. The main risks are scammers and people seeking dates, which HelloTalk warns about itself. Keep chats in the app and never send money.

### Is HelloTalk safe for minors?

HelloTalk does not allow children under 12, requires parental approval for users under 18, and says under-18s can only find and interact with other minors across search, Moments, livestreams and Voicerooms. This relies on users giving their real age.

### Does HelloTalk read your messages?

HelloTalk's privacy policy says messages are deleted from its servers after delivery (undelivered ones are kept for up to 30 days) and that it does not monitor or record calls. Moderators review reports, which is why HelloTalk asks you to attach the conversation when you report someone.

### What should I do if I get scammed on HelloTalk?

Stop replying, report the account in the app with the conversation attached, and block it. If you sent money, contact your bank or payment provider immediately and report it to your local police or fraud authority.
