The Bulgarian alphabet has 30 letters, all of them Cyrillic. It is a true alphabet, so vowels and consonants are written as equal letters, and words are spelled close to how they sound. If you know Russian Cyrillic you know most of it already; the differences are that Bulgarian has no Ы, Э or Ё, uses Ъ as a real vowel, and reads Щ as "sht".

This guide takes the letters, names and sounds from our free [Bulgarian alphabet chart](/tools/alphabet/bulgarian), and every practice word comes from our list of the [10,000 most common Bulgarian words](/tools/most-common-words/bulgarian), with its rank.

## What kind of script is Bulgarian?

Bulgarian is written in the Cyrillic script, the same family of letters used for Russian, Ukrainian, Serbian and Macedonian. Cyrillic has deep roots in Bulgaria: it was developed in the First Bulgarian Empire around the late 9th and early 10th centuries, by the school that followed the missionaries Cyril and Methodius, and it is named after Cyril. Bulgaria still celebrates the alphabet every year on May 24. When Bulgaria joined the European Union on 1 January 2007, Cyrillic became the EU's third official script, alongside Latin and Greek.

The modern alphabet dates from the spelling reform of 1945, which removed two old letters, ѣ (yat) and ѫ (big yus). What remains is the smallest of the Cyrillic alphabets in our charts.

Fewer letters does not mean fewer sounds. Bulgarian simply doesn't need Ы or Э, and it writes the "yo" sound with two letters instead of one (more on that below).

## The Bulgarian vowels

Bulgarian has six vowel sounds, and eight letters to write them: six plain vowels and two that add a "y" sound in front.

| Letter | Name | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| А а | a | a in father |
| Е е | e | e in bet |
| И и | i | ee in see |
| О о | o | o in more |
| У у | u | oo in boot |
| Ъ ъ | er goljam | u in but |
| Ю ю | yu | u in use |
| Я я | ya | ya in yard |

Two things catch Russian learners here. First, Bulgarian Е is a plain *e*, as in *bet*. It does not start with a "y" sound the way Russian Е does, so не (no, #3 on our list) is simply *ne*. Second, Ъ is a full vowel, not a silent sign. It sounds roughly like the *u* in *but* and appears in some of the most common words in the language, such as съм (I am, #20) and път (road, #127).

Stress is not written in Bulgarian and can fall on any syllable, so learn each new word with its stress. In relaxed speech, unstressed vowels also weaken a little: an unstressed а can drift toward the sound of ъ, and an unstressed о toward у. You'll hear this long before you need to produce it.

## The Bulgarian consonants

Here are the 21 consonants from our chart, plus the one sign. Bulgarian names each consonant by adding ъ after it, so Б is *bŭ*, В is *vŭ* and so on, which makes the names easy to remember.

| Letter | Name | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Б б | bŭ | b |
| В в | vŭ | v |
| Г г | gŭ | g (always hard, as in go) |
| Д д | dŭ | d |
| Ж ж | zhŭ | s in measure |
| З з | zŭ | z |
| Й й | i kratko | y in boy |
| К к | kŭ | k |
| Л л | lŭ | l |
| М м | mŭ | m |
| Н н | nŭ | n |
| П п | pŭ | p |
| Р р | rŭ | rolled r |
| С с | sŭ | s |
| Т т | tŭ | t |
| Ф ф | fŭ | f |
| Х х | hŭ | ch in loch |
| Ц ц | tsŭ | ts in cats |
| Ч ч | chŭ | ch in chair |
| Ш ш | shŭ | sh |
| Щ щ | shtŭ | sht |
| Ь ь | er malŭk | silent, softens |

A note on Х: it is the same sound as Russian х, like the *ch* in Scottish *loch*, made at the back of the mouth rather than a breathy English *h*.

## Bulgarian vs Russian: what is different

If you already read Russian, this is the section to study. The two alphabets share 30 letters, and Russian adds three more, but several shared letters are used differently. You can compare the two charts side by side: [Bulgarian](/tools/alphabet/bulgarian) and [Russian](/tools/alphabet/russian), or read our [Russian alphabet guide](/russian-alphabet-guide).

| Letter | In Bulgarian | In Russian |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ъ | a vowel, like u in but | silent, separates sounds |
| Щ | sht | a long, soft sh |
| Е | e in bet | ye in yes, or softens the consonant before it |
| Ь | only before о, as in ьо | common, softens the consonant before it |
| Ы, Э, Ё | not used | used |

The "yo" sound is a good example. Russian has the letter Ё for it. Bulgarian writes йо at the start of a syllable and ьо after a consonant: шофьор (driver, #1,946) and синьо (blue, #2,317). The ь in those words is the only job the soft sign has in Bulgarian.

Щ is the difference you'll meet first, because ще (will, #9) is one of the most common words in the language. It is said *shte*. The same letter gives още (yet, more, #57), *O-shte*, and щастие (happiness, #1,105), *SHTAS-ti-e*.

Other Cyrillic alphabets differ again. [Ukrainian](/tools/alphabet/ukrainian) has 33 letters, including І, Ї, Є and Ґ, and its Щ is closer to *shch*. [Macedonian](/tools/alphabet/macedonian), Bulgarian's closest relative, has 31 letters, adds Ѓ, Ќ, Ѕ, Љ, Њ and Џ, and drops both Ъ and Щ. One word shows it well:

## The letters that look Latin but aren't

These are the real traps for English speakers. Some Cyrillic letters look exactly like Latin letters and sound the same, and some look the same but sound completely different.

The honest ones, which look and sound like their Latin twins: **А, Е, К, М, О, Т**.

The false friends:

| Letter | Looks like | Actually sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| В в | B | v |
| Н н | H | n |
| Р р | P | r |
| С с | C | s |
| У у | y | oo in boot |
| Х х | X | ch in Scottish loch |

A few lowercase letters are traps of their own: п looks like an *n* but is *p*, и looks like a backwards *N* and is *ee*, and я looks like a backwards *R* and is *ya*.

Put them together and you get words that read like nonsense in Latin letters. Try ресторант (#1,451): read as Latin it looks like *pectopaht*, but it is *res-to-RANT*, restaurant. хотел (#1,055) is *ho-TEL*, hotel, and такси (#1,327) is *tak-SI*, taxi.

One more thing to expect on signs and menus in Bulgaria: many fonts there use lowercase shapes closer to handwriting, so д can look like a *g* and т like an *m*. They are the same letters, just drawn differently.

Start with да, не and мама: nothing new except that Н is *n*. Then кафе and вода, where В and Ф appear. Then път and добър for ъ, and ще, още and баща for щ. By благодаря you are reading a four-syllable word with я at the end.

## FAQ

### How many letters are in the Bulgarian alphabet?

Thirty: eight vowel letters (а, е, и, о, у, ъ, ю, я), 21 consonants, and the soft sign ь. It is the smallest of the Cyrillic alphabets in our charts; Russian and Ukrainian have 33, Macedonian 31.

### Is the Bulgarian alphabet the same as the Russian one?

No, but it is close. Bulgarian uses 30 of Russian's 33 letters and has none of its own; it leaves out Ы, Э and Ё. Some shared letters are read differently: Ъ is a vowel in Bulgarian, Щ is sht, and Е has no y sound in front.

### How do you pronounce Ъ in Bulgarian?

Roughly like the *u* in *but*, or the vowel in the second syllable of *sofa*. It is a real vowel, never silent, and it appears in common words like съм (I am) and път (road).

### Which Bulgarian letters look like English letters?

А, Е, К, М, О and Т look and sound much like their English twins. В, Н, Р, С, У and Х look like English letters but stand for v, n, r, s, oo and the ch in loch.

### How long does it take to learn the Bulgarian alphabet?

It depends on how much you practice, but it is one of the quicker alphabets to learn: 30 letters, several already familiar, and spelling that follows the sound. Learning it in the order above and reading real words early makes the letters stick.
