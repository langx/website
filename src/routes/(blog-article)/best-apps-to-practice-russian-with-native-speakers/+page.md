The best apps to practice Russian with native speakers are **HelloTalk** and **Tandem**, the two largest exchange communities, where Russian speakers learning English are easy to find. **LangX** suits learners who want every message open to correction and no ads, **Conversation Exchange** helps you meet Russian speakers in person, and **italki** gives you a paid tutor on video. For listening, the free **Learn Russian with Max** podcast and **Easy Russian** videos fill the gaps between conversations.

Russian is a language where a partner pays off quickly. Its six cases change the ends of nouns, adjectives and pronouns, and the stress in a word moves around without any mark on the page. You can study the tables for months, but a native speaker who corrects *с моя подруга* to *с моей подругой* teaches the instrumental case in one line.

## How to pick an app to practice Russian

**Learn Cyrillic before you choose.** Russian uses a 33-letter Cyrillic alphabet, and it takes most learners a few days, not months. Five letters look like Latin ones but are not: В is *v*, Р is *r*, С is *s*, Н is *n*, У is *u*. Our [Russian alphabet](/tools/alphabet/russian) page lists every letter with a rough English sound. Once you can read, text chat becomes useful immediately.

**Russian speakers live in many countries.** Russian is spoken in Russia, and also widely in Belarus, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, and by large communities in the Baltic states, Israel, Germany, the US and elsewhere. Standard Russian is very uniform, so a partner from Almaty or Riga will teach you the same language as one from Moscow, with small differences in accent and vocabulary.

**Know what works where your partner is.** Some tools behave differently depending on the country. Preply, for example, blocked tutors located in Russia in 2022, so its Russian tutors live elsewhere. Since August 2025, Russian authorities have restricted voice and video calls on WhatsApp and Telegram. If your partner lives in Russia, do not assume a call will work on any given app; agree on something that works for both of you, and fall back on voice messages if needed.

**Look for correction tools.** Russian mistakes are mostly endings: the wrong case after a preposition, the wrong gender on an adjective, the wrong aspect of a verb. An app where your partner can mark up your exact sentence shows you which ending changed.

**Match the format to your level.** Text chat gives you time to work out cases. Voice messages matter more in Russian than in many languages, because stress is not written.

## Language exchange apps for Russian

### HelloTalk

[HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com) is the biggest exchange community, and Russian is well represented. Its **Moments** feed lets you post a few sentences in Cyrillic and get corrections from several native speakers, handy for checking cases and word order in one go.

- **Best for:** volume, and live group audio in Voicerooms.
- **Keep in mind:** the free tier has ads and daily caps on translation and some tools; VIP removes them.

Compare: [LangX vs HelloTalk](/open-source-alternative-to-hellotalk).

### Tandem

[Tandem](https://tandem.net) reviews new members and focuses on one-to-one conversations with text, voice messages and calls. For Russian, a single patient partner who knows your recurring case mistakes is worth more than many short chats.

- **Best for:** a regular weekly call, and filtering for a partner in a specific country.
- **Keep in mind:** approval can take time, and some filters and unlimited translation are in Tandem Pro.

## Ty or vy in a Russian language exchange

- **Вы (vy) is the polite default** with strangers, older people and anyone in a professional setting. It takes plural verb forms, and it is sometimes capitalized (Вы) in letters to one person.
- **Ты (ty) is normal between exchange partners** of similar age, especially after the first few messages. Many younger Russian speakers use it online straight away.
- **Start with вы if you are unsure.** Switching from вы to ты is friendly; the other way round can feel cold.
- **If in doubt, ask.** *Можно на ты?* ("Can we use ty?") is the standard question.
- **Вы is also the plural "you",** whatever the relationship.

## Your first message in Russian

Keep it short, friendly and specific to their profile.

> Привет, Аня! Меня зовут Сэм, я из Канады. Я учу русский язык и увидел, что ты учишь английский. Давай помогать друг другу? Я помогу тебе с английским, а ты будешь исправлять мои ошибки.

In English: "Hi, Anya! My name is Sam, I'm from Canada. I'm learning Russian and saw that you're learning English. Shall we help each other? I'll help you with English, and you'll correct my mistakes." Notice *увидел*: past-tense verbs in Russian agree with the speaker's gender, so a woman writes *увидела*. And *с английским* puts "English" in the instrumental case after *с*. Your partner will spot both.

Three more phrases you will use every week:

- **Как сказать... по-русски?** How do you say... in Russian?
- **Это звучит естественно?** Does that sound natural?
- **Можешь говорить помедленнее?** Can you speak a bit more slowly?

## Tips for Russian learners on exchange apps

**Ask for stress marks.** Russian dictionaries and textbooks mark stress (*моло́ко*), but native speakers never write it. Ask your partner to add an accent mark on new words, or to send a short voice message with them.

**Ask for corrections by name.** Tell your partner you want help with cases after prepositions, verb aspect (*делать* vs *сделать*) and verbs of motion (*идти*, *ходить*, *ехать*). These are the mistakes learners make constantly, and a partner who knows to look for them will catch far more.

**Set up a Russian keyboard.** Every phone has one. Typing in transliteration (*privet*) works in a pinch, but your partner reads Cyrillic faster and you learn spelling by typing it. Ё is usually written as Е in everyday text, so do not worry if your partner leaves the dots off.

**Learn the soft sign early.** Ь has no sound of its own; it softens the consonant before it, and it changes meaning (*брат* is brother, *брать* is to take). Voice messages are the best way to hear it.

## FAQ

### What is the best app to practice Russian with native speakers?

HelloTalk has the largest community and Tandem is strong for a steady one-to-one partner. LangX suits learners who want unlimited corrections and no ads. For a paid teacher, italki has many Russian tutors.

### Can I practice Russian speaking for free?

Yes. HelloTalk and Tandem include free calls with partners, and Conversation Exchange helps you meet someone in person. LangX supports voice messages but not live calls. Learn Russian with Max and Easy Russian are free for listening.

### How long does it take to learn the Cyrillic alphabet?

Most learners can read slowly within a few days. Start with the letters that look Latin but sound different, then read your partner's messages aloud.

### Should I use ty or vy with a language exchange partner?

Ty is normal between exchange partners of similar age. Use vy with someone older or in a formal setting, and if you are unsure, ask "Можно на ты?"

### Can I practice Russian with speakers outside Russia?

Yes. Russian is widely spoken in Belarus, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan and by communities worldwide, and standard Russian is very uniform.
