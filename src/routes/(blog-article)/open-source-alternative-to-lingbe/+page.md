If you want an open source alternative to Lingbe, LangX is a free, open source (BSD-3) language exchange app for iOS, Android and the web built around chat: two-way matching, corrections on any message, translation inside the conversation, and voice messages. Lingbe does something different and does it well: you press a button and get a live voice call with a native speaker. So LangX vs Lingbe is really a question of whether you want to practice by writing and recording at your own pace, or by talking live right now.

This page compares the two fairly, as of September 2026. We make LangX, and we will tell you plainly when Lingbe is the better pick.

## What is Lingbe?

Lingbe is a language exchange app from lingbe SL, a company based in Madrid. At the time of writing (September 2026) it is **still active**: it is listed on both Google Play and the App Store, the Android app was last updated on September 30, 2025, and the website is online.

Its idea is simple. You press the call button and Lingbe connects you with someone who can help you practice your target language in that moment. Practice time runs on a credit called **lingos**: you get some free practice minutes when you sign up, you earn more lingos by helping people who are learning your native language, and you spend them to practice your own. There is also a paid Pro subscription and lingo packs for sale.

## What Lingbe does well

Lingbe solves the hardest part of speaking practice: actually opening your mouth.

- **Zero setup.** No scrolling profiles, no "hi, how are you?" messages that never get a reply. You press a button and you are talking. For many learners, that is exactly the push they need.
- **Real speaking, in real time.** A live call trains listening speed and spontaneous speech in a way that messages cannot. If your goal is conversation fluency, this matters.
- **A fair exchange built into the economics.** Because you earn lingos by helping other learners, the community has a built-in reason to give time as well as take it.
- **Feedback after each call.** After a conversation, partners rate your grammar, pronunciation and fluency, so you see your progress over time.
- **Keep the good partners.** If you both like each other after a call, you can chat and practice again later, like a normal messaging app.
- **Lingbe Tasks.** You can ask native speakers specific questions about their language, and answer others' questions to earn lingos.

If you already hold basic conversations and you want more live speaking time, Lingbe is a genuinely good choice.

## Where LangX differs

LangX is for the practice that happens between and around live conversations: writing, recording, getting corrected, and looking things up, at a pace you control.

### You pick who you talk to

On LangX you only see people who speak the language you are learning *and* are learning a language you speak, and you choose whom to message. Free filters cover country, age and level. Lingbe's instant matching is faster; LangX gives you more control over who your partners are.

### Corrections on the actual sentence

Lingbe's ratings tell you how you did overall. On LangX, your partner holds the exact message and corrects it, so you see what was wrong and what the right version is. Corrections are unlimited on every plan, including Free.

### No credits to manage

On LangX, conversations are not metered by the minute. The free plan limits how many *new* conversations you start (5 per rolling 24 hours) and how many translations you use (20), but replies and corrections are unlimited, so an ongoing exchange never runs out. Paid plans (Fluent and Polyglot) lift the new-conversation limit and add more translations and filters. See the [plans page](/pro).

### Lower pressure for beginners and introverts

A live call with a stranger is a big step at A1 or A2 level. Text and voice messages let you think, look words up with in-chat translation, record again, and replay. If that sounds like you, our [introvert's guide to learning a language](/mastering-a-new-language-an-introverts-guide) has more low-pressure ways to practice.

### Open source, no ads, and a web version

LangX's app and API are open source under BSD-3 on [GitHub](https://github.com/langx/langx), you can host your own instance, and there are no ads on any plan. It also runs in the browser at [app.langx.io](https://app.langx.io), not only on phones.

### Where LangX is weaker

- **No live calls.** This is the big one. If live speaking is your main goal, LangX alone will not give it to you.
- **Slower pace.** Messages can wait hours for a reply; a Lingbe call is immediate.
- **A smaller community,** so some language pairs have fewer people online.

## Who should pick which?

**Pick Lingbe if** you want live speaking practice now, you are comfortable holding at least a simple conversation, and you like the idea of earning practice time by helping others.

**Pick LangX if** you want a free Lingbe alternative that focuses on written and recorded practice with corrections and translation, you prefer choosing your partners, or you want no ads and open source code.

**Use both if** you want a complete routine. Many learners do their daily practice in chat, where mistakes get corrected precisely, then take what they learned into a live call. ## FAQ

### What is the best open source alternative to Lingbe?

LangX is an open source alternative to Lingbe. Its app and API are BSD-3 licensed on GitHub. It is chat-based rather than call-based, with two-way matching, unlimited corrections, in-chat translation and voice messages.

### Is Lingbe still working in 2026?

Yes. At the time of writing (September 2026) Lingbe is on Google Play and the App Store, and its Android app was last updated on September 30, 2025.

### LangX vs Lingbe: does LangX have voice calls?

No. LangX has voice, photo and video messages and read-aloud, but no live calls. If live calls are what you want, Lingbe or Tandem is a better fit.

### Is there a free Lingbe alternative without credits?

Yes. LangX does not meter practice by the minute. Its free plan has unlimited replies and corrections, 5 new conversations and 20 translations per rolling 24 hours, and no ads.

### Which apps like Lingbe are best for beginners?

Beginners often find text and voice messages easier than live calls. LangX, HelloTalk and Tandem all let you practice at your own pace before moving to calls.
