## Language learning trends heading into 2027

### 1. AI conversation practice is becoming standard, even on free plans

Two years ago, talking out loud to an AI in your target language was a premium novelty. By 2026 it was everywhere:

- Duolingo expanded **Video Call** with its character Lily to Android in January 2025 and to nine courses for all Max subscribers in September 2025, then made its AI **Explain My Answer** feature free for everyone from January 1, 2026.
- Babbel launched **Babbel Speak**, an AI voice trainer, in September 2025.
- Google added a **practice** mode to the Translate app in August 2025 and expanded its languages in the following months.
- Memrise added **Podchats**, unscripted AI voice conversations, in February 2026.
- Speaky, a language exchange app, now lists AI practice partners for when nobody is online.

**What we expect in 2027:** more AI speaking practice in free tiers, as Duolingo's move suggests, and less willingness to pay for AI chat on its own. That makes the human part of language exchange, which AI cannot copy, the thing worth choosing an app for. Our [AI conversation partner vs real language exchange](/ai-conversation-partner-vs-language-exchange) guide explains where each one helps.

### 2. Human plus AI hybrids inside exchange apps

The big exchange apps did not replace people with AI. They added AI around the conversation. At the time of writing, Tandem Pro includes an AI Toolkit with grammar check, a word finder and a rewrite tool, and HelloTalk offers a set of AI apps, including an AI conversation partner, alongside its community.

**What we expect in 2027:** AI as a helper that explains a correction or suggests a word, with the partner still doing the talking. The design question worth asking of any app is whether the AI supports your conversation or quietly becomes it.

### 3. Human-led lessons are moving to the premium end

Babbel stopped taking individual learners for **Babbel Live** after June 30, 2025, and now offers live classes only through Babbel for Business. Tutor marketplaces like [italki](https://www.italki.com) and [Preply](https://preply.com) carry on, but live teaching is expensive to run.

**What we expect in 2027:** paid human time becomes more clearly a premium product, while language exchange remains the main way to talk to real native speakers for free, because both people teach and nobody needs to be paid.

### 4. Voice and real-time translation

In September 2025 Apple announced Live Translation for AirPods, translating face-to-face conversations, and extended it to the EU that November. Instant translation raises a fair question: why learn at all? The answer is the same one it has always been. Translation gets you through a transaction; it does not make you part of a conversation, a friendship or a family dinner.

On the practice side, voice is where apps differ most. Lingbe is built on live calls, HelloTalk has Voicerooms, and Tandem has calls and Language Parties. LangX has voice, photo and video messages and read-aloud, but **no live calls** today, so if live speaking is your main goal for 2027, one of those apps is the better fit.

### 5. Privacy and open source get more attention

As apps add AI, analytics and recordings, it becomes more worthwhile to read what an app collects before you install it. Both Apple and Google show data-collection labels on store listings, and they are worth two minutes of your time.

Since we are asking you to check, here is LangX's own answer, plainly. LangX has **no ads and no advertising identifiers**, and its app and API are [open source under BSD-3](https://github.com/langx/langx). It does use **PostHog analytics on its EU cloud**, and on iOS and Android that includes **screen recording masked on the device** (text and images replaced by grey blocks before anything is sent). You can turn it off in **Settings → Privacy → Share usage data**. The [privacy policy](/privacy-policy) describes all of it. That is not "no tracking", and we do not claim it is.

**What we expect in 2027:** more learners weighing open code, opt-outs and store privacy labels alongside features and price.

### 6. Check that an app is still alive

Bilingua disappeared from the stores years ago, italki's free partner search is long gone, and Lingbe's Android app has not been updated since September 30, 2025. Meanwhile Speaky, now published by Altissia, was updated through 2026. Older apps change owners, slow down or close. Former users of the ones that closed can find alternatives on our [LangX vs Bilingua](/open-source-alternative-to-bilingua) and [LangX vs Lingbe](/open-source-alternative-to-lingbe) pages.

## FAQ

### What will be the best language exchange app in 2027?

Based on what is available in September 2026, HelloTalk is likely to remain the best for community size and Tandem for focused one-to-one exchange. LangX is the pick if you want open source, no ads and unlimited corrections, and Lingbe if you want instant voice calls. We will update this answer during 2027.

### What are the biggest language learning trends for 2027?

The trends to watch are AI conversation practice reaching free plans, AI helpers inside human exchange apps, live human teaching becoming a premium product, real-time voice translation, and more attention to privacy and open source. Each is based on changes already made in 2025 and 2026.

### Will AI replace language exchange partners in 2027?

We do not expect it to. AI is excellent for rehearsal and instant explanations, but it cannot replace a real person who shares their culture, remembers your conversations and reacts like a native speaker. Most learners will likely use both.

### Is LangX adding AI features?

LangX Copilot, private AI feedback in the chat, is planned for the Polyglot plan and is coming later. It has not shipped yet, and there is no public date.

### Is there a free language exchange app with no ads for 2027?

At the time of writing, LangX has no ads on any plan, including Free, where replies and corrections are unlimited and you can start 5 new conversations a day. Speaky, Conversation Exchange and InterPals also need no subscription, though all three show ads.
