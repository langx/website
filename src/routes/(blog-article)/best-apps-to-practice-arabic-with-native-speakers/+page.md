## MSA or a dialect: decide this first

**Modern Standard Arabic (MSA, الفصحى)** is the written language of books, news, signs and formal speeches, and it is the same across the Arab world. Nobody grows up speaking it at home, but every educated Arabic speaker reads and understands it. Most courses, including Duolingo's and Busuu's, teach MSA.

**The dialects (العامية)** are what people actually speak with family and friends. The big groups are:

- **Egyptian.** The most widely understood dialect, thanks to decades of Egyptian film, TV and music. A safe choice if you have no particular country in mind.
- **Levantine.** Spoken in Syria, Lebanon, Jordan and Palestine, and also widely understood, partly thanks to Syrian and Lebanese TV drama and music.
- **Gulf.** Spoken in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman. The one to pick if you live or work in the Gulf.
- **Maghrebi.** Moroccan, Algerian and Tunisian Arabic, with many French and Amazigh loanwords. The furthest from the others; speakers from the Middle East often find it hard to follow.

A simple test of the difference: "what?" is *ماذا* (*mādhā*) in MSA, *إيه* (*eh*) in Egypt, *شو* (*shū*) in the Levant and *إيش* (*ēsh*) in much of Saudi Arabia. "How are you?" is *إزيك* (*izzayyak*) in Cairo and *كيفك* (*kīfak*) in Beirut.

**What we suggest:** learn to read and write with MSA, and pick one dialect for speaking as soon as you can hold a basic conversation. On exchange apps, partners will write to you in a mix of both, and that is normal.

## How to pick an app to practice Arabic

**Filter by country, not just language.** Exchange apps are built around languages, not dialects, so your partner's country is the most reliable dialect filter. If you are learning Egyptian, look for partners in Egypt; if Moroccan, Morocco.

**Expect partners to write in Arabizi.** Many Arabic speakers text in Latin letters with numbers for sounds English lacks: *3* for ع, *7* for ح, *2* for the glottal stop. *Ma3 salama* is "goodbye". Ask your partner to write in Arabic script if you want to practice reading.

**Be ready to give back.** Many Arabic speakers are learning English, and many in North Africa are learning or already speak French. Either is a strong offer in an exchange.

[HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com) is the biggest exchange community, and Arabic speakers from across the region use it. Its **Moments** feed lets you post a few sentences and get corrections from several native speakers, which for Arabic has a bonus: you often see an Egyptian and a Jordanian suggest different ways to say the same thing.

## Arabic-specific resources worth adding

**[Al Jazeera Learning Arabic](https://learning.aljazeera.net/en)** is a free site from the Qatari broadcaster, built on real news articles, video and audio, with lessons by level. It teaches the standard language and has no speaking practice, which is exactly the half an exchange partner covers.

**Easy Arabic**, part of the Easy Languages network on YouTube, films conversations with native speakers, subtitled in Arabic and English.

**The alphabet.** Arabic has 28 letters, written right to left, and most change shape depending on where they sit in a word. Our [Arabic alphabet](/tools/alphabet/arabic) page lists every letter with a rough English sound. Short vowels are usually not written, which is why reading feels slow at first.

**Frequency lists and tests.** Our [most common Arabic words](/tools/most-common-words/arabic) lean toward the standard language: *ماذا* for "what" is in the top 15, where a Cairene would say *إيه*. That makes the list a good base for reading and for MSA; add your dialect's versions as your partner teaches them. Then check your level with the [Arabic vocabulary test](/tools/vocabulary-test/arabic).

**Related languages.** Persian is not related to Arabic, but it uses the same script and has borrowed many Arabic words. See [Arabic and Persian](/tools/similar/arabic-and-persian) for the overlap.

## Typing Arabic right to left

- **Add an Arabic keyboard** on your phone; it takes a minute, and letters join up automatically as you type.
- **Expect mixed text to jump around.** When you type an English word inside an Arabic sentence, the cursor and punctuation can appear in unexpected places. That is normal; reread the message once before you send it.
- **Use the Arabic punctuation.** The question mark is ؟ and the comma is ، on an Arabic keyboard.
- **Ask for vowel marks on new words.** Your partner can add the small marks (تشكيل) that show short vowels, so you learn how a word sounds, not just how it looks.

## Your first message in Arabic

Write your first message in MSA: any Arabic speaker understands it, and your partner will probably answer in a mix of MSA and their dialect. Keep it short and specific to their profile.

> مرحبا يا سارة! اسمي سام وأنا من كندا. أتعلم اللغة العربية، ورأيت أنك تتعلمين الإنجليزية. هل تريدين أن نتبادل اللغات؟ أساعدك في الإنجليزية وتصححين أخطائي في العربية.

In English: "Hi Sarah! My name is Sam and I'm from Canada. I'm learning Arabic, and I saw that you're learning English. Would you like to exchange languages? I help you with English and you correct my mistakes in Arabic." Notice *تتعلمين*, *تريدين* and *تصححين*: Arabic verbs change when you speak to a woman. Writing to a man, you would use *تتعلم*, *تريد* and *تصحح*.

Three more phrases you will use every week:

- **كيف أقول ... بالعربية؟** (*kayfa aqūlu... bil-ʿarabiyya?*) How do I say... in Arabic?
- **هل تبدو الجملة طبيعية؟** (*hal tabdū al-jumla ṭabīʿiyya?*) Does the sentence sound natural?

## Tips for Arabic learners on exchange apps

**Say which Arabic you want corrected toward.** "Please correct me in MSA" and "please tell me how you would say it in Egyptian" get very different answers. Both are useful; say which one you want each time.

**Learn words by their root.** Most Arabic words are built on three consonants. *ك ت ب* (k-t-b) gives *كتب* (he wrote), *كتاب* (book), *مكتب* (office, desk) and *كاتب* (writer). Ask your partner for other words from the same root and your vocabulary grows in families.

**Send voice messages for the throat sounds.** ع, ح, خ, غ and ق have no English equivalents, and you only get them right by hearing yourself next to a native speaker. A short voice message a day, answered by your partner, is the best drill there is.

**Learn the everyday politeness phrases.** *السلام عليكم* (*as-salāmu ʿalaykum*) and its reply *وعليكم السلام* (*wa-ʿalaykum as-salām*), *إن شاء الله* (*in shāʾ allāh*, "God willing") and *الحمد لله* (*al-ḥamdu lillāh*, "thank God", the usual answer to "how are you?") appear in almost every conversation, whatever the dialect.

## FAQ

### What is the best app to practice Arabic with native speakers?

HelloTalk and Tandem are the best free options for finding Arabic-speaking partners. italki is the best paid option for a specific dialect. LangX suits learners who want unlimited corrections and no ads.

### Should I learn Modern Standard Arabic or a dialect?

Both, in order: MSA for reading, writing and news, and one dialect for conversation. If you have no reason to pick a particular country, Egyptian or Levantine are widely understood.

### Can I practice Egyptian or Levantine Arabic on exchange apps?

Yes. Use the country filter to find partners in Egypt, or in Syria, Lebanon, Jordan or Palestine, and tell them which dialect you want to practice.

### What is Arabizi?

Arabic written in Latin letters with numbers for extra sounds, such as 3 for ع and 7 for ح. Many Arabic speakers use it when texting, so it helps to recognize it, even if you practice with Arabic script.
