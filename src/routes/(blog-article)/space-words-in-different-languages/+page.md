Universe is *universo* in Spanish, 宇宙 in Chinese, کائنات in Urdu and *heelal* in Afrikaans. Space is *espacio* in Spanish, *Raum* in German and 太空 in Chinese, and star is *estrella* in Catalan, ستاره in Persian and *yıldız* in Turkish. Below are five cosmic words (universe, space, star, planet and night) in 46 languages and more than a dozen scripts.

Every word comes from real usage data, not a phrasebook, and each section links to the full list on our free [Say it in any language](/tools/say) tool.

## Where these words come from

Each [/tools/say](/tools/say) page lists an English word in up to 53 languages. The words are ranked by how often they appear in film and TV subtitles (the OpenSubtitles 2018 frequency lists) and matched to English through Wiktionary, so every page shows where the word ranks in its language.

Two things to know:

- **Frequency lists count spellings, not meanings.** If a word for "night" is spelled like "I don't want", it ranks very high for the wrong reason. We only print rows whose meaning matches, and we name the ones we left out.
- **The lists are lowercase.** We capitalize German nouns (*Raum*, *Planeten*, *Nacht*) the way you'd see them in a sentence.

Sun, sky, moon and earth are already covered in our post on [nature words in different languages](/nature-words-in-different-languages), so they don't get a section here. For light, dark and dream, see our [beautiful words post](/beautiful-words-in-different-languages), which has a section for each.

Chinese 宇宙 (#463) is the most common real match, and its gloss also covers "cosmos" and "space". Half the table is the Latin *universum* in local spelling. Bulgarian свят (#359) is glossed "world, earth, universe", and Persian جهان and Georgian სამყარო are glossed "world" as well as "universe".

## Mix-ups to watch for

These words are short and common, so they collide with other words more than most. The patterns worth knowing:

- **Universe, world and earth overlap.** Greek κόσμο, Bulgarian свят, Georgian სამყარო and Persian جهان all lean toward "world". Catalan *món* turns up as "planet", and Indonesian *alam* is on the universe page but also means nature.
- **Space as in room.** Polish *miejsce*, Korean 자리, Norwegian *plass* and Icelandic *pláss* are about a place or room, not the cosmos.
- **Star as in celebrity.** Chinese 明星, Korean 스타, Hindi स्टार, French *star* and Tagalog *artista* are film stars.

For earth and moon, where the same thing happens (moon and month share a word in Persian, Turkish and Tagalog), see the [nature words post](/nature-words-in-different-languages). The [strong words post](/strong-words-in-different-languages) and [fantasy words post](/fantasy-words-in-different-languages) cover more words people use in names and stories.

<Callout type="info">
Words like universe, star and night are popular for tattoos, names and story titles. Before you use one, check it with a native speaker: the data is real, but it can't tell you whether a word sounds poetic, plain or means something else too.
</Callout>

## FAQ

### How do you say "universe" in different languages?

Spanish, Italian and Portuguese *universo*, French *univers*, Dutch *universum*, Afrikaans *heelal*, Ukrainian всесвіт, Croatian *svemir*, Urdu کائنات and Chinese 宇宙. The [universe page](/tools/say/universe) has 31 languages.

### How do you say "space" in other languages?

For space as in room or area: Spanish *espacio*, French *espace*, German *Raum*, Czech *prostor* and Indonesian *ruang*. For outer space: Chinese 太空, Hindi अंतरिक्ष, Tamil விண்வெளி and Hebrew חלל. The [space page](/tools/say/space) has 42 languages, some of them false matches.

### How do you say "star" in different languages?

Portuguese *estrela*, Swedish *stjärna*, Czech *hvězda*, Bulgarian звезда, Arabic نجمة, Persian ستاره, Turkish *yıldız*, Indonesian *bintang* and Basque *izar*. The [star page](/tools/say/star) has 44 languages; skip the rows that mean a celebrity.

### Why do some pages list a strange word near the top?

The ranks come from counting spellings in subtitles. When a word for "space" or "night" is spelled like a common word such as "to" or "I don't want", it ranks high for the wrong reason. We left those rows out and used the most common real match instead.
