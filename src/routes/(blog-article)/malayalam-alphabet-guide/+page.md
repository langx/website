The Malayalam alphabet has 49 basic letters in our chart: 13 vowels and 36 consonants. It is an abugida, which means every consonant already carries a short "a" sound, and the other vowels are written as signs attached to the consonant. Learn the vowels, then the consonants row by row, then the vowel signs, and you can sound out real Malayalam words.

This guide uses the letters, names and sounds from our free [Malayalam alphabet chart](/tools/alphabet/malayalam), and every practice word comes from our list of the [3,457 most common Malayalam words](/tools/most-common-words/malayalam).

## What kind of script is Malayalam?

Malayalam is written in its own script, used for the language of Kerala in southern India. Our alphabet data describes it as "the roundest of the Indian scripts and the one with the most letters, because Malayalam kept sounds its neighbours dropped."

The important word for a learner is **abugida**. In an alphabet like English, vowels and consonants are equal: you write *k* and then *a*. In an abugida, the consonant letter already includes a vowel. The letter ക on its own is read *ka*, not *k*. To say *ki* or *koo* instead, you add a vowel sign to ക. The standalone vowel letters are used mainly at the start of a word, where there is no consonant to attach a sign to.

Devanagari (Hindi), Bengali, Tamil and Telugu work the same way. What sets Malayalam apart in our charts is the count.

| അ | a | u in but |

Most of them come in short and long pairs: a and aa, i and ii, u and uu, e and ee, o and oo. The long one is usually the short one with an extra stroke or loop, so learn them in pairs. Length matters in Malayalam: a short and a long vowel can make two different words.

Two of these vowels are also everyday words on their own. ഈ (ii) means "this" and is the 6th most common word on our list; ആ (aa) means "that" and is 7th.

## Malayalam consonants

Our chart lists 36 consonants. The first 25 fall into five rows of five, and each row follows the same pattern: a plain sound, the same sound with a puff of air, a voiced sound, the voiced sound with a puff of air, and a nasal. Learn the pattern once and each new row is quick.

| Row | Plain | With a puff of air | Voiced | Voiced, puff of air | Nasal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| k-row | ക ka | ഖ kha | ഗ ga | ഘ gha | ങ nga |
| ch-row | ച cha | ഛ chha | ജ ja | ഝ jha | ഞ nya |
| t-row, tongue curled back | ട ta | ഠ tha | ഡ da | ഢ dha | ണ na |
| t-row, tongue on the teeth | ത ta | ഥ tha | ദ da | ധ dha | ന na |
| p-row | പ pa | ഫ pha | ബ ba | ഭ bha | മ ma |

## The letters that share a name

This is the part that trips up English speakers. Several Malayalam letters have the same romanized name because English has only one sound where Malayalam has two.

| Name | Letter 1 | Letter 2 | The difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ta | ട (tongue curled back) | ത (on the teeth) | where the tongue touches |
| tha | ഠ | ഥ | the same, with a puff of air |
| da | ഡ (tongue curled back) | ദ (on the teeth) | where the tongue touches |
| dha | ഢ | ധ | the same, with a puff of air |
| na | ണ (tongue curled back) | ന | where the tongue touches |
| sha | ശ | ഷ (tongue curled back) | where the tongue touches |
| la | ല | ള (tongue curled back) | where the tongue touches |
| ra | ര | റ (hard r) | how hard the r is |

"Tongue curled back" means the tip of the tongue curls up and back toward the roof of the mouth. You won't hear the difference from a table. You'll hear it from a native speaker, which is why these pairs are worth asking about early.

Then there is ഴ, named *zha* and described in our chart as "r and l at once". It has no English equivalent, and you'll meet it in common words such as വഴി (path), the 82nd most common word on our list.

## How vowel signs work

Every consonant carries a built-in short *a*. To put any other vowel after a consonant, you attach that vowel's sign. Here are the signs on ക (ka):

| Syllable | Sound | Vowel it uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ക | ka | built in |
| കാ | kaa | ആ |
| കി | ki | ഇ |
| കീ | kii | ഈ |
| കു | ku | ഉ |
| കൂ | kuu | ഊ |
| കൃ | kru | ഋ |
| കെ | ke | എ |
| കേ | kee | ഏ |
| കൈ | kai | ഐ |
| കൊ | ko | ഒ |
| കോ | koo | ഓ |
| കൗ | kau | ഔ |

Notice where the signs sit. The aa, i and ii signs come after the consonant. The e, ee and ai signs are written **before** it, even though you say them after. The o, oo and au signs wrap around, with one part on each side. The u and uu signs join onto the consonant and can change its shape.

### Four more marks you'll see in real words

The chart lists the 49 base letters. Real words use a few more marks, and all of these examples are from the word list:

- **The vowel killer (്, called chandrakkala).** It removes the built-in *a*. At the end of a word it is usually said as a very short, murmured vowel rather than silence. You'll see it in അത് (that, #4), ഇത് (this, #8) and പേര് (name, #52).
- **The m sign (ം, called anusvaram).** A small circle that adds an *m* sound, very often at the end of a word: മരം (tree, #443), സമയം (time, #67), എല്ലാം (all, #29). The name of the language itself, മലയാളം, ends with it.
- **Chillu letters.** Special forms for a consonant with no vowel at all, common at the end of words: ൻ (n) in ഞാൻ (I, #3) and അവൻ (he, #47), ൾ (the curled l) in അവൾ (she, #77), ർ (r) in അവർ (they, #48).
- **Doubled and joined consonants.** Two consonants with no vowel between them are often fused into one shape: മ്മ (mm) in അമ്മ (mother, #95), ല്ല (ll) in നല്ല (good, #25).

## Read your first Malayalam words

Put the pieces together. Each of these is on our most common words list, with its rank.

| Word | Rank | Meaning | Letters | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| നീ | 2 | you | ന + the ii sign | nii |
| ശരി | 20 | correct | ശ + ര + the i sign | sha-ri |
| വലിയ | 50 | big; large | വ + ല + the i sign + യ | va-li-ya |
| വഴി | 82 | path | വ + ഴ + the i sign | va-zhi |
| അമ്മ | 95 | mother | അ + doubled മ | am-ma |
| മരം | 443 | tree | മ + ര + the m sign | ma-ram |
| കട | 526 | store, shop | ക + ട | ka-ta (curled t) |
| ആന | 1,321 | elephant | ആ + ന | aa-na |

Start with കട and ആന: no vowel signs at all, just letters with their built-in *a*. Then നീ and ശരി add one sign each. By വലിയ you're reading a three-syllable word.

## FAQ

### How many letters are in the Malayalam alphabet?

Our chart lists 49 basic letters: 13 vowels and 36 consonants. Other charts give different totals depending on whether they include signs, chillu letters and rarer forms.

### Is Malayalam an alphabet or an abugida?

An abugida. Each consonant carries a built-in short *a*, and other vowels are written as signs attached to the consonant. Standalone vowel letters are used mainly at the start of a word.

### Why do some Malayalam letters have the same name?

Because Malayalam distinguishes sounds English doesn't. ട and ത are both "ta", but one is said with the tongue curled back and the other with the tongue on the teeth. The same goes for the two "da", "na", "sha", "la" and "ra" letters.

### What is the hardest Malayalam letter to pronounce?

For most English speakers it is ഴ (zha), which our chart describes as "r and l at once". It has no English equivalent, so it is best learned by listening to a native speaker.

### How long does it take to learn the Malayalam alphabet?

It depends on how much you practice. Learning it in the order above, vowels first, then consonant rows, then vowel signs, is faster than memorizing 49 unrelated shapes, and reading real words early makes the letters stick.
