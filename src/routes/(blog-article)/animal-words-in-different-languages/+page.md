Cat is *gato* in Spanish, γάτα in Greek, 고양이 in Korean and *pusa* in Tagalog. Dog is *perro* in Spanish, собака in Russian and كلب in Arabic, and bird is *Vogel* in German, *kuş* in Turkish and পাখি in Bengali. Below are nine animal words, from cat and dog to bear and fly, across 52 languages besides English and more than a dozen scripts.

Every word comes from real usage data, not a phrasebook, and each section links to the full list on our free [Say it in any language](/tools/say) tool.

## Where these words come from

Each [/tools/say](/tools/say) page lists an English word in up to 53 languages. The words are ranked by how often they appear in film and TV subtitles (the OpenSubtitles 2018 frequency lists) and matched to English through Wiktionary, so every page shows where the word ranks in its language.

Two things to know:

- **Frequency lists count spellings, not meanings.** If a word for "fish" is spelled like an everyday verb, it ranks very high for the wrong reason. We only print rows whose meaning matches, and we say where a page's "most common" claim is one of these.
- **The lists are lowercase and keep the form people used.** We capitalize German nouns (*Hund*, *Pferd*). Some rows are plurals or case forms, and we mark those.

## Mix-ups to watch for

Frequency data is honest about what people say, but it can't tell a fish from a verb that looks the same. The ones worth knowing:

- **Give, change and wrong.** Malay and Indonesian *berikan* (fish page), Telugu మార్పు (fish page) and Danish *galt* (pig page) rank high because of their other meaning.
- **A girl and a name.** Hebrew בחורה (girl) and French and Romanian *martin* sit on the bird page.
- **A car, not a horse.** Greek αμάξι is glossed "horse carriage", but it means car today.
- **Insults.** Hindi कुतिया, Icelandic *tík* and Korean 새끼 appear because people swear in films.
- **Verbs.** Most rows on the bear and fly pages mean "to bear" and "to fly".
- **Plurals and case forms.** Telugu పిల్లులు (cats), Georgian ძაღლები (dogs), Serbian *konja* and Norwegian *hesten* are not the dictionary form.

If you're reading a new script to check a word like this, the [Tamil](/tools/alphabet/tamil), [Telugu](/tools/alphabet/telugu), [Georgian](/tools/alphabet/georgian) and [Korean](/tools/alphabet/korean) alphabet pages show every letter.

## Learn animal words by talking about pets

Animal words stick when they belong to a real animal: your cat, your neighbor's dog, the birds outside. Pets are one of the easiest [language exchange conversation topics](/language-exchange-conversation-topics), because almost everyone has a pet story.

- **Introduce your pet.** Its name, what kind of animal it is, what it eats and what it did today.
- **Ask what animals say.** Dogs, cats and birds make different sounds in different languages, and a native speaker will enjoy telling you.

## FAQ

### How do you say "bird" in different languages?

German *Vogel*, Italian *uccello*, Greek πουλί, Turkish *kuş*, Persian پرنده, Bengali পাখি, Malayalam പക്ഷി, Indonesian *burung* and Vietnamese *chim*. The [bird page](/tools/say/bird) has 39 languages, but check it for false matches.

### How do you say "fish" in every language?

French *poisson*, Italian *pesce*, German *Fisch*, Greek ψάρι, Turkish *balık*, Arabic سمك, Hindi मछली, Bengali মাছ, Georgian თევზი, Korean 물고기 and Tagalog *isda*. The [fish page](/tools/say/fish) has 45 languages.

### How do you say "cat" and "dog" in different languages?

Cat is *gato* in Spanish, *chat* in French, *Katze* in German, گربه in Persian and 고양이 in Korean. Dog is *perro*, *chien*, *Hund*, سگ and 개. See the [cat](/tools/say/cat) and [dog](/tools/say/dog) pages.

### Which animal word is found in the most languages?

Dog: it is in all 53 languages on the site. Horse is in 49, while pig and bird are in 39 each.

### Why does the bear page list verbs?

English "bear" is also a verb, and the data matches every meaning of the English word. More than half of the bear page's rows mean "to bear" or "to endure", so we list only the animal here.
