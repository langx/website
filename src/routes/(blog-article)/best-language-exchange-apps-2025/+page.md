## Our 2025 picks, one by one

These are short on purpose. For full, current reviews of each app, see [our 2026 list](/best-language-exchange-apps).

### HelloTalk: the default choice of 2025

If you asked a language forum in 2025 which exchange app to download, the most common answer was [HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com). Its advantage was the same one it has now: so many people that popular pairs, such as English with Spanish, Japanese or Korean, rarely wait long for a reply. Moments (a public feed that native speakers correct) and Voicerooms gave people who were not ready for one-to-one chat a way in. The trade-off was also familiar: ads on the free tier, daily caps on extras, and a social-network feel that not everyone enjoys.

### Tandem: the focused alternative

[Tandem](https://tandem.net) kept its reputation as the calmer of the two big apps. Profiles are reviewed before you can chat, and the app is built around finding a partner rather than posting to a feed. Language Parties, its live audio rooms, suited people who wanted to listen before they talked. If you are choosing between the two giants today, our [Tandem vs HelloTalk](/tandem-vs-hellotalk) comparison goes feature by feature.

### Speaky: the free starting point

[Speaky](https://www.speaky.com) was, and still is, one of the easiest ways to message a native speaker without committing to a subscription. It had fewer learning tools in the chat than the big two, and quality varied a lot by language pair, but for a no-cost first try it was hard to beat.

### Lingbe: best for speaking out loud

[Lingbe](https://www.lingbe.com) did one thing: press a button and get connected to a native speaker for a live call. For learners whose problem was freezing when they spoke, that was exactly the right kind of pressure. It is still available, but its Android app was last updated on September 30, 2025, so 2025 may turn out to be the last year it was actively developed. Our [LangX vs Lingbe](/open-source-alternative-to-lingbe) page tracks where it stands.

### Conversation Exchange and InterPals: the web veterans

[Conversation Exchange](https://www.conversationexchange.com) remained the best tool for finding a partner in your own city, and [InterPals](https://www.interpals.net) remained the home of long, thoughtful pen-pal messages.

### LangX v1: small, free and open source

In 2025, LangX was the v1 app: free with no in-app purchases, no ads, open source, and a much smaller community than the apps above. It also carried a token that its old litepaper described as something tradable. v2, launched in late August 2026, rebuilt the app from scratch, added two-way matching and unlimited corrections on every plan, introduced paid Fluent and Polyglot plans, and turned the token into a plain in-app point. We explain all of that, including the promises v2 does not keep, in [LangX v2: what changes, and why](/langx-v2-what-changes-and-why).

## What changed in the language app market during 2025

The exchange apps themselves had a quiet year. Most of the change happened around them, in course apps, AI tutors and translation tools. Here are the dated events that mattered for anyone practicing a language.

- **January 16, 2025:** [Duolingo brought Video Call](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-launches-ai-powered-video-call-android), its AI conversation with the character Lily, to Android for Duolingo Max subscribers.
- **Late April 2025:** Duolingo's CEO sent an internal memo declaring the company "AI-first", saying it would gradually stop using contractors for work AI can handle. The memo drew a strong public backlash.
- **April 30, 2025:** Duolingo [launched 148 new language courses](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-launches-148-new-language-courses), built with generative AI, more than doubling its course catalog.
- **June 30, 2025:** the last day individual learners could book **Babbel Live** classes. Babbel's help center says most individual learners "did not accept Babbel Live as part of their language learning path"; live classes continue only through Babbel for Business.
- **August 2025:** Google started a **practice** beta inside the Google Translate app, with AI-generated listening and speaking exercises, first for English, Spanish, French and Portuguese speakers.
- **September 2025:** Apple announced **Live Translation** for AirPods, translating face-to-face conversations in a handful of languages, and extended it to the EU in November.
- **September 16, 2025:** Babbel launched **Babbel Speak**, an AI voice trainer for beginners, in beta. The same day, at Duocon 2025, Duolingo said Video Call was now available in nine courses for all Max subscribers.
- **November 2025:** Memrise said it would not shut down its community courses, after years of uncertainty about them.
- **Late December 2025:** Duolingo said its AI "Explain My Answer" feature would be free for all learners from January 1, 2026.

Two older changes still shaped 2025. **Bilingua**, an interest-based matching app, had already disappeared from the stores (its Google Play removal was reported in June 2023), and **italki** had removed its free language-partner search years earlier. Former users of both spent 2025 looking for replacements; see [LangX vs Bilingua](/open-source-alternative-to-bilingua) and [LangX vs italki](/open-source-alternative-to-italki).

Meanwhile, the big exchange apps added AI helpers around the human conversation rather than instead of it. By the time we reviewed them in 2026, Tandem Pro included an AI Toolkit (grammar check, a word finder, a rewrite tool) and HelloTalk offered a set of AI apps alongside its community. The pattern was consistent: AI for the moments when your partner is asleep or you do not want to bother them, humans for the conversation itself.

## What 2025 taught language learners

### AI tutors are good at rehearsal, not at relationships

By the end of 2025, anyone with Duolingo Max, Babbel or Google Translate could talk to an AI in their target language. What an AI could not give you was a person who remembers your week, laughs at a joke in their own language, or tells you that nobody actually says that. The lesson of 2025 was not "AI or humans" but "AI for volume, humans for real conversation". We go deeper in [AI conversation partner vs real language exchange](/ai-conversation-partner-vs-language-exchange).

### Human-led services are expensive, and they can disappear

Babbel Live was a well-known brand with professional teachers, and it still closed to individual learners in 2025. Paid tutors on marketplaces like italki and Preply carried on, but the Babbel decision was a reminder that live human teaching is costly to run. Language exchange is the one form of human practice that does not need anyone to be paid, because both people teach.

### Check that an app is still alive before you invest in it

Bilingua vanished, Lingbe slowed down, and italki's partner search is long gone. In 2025 it paid to check an app's last update date before building a habit around it. It also made the case for open source: when an app's code is public, the community does not vanish just because one company changes direction.

### Speaking finally got attention

In 2025 speaking features appeared almost everywhere, from AI calls to Translate's practice mode. If you still want a real person on the other end, our list of [Duolingo alternatives for speaking](/duolingo-alternatives-for-speaking) covers the options.

- **Intermediate, wants conversation:** HelloTalk or Tandem for a text-and-voice partner.

## FAQ

### What was the best language exchange app in 2025?

HelloTalk was the best all-round language exchange app of 2025 because of its community size, with Tandem the best choice for learners who wanted a focused one-to-one partner. Lingbe was the best for live voice practice and Speaky the best for starting free.

### Did any language exchange apps shut down in 2025?

None of the major exchange apps shut down in 2025. The biggest closure nearby was Babbel Live, which stopped taking bookings from individual learners on June 30, 2025. Bilingua had already left the app stores in 2023, and Lingbe's updates slowed after September 2025.

### Did language exchange apps add AI in 2025?

AI spread fastest through course apps in 2025: Duolingo's Video Call, Babbel Speak and Google Translate's practice mode. Exchange apps like Tandem and HelloTalk offer AI helpers alongside human partners, rather than as a replacement for them.

### Are the best language exchange apps of 2025 still good in 2026?

Mostly, yes. HelloTalk, Tandem, Speaky, Conversation Exchange and InterPals are all active in September 2026. Lingbe still works but is updated less often, and LangX has been rebuilt as v2. See [our 2026 list](/best-language-exchange-apps) for current details.

### Was LangX available in 2025?

Yes, as LangX v1: free, ad-free and open source, with a small community. It was replaced by LangX v2 in late August 2026. Former v1 users have to sign up again, and the [welcome back page](/welcome-back) explains what carries over.
