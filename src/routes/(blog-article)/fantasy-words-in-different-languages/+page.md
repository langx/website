Princess is *princesa* in Spanish, πριγκίπισσα in Greek, 公主 in Chinese and *putri* in Indonesian. Prince is *príncipe* in Spanish, 王子 in Chinese and شاهزاده in Persian, and sword is *espada* in Spanish, *Schwert* in German and तलवार in Hindi. Below are 12 fantasy words, from princess and king to ghost and devil, in more than 40 languages and 14 scripts.

Every word comes from real usage data, not a phrasebook, and each section links to the full list on our free [Say it in any language](/tools/say) tool.

## Where these words come from

Each [/tools/say](/tools/say) page lists an English word in up to 53 languages. The words are ranked by how often they appear in film and TV subtitles (the OpenSubtitles 2018 frequency lists) and matched to English through Wiktionary, so every page shows where the word ranks in its language.

Two things to know:

- **Frequency lists count spellings, not meanings.** If a word for "sword" is spelled like an everyday verb, it ranks high for the wrong reason. We only print rows whose meaning matches, and we say where a row on the full page means something else.
- **The lists are lowercase.** We capitalize German nouns (*Prinzessin*, *Schwert*) the way you'd see them in a sentence.

Fantasy films and series are a big part of any subtitle collection, which is why words like sword and witch turn up in more than 30 languages each.

## Mix-ups to watch for

Frequency data is honest about what people say, but it can't tell a sword from a verb that looks the same. The ones worth knowing:

- **Lady, not princess.** Arabic سيدة and Hebrew שרה show up on the princess page, but they mean "lady" first.
- **Princess on the prince page.** The Russian row for prince is принцесса, which is the princess.
- **Knife, not sword.** Korean 칼 is glossed "knife, sword".
- **Bad, cat and a small word.** Spanish *mala* (devil page), Swedish *katt* (devil page) and Macedonian али (monster page) are false matches.
- **King or angel?** Arabic ملك can be read either way, and Hebrew מלך and מלאך differ by one letter.
- **"The" is built in.** Albanian *mbreti* and Romanian *regele* mean "the king", and Swedish *prinsessan* is "the princess".

If you're reading a new script to check a word like this, the [Georgian](/tools/alphabet/georgian), [Korean](/tools/alphabet/korean), [Hebrew](/tools/alphabet/hebrew) and [Arabic](/tools/alphabet/arabic) alphabet pages show every letter.

## Practice fantasy words with native speakers

Fantasy words are easy to bring into a conversation, because almost everyone has a favorite film, series, game or fairy tale. Tell your partner about one, and you'll need *king*, *sword* and *monster* in the first few sentences.

- **Retell a story you both know.** A fairy tale or a famous film gives you the plot, so all your effort goes into the words.
- **Ask what the children's stories are.** Which witch or ghost do kids grow up with where your partner lives? That's something a table can't tell you.
- **Check the swear words.** Before you say *faen* or *kurat* out loud, ask how strong it sounds.

## FAQ

### How do you say "princess" in different languages?

### Why do some pages list a strange word as the most common?

The ranks come from counting spellings in subtitles. When a word for "devil" or "sword" is spelled like "bad", "cat" or a common verb, it ranks high for the wrong reason. We left those rows out and gave the most common real match instead.
