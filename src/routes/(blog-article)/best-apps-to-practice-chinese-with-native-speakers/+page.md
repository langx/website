The best app to practice Mandarin Chinese with native speakers is usually **HelloTalk**, which is based in Shenzhen and has a very large Chinese-speaking community. **Tandem** is good for a steady one-to-one partner, **LangX** for corrections on every message with no ads, and **italki** for a paid Chinese teacher. Chinese asks different things of a language exchange than European languages do. You need to hear tones, not just read words; you have to choose between simplified and traditional characters; and at the start you depend on pinyin to read anything at all. The apps below are chosen with that in mind.

## How to pick an app to practice Chinese

**Say which Chinese you mean.** "Chinese" covers several languages that are not mutually intelligible when spoken, above all Mandarin and Cantonese. This guide is about Mandarin, the official language of mainland China and Taiwan and one of the official languages of Singapore. Whatever app you use, write "Mandarin" in your profile so Cantonese speakers do not match with you by mistake, or so they do, if Cantonese is what you want.

**Choose simplified or traditional characters.** Mainland China and Singapore use **simplified** characters; Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau use **traditional** ones. Most learners start with simplified, but if you plan to live in Taipei, learn traditional. Many common characters are the same in both, and partners who grew up with one can usually read the other, with some effort.

**Make sure you can hear your partner.** Mandarin is tonal, so text alone will never teach you to speak. Voice messages, calls or read-aloud are essential, much more than for Spanish or French.

**Look for pinyin and translation support.** Pinyin, the official romanization, is how you type Chinese and how you read words you cannot recognize yet. Transliteration and translation inside the chat save you from copying every sentence into a dictionary.

**Expect a long road.** The US Foreign Service Institute puts Mandarin in its hardest category for English speakers (see [the easiest and hardest languages](/easiest-languages-to-learn-for-english-speakers)). Pick apps you will still enjoy in a year.

## Language exchange apps for Chinese

### HelloTalk

[HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com) was founded in 2012 in Shenzhen, China, and according to its Wikipedia entry more than half of its users come from China, Japan, South Korea and the United States. That makes it the most practical place to find Mandarin speakers learning English.

- **Best for:** finding partners quickly. Transliteration and translation inside the chat help with characters you cannot read yet, and **Moments** lets several native speakers correct a short post.
- **Keep in mind:** the free tier has ads and caps on translation and other extras; VIP removes them. With so many learners writing to Chinese speakers, a specific opener helps.

Compare: [LangX vs HelloTalk](/open-source-alternative-to-hellotalk).

### Tandem

[Tandem](https://tandem.net) reviews new members and is built around one-to-one conversations with text, voice messages and calls. Its country filter is useful if you specifically want a partner in Taiwan or Singapore.

- **Best for:** a regular weekly call with one Mandarin partner.
- **Keep in mind:** approval can take time, and some filters and unlimited translation are in Tandem Pro.

Compare: [LangX vs Tandem](/open-source-alternative-to-tandem), or read [Tandem vs HelloTalk](/tandem-vs-hellotalk).

Our app. [LangX](https://get.langx.io) matches in both directions: set Chinese as the language you are learning and you see Chinese speakers who are learning a language you speak. Your partner can hold any message to correct it, and corrections are **unlimited on every plan**, so a wrong character or a missing measure word can be fixed right where you wrote it. Translation is inside the chat (20 a day on Free), and read-aloud lets you hear any message spoken, useful when you cannot yet sound out the characters.

## Tutors and AI for Chinese speaking practice

**[italki](https://www.italki.com)** and **[Preply](https://preply.com)** list many Mandarin teachers. For Chinese, a tutor is worth more than for most languages at the start, because a good teacher will fix your tones before bad habits set in. Professional teachers can also prepare you for the HSK, China's official proficiency test. See [LangX vs italki](/open-source-alternative-to-italki) and [LangX vs Preply](/open-source-alternative-to-preply).

Speaking to an AI with instant feedback is a low-pressure way to drill tones before a real call. ## Chinese-specific tools worth adding

**[Pleco](https://www.pleco.com)** is the dictionary most Chinese learners use, on iOS and Android. The free app includes dictionaries with pinyin and example sentences, and you can look characters up by pinyin or by drawing them, with extras such as camera lookup and stroke-order animations available as add-ons. Keep it open during every chat.

**[HelloChinese](https://www.hellochinese.cc)** is a beginner-friendly Mandarin course app with speech recognition that scores your pronunciation, handwriting practice and graded videos and stories. It gives you the structure an exchange lacks.

## Tones and pinyin in a Chinese language exchange

Mandarin has **four tones plus a neutral tone**, and the tone changes the word. The classic example is *ma*: 妈 (mā, mother), 麻 (má, hemp), 马 (mǎ, horse) and 骂 (mà, to scold).

- **Ask for tone corrections explicitly.** Many native speakers understand you from context and will not mention a wrong tone unless you ask. Tell your partner you want to know every time.
- **Send voice messages and ask for them back.** Record a sentence, have your partner record the same sentence, and compare. This is the single most useful exchange habit for Chinese.
- **Learn words with their tones from day one.** Pleco and pinyin with tone marks (mā, má, mǎ, mà) make this easy; writing pinyin without tones hides the problem.
- **Type in pinyin.** On a Chinese keyboard you type pinyin, without tone marks, and pick the right characters from a list. Writing back in characters, even slowly, earns you much better corrections than writing in pinyin.

## Your first message in Chinese

Keep it short and simple. Here it is in simplified characters, with pinyin:

> 你好！我叫Sam，我是加拿大人。我在学中文。很高兴认识你！

(Nǐ hǎo! Wǒ jiào Sam, wǒ shì Jiānádà rén. Wǒ zài xué Zhōngwén. Hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ!)

In English: "Hello! My name is Sam, I'm Canadian. I'm learning Chinese. Nice to meet you!" In traditional characters, three words change: 学 becomes 學, 高兴 becomes 高興, and 认识 becomes 認識.

It is fine to add a line in English saying what you can help with and asking them to correct your Chinese. Then a few phrases you will use constantly:

- **谢谢** (xièxie): thank you. You can also hear [thanks in other languages](/tools/say/thanks).
- **这个用中文怎么说？** (Zhège yòng Zhōngwén zěnme shuō?): How do you say this in Chinese?
- **我说得对吗？** (Wǒ shuō de duì ma?): Did I say that right?

## Tips for Chinese learners on exchange apps

**Learn pinyin before your first exchange.** A week or two with the initials, finals and tone marks makes every chat easier, and partners can then send you pinyin when a character stumps you.

**Collect measure words.** Chinese uses a measure word between a number and a noun: 一本书 (yì běn shū, one book), 一个人 (yí gè rén, one person). When a partner corrects one, write down the noun with its measure word.

**Do not panic about writing by hand.** For chatting, you need to recognize characters and type them in pinyin. Handwriting can come later, if you want it.

**Keep a correction notebook.** Chinese corrections are often one character or one tone. Write them down the same day, with pinyin and tone marks.

## FAQ

### What is the best app to practice Chinese with native speakers?

HelloTalk is usually the easiest place to find Mandarin speakers, because a large share of its community is in China and East Asia. Tandem suits a calmer one-to-one exchange, LangX gives unlimited corrections with no ads, and italki is best for a paid teacher.

### Should I learn simplified or traditional Chinese?

Learn simplified if you are aiming at mainland China or Singapore, and traditional if you are aiming at Taiwan or Hong Kong. If you have no preference, most courses and apps start with simplified.

### Can I practice Chinese speaking for free?

Yes. HelloTalk and Tandem include free calls with exchange partners. LangX supports voice messages but not live calls. Tutors on italki and Preply are paid.

### How do I practice Chinese tones with a partner?

Send short voice messages, ask your partner to record the same sentence back, and ask them to point out every wrong tone. Native speakers often let tone mistakes pass unless you ask.
