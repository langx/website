## Where these words come from

Each [/tools/say](/tools/say) page lists an English word in up to 53 languages. The words are ranked by how often they appear in film and TV subtitles (the OpenSubtitles 2018 frequency lists) and matched to English through Wiktionary, so every page shows where the word ranks in its language.

Two things to know:

- **Frequency lists count spellings, not meanings.** If a word for "time" is spelled like the everyday word for "now" or "about", it ranks very high for the wrong reason. We only print rows whose meaning matches, and we say where a page's "most common" claim is one of these.
- **The lists keep the form people used.** Some rows are plurals or case forms, such as "at home" or "in the kitchen", and we mark those. We capitalize German nouns (*Küche*, *Geld*).

Kitchen is one of the rarest words here: every row ranks past #700. Afrikaans *kombuis* (#708) is the most common, then Galician *cociña* (#894), Telugu వంటగది (#913) and French *cuisine* (#959). *Kombuis* comes from a Dutch word for a ship's galley.

Most European words go back to Latin *coquina*, from "to cook": *cocina*, *cucina*, *cuisine*, *Küche*, *keuken*, *kuchyně* and, through Spanish, Tagalog *kusina*. The only false match is the English row, "chef".

## Mix-ups to watch for

Frequency data is honest about what people say, but it can't tell a house from a wall. The ones worth knowing:

- **Small words.** Most false matches on the time page are words like "now", "then" and "never", and Hindi बारे ("about").
- **Phrases.** "On the house" (Estonian *ilma*), "at the house of" (Arabic عند) and "house of worship" (church) put rows on the house page.
- **Near spellings.** Swedish *bröt* (broke) on the bread page and Dutch *boek* (to book) on the book page.
- **Slang.** French *fric* is money, not bread.
- **Place forms.** Many rows mean "at home", "in the house" or "in the kitchen" rather than the plain noun.

If you're reading a new script to check a word like this, the [Persian](/tools/alphabet/persian), [Hindi](/tools/alphabet/hindi), [Tamil](/tools/alphabet/tamil) and [Korean](/tools/alphabet/korean) alphabet pages show every letter.

## Learn everyday words by talking about your day

Everyday words are the ones you need first, because they are what you talk about: where you live, what you ate, how work went. They are also the easiest [language exchange conversation topics](/language-exchange-conversation-topics).

- **Describe your home.** How many rooms, what the kitchen looks like, what you can see from the window.

## FAQ

### How do you say "kitchen" in different languages?

Spanish *cocina*, French *cuisine*, Italian *cucina*, German *Küche*, Greek κουζίνα, Icelandic *eldhús*, Hindi रसोई, Korean 부엌, Indonesian *dapur* and Tagalog *kusina*. The [kitchen page](/tools/say/kitchen) has 35 languages.

### How do you say "house" in different languages?

Italian *casa*, French *maison*, German *Hause* (as in *zu Hause*, at home), Greek σπίτι, Hebrew בית, Persian خونه, Hindi घर, Korean 집 and Tagalog *bahay*. The [house page](/tools/say/house) has 50 languages, but check it for false matches.

### How do you say "money" in different languages?

Spanish *dinero*, Italian *soldi*, German *Geld*, Russian деньги, Persian پول, Tamil பணம், Korean 돈, Indonesian *uang* and Turkish *para*. The [money page](/tools/say/money) has 48 languages.

### What is the difference between house and home in other languages?

Many languages don't make the distinction with a noun. In this data, Greek, Hindi, Korean, Vietnamese, Malayalam and Urdu use the same word for both. Others have special forms for "at home" and "homeward", like Czech *doma* and Russian домой.

### Which everyday word is the most common?

Chinese 工作 (work) ranks #21, the highest real match in this post. Turkish *zaman* (time) is #37 and Persian خونه (house) is #56.
