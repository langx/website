## Where these words come from

Each [/tools/say](/tools/say) page lists an English word in up to 53 languages. The words are ranked by how often they appear in film and TV subtitles (the OpenSubtitles 2018 frequency lists) and matched to English through Wiktionary, so every page shows where the word ranks in its language.

Three things to know:

- **Frequency lists count spellings, not meanings.** If a word for "blue" is spelled like an everyday noun, it ranks very high for the wrong reason. We only print rows whose meaning matches, and we say where a page's "most common" claim is one of these.
- **Colors change their endings.** In many languages an adjective agrees with its noun, so the data holds whichever form people said most: Serbian *crvena* (feminine), Dutch *rode* and Norwegian *røde*. We print the form in the data.
- **The lists are lowercase.** We capitalize the German noun *Gold*; German color adjectives stay lowercase.

## Blue and green: what this data does and doesn't show

You may have read that some languages use one word for both blue and green. We checked: no language in this data has the same word on the blue page and the green page. That doesn't prove such languages don't exist; it means these pages can't show it, so we won't name any.

What the data does show is that a color word can cover more than one English color. Tamil நீல is glossed "blue, azure or purple". The Vietnamese rows don't help either way: the blue row is *lam* (#2127), which is rare in subtitles, and the green row is *hành*, which means "green onion", so there's no Vietnamese word for green in the data at all. If you're curious how a language splits up the colors, that's a great question to put to a native speaker.

## Mix-ups to watch for

Frequency data is honest about what people say, but it can't tell a color from a word that looks the same. The ones worth knowing:

- **Son, forehead and "how are you".** Macedonian син (blue page), Turkish *alın* (red page) and Arabic حالك (black page) rank high because of their other meaning.
- **A person, not a color.** Chinese 黑人 and 白人 and Korean 흑인 and 백인 mean a black or white person, and Tagalog *taong* is spelled like "person".
- **A vegetable.** Vietnamese *hành* on the green page is a green onion.
- **Endearments.** Czech and Slovak *zlato* and Finnish *kulta* are gold, but also "darling".
- **Forms, not dictionary words.** Serbian *crvena*, Dutch *rode*, Hindi काले and Latvian *zelta* are inflected forms.

If you're reading a new script to check a word like this, the [Georgian](/tools/alphabet/georgian), [Tamil](/tools/alphabet/tamil), [Bengali](/tools/alphabet/bengali) and [Persian](/tools/alphabet/persian) alphabet pages show every letter.

## Learn color words by describing what you see

Color words are easy to practice, because everything around you has one. Clothes, flags, food and the view from your window are all quick [language exchange conversation topics](/language-exchange-conversation-topics).

- **Describe a photo.** Send a picture and name every color in it, with the right ending for each noun.
- **Ask about the in-between colors.** Where does your partner's language put the line between blue and green, or red and orange? A table can't tell you that.

## FAQ

### How do you say "red" in different languages?

Spanish *rojo*, French *rouge*, German *rot*, Russian красный, Greek κόκκινο, Arabic أحمر, Hebrew אדום, Hindi लाल and Vietnamese *đỏ*. The [red page](/tools/say/red) has 49 languages.

### How do you say "blue" in different languages?

Spanish *azul*, French *bleu*, German *blau*, Greek μπλε, Arabic أزرق, Persian آبی, Turkish *mavi*, Bengali নীল and Korean 파란색. The [blue page](/tools/say/blue) has 44 languages.

### Do some languages use one word for blue and green?

This data can't show it: no language here has the same word on both pages. Ask a native speaker how their language divides the colors.

### Which color word is found in the most languages?

Black, in 51 languages. Red and white are in 49 each, and green is in the fewest, 39.

### Is there a difference between "color" and "colour"?

Only the spelling. The site has a page for each, with 44 and 34 languages, and most of the words are the same.
