LangX is a social alternative to Duolingo: a social language app where the "lesson" is a conversation with a real person, a native speaker of the language you are learning who is learning yours. You chat, they correct you, you correct them. Think of it as Duolingo with real people instead of a lesson path, and it works best alongside Duolingo rather than instead of it.

We make LangX, so read this with that in mind. We also like Duolingo, and we will be fair about it: Duolingo is not an antisocial app. It has friends, leagues and shared quests. What it does not have is a real person to talk to. That one gap is what this page is about.

## What Duolingo does well

Before talking about what is missing, it is worth being clear about what works, because a lot of it is exactly what a social language learning app should copy.

- **It makes you show up.** Streaks, short lessons, reminders and the owl turn five minutes a day into a habit. For most learners, consistency is the hardest part.
- **It is a great on-ramp.** You can start a language from zero and be reading simple sentences within days.
- **It is more social than people think.** At the time of writing (September 2026), Duolingo lets you follow friends, share a **Friend Streak** with up to five friends, team up on weekly **Friends Quests**, send nudges and high-fives, and compete in weekly **leagues** against a group of other learners.
- **It has started on speaking.** Duolingo Max includes **Video Call with Lily**, an AI character you talk to, and Roleplay scenarios with AI feedback. In 2026 Duolingo also began expanding Video Call to some Super subscribers.

If you are starting a language from scratch, Duolingo is a very reasonable first app. Our [LangX vs Duolingo comparison](/open-source-alternative-to-duolingo) goes feature by feature.

## What Duolingo can't give you: a person

Look closely at Duolingo's social features and they share one thing: they are about *progress*, not *conversation*. You can see that a friend did a lesson, race them for XP and send them a high-five. You cannot chat with them in the app, and you cannot be matched with a native speaker who wants to talk. Duolingo's old sentence discussion forums, once the place learners helped each other, no longer take new comments.

The speaking practice it does offer is with AI. Lily is patient, always available and good for losing the fear of speaking. But she is not a person. She will not tell you what people actually say in Mexico City this year, she will not ask you a question you truly did not see coming, and she will never become a friend you want to keep writing to. That last part matters more than it sounds: people keep practicing for years because of the people, not the tool. We wrote more about that trade-off in [AI conversation partner vs language exchange](/ai-conversation-partner-vs-language-exchange).

So the fair way to put it is this: **on Duolingo you practice with the app; on a social language app you practice with real people.** Both are practice. Only one is conversation.

## How LangX works: Duolingo with real people

LangX keeps the parts of Duolingo that make learning feel like a game, and swaps the lesson path for people.

### Two-way matching

You only see people who speak the language you are learning *and* are learning a language you speak. If you speak English and are learning Spanish, you see Spanish speakers learning English. Every chat is useful to both of you, which is why people answer. If this is new, [how to do a language exchange](/how-to-do-a-language-exchange) explains how to run one well.

### Corrections on any message, unlimited

Hold any message in a chat to correct it, and the fix appears right in the conversation. You can also post a sentence to the Feed and let the community correct it. Corrections are **unlimited on every plan**, Free included, and so are replies to anyone who writes to you. This is the part that teaches, so it is never rationed.

### Translation, voice notes and read-aloud

Stuck on a word? Translate it inside the chat (20 translations a day on Free, 300 on Fluent, 1000 on Polyglot). Send voice notes to practice pronunciation, photos and short videos to show what you mean, and hold a message to hear it read aloud. There are no live calls on LangX; conversations happen through text, voice, photo and video messages, which also means you have time to think before you answer.

### Streaks, tokens and leaderboards

If the game side is what you love about Duolingo, it is here too:

- **Daily streaks**, with milestone bonuses at 7, 30, 100 and 365 days and beyond.
- **The [LangX Token](/tokens)**, an in-app point you earn by sending messages, correcting other people's sentences and answering pronunciation requests. Spend it on streak freezes, stickers and cosmetic frames and titles. It is a point, not money: it cannot be bought, sold, traded or withdrawn.
- **Leaderboards** by week, month, year and all time, so there is always a table you can climb.

The difference from Duolingo is what earns the points. On LangX you climb by talking to people and helping them learn your language.

<Callout type="info">LangX is not a course. There is no lesson path, grammar unit or placement test — Echo, its free spaced-repetition tab, gives you phrase packs to review, not lessons. It works best once you can write a few simple sentences, which is roughly where Duolingo gets you.</Callout>

## Who a social language app is for

LangX, and social language learning apps in general, suit you if you:

- have finished a few Duolingo units and want to *use* what you learned
- want corrections from a native speaker, not an answer key
- want to know how people actually talk, including slang and everyday phrasing
- are motivated by people more than by points (or by both)
- prefer writing and voice notes to live video calls, at least at first

It is probably not the right first step if you are starting from zero and want a structured course; stick with Duolingo for a few weeks and come back. And if you want a live speaking lesson with a professional, a tutor is a better fit, as our [Duolingo alternatives for speaking](/duolingo-alternatives-for-speaking) guide explains.

## Is LangX free like Duolingo?

Yes. The Free plan is a real plan, not a trial: unlimited text messages, unlimited replies and corrections, 5 new conversations a day and 20 translations a day, with no ads. Fluent and Polyglot add unlimited new conversations, more translations, more languages and extra filters; see the [plans page](/plans). Prices are set per region and shown in the app. Tokens cannot buy a paid plan.

A note on privacy, since social apps involve other people's messages as well as yours: LangX has no ads and no advertising identifiers. It does use product analytics (PostHog, on its EU cloud), including a masked screen recording on iOS and Android that replaces every word and image with a grey block on your device. It is on by default and switches off in Settings → Privacy → Share usage data.

## FAQ

### Is there a social version of Duolingo?

Not from Duolingo itself: its social features are friends, Friend Streaks, Friends Quests and leagues, but no chat with other people. The closest thing to a social version of Duolingo is a social language app like LangX, where you practice with real people who are learning your language, with streaks, tokens and leaderboards on top.

### Can you talk to real people on Duolingo?

Not in a conversation. You can follow friends, compete with them and send nudges or high-fives, but there is no messaging between learners. Duolingo's conversation practice is with AI characters, such as Video Call with Lily on its paid plans.

### What app is like Duolingo but with real people?

LangX is built to be like Duolingo but social: streaks, tokens and leaderboards, but every "lesson" is a chat with a native speaker who is learning your language. HelloTalk and Tandem are other apps where you practice with real people; our [best language exchange apps](/best-language-exchange-apps) guide compares them.

### Is LangX free like Duolingo?

Yes. LangX Free has no ads and includes unlimited replies and corrections, 5 new conversations a day and 20 translations a day. Paid plans (Fluent and Polyglot) raise the limits.

### Should I quit Duolingo for LangX?

No need. Duolingo teaches you the words; LangX gives you real people to use them with. Most learners get the most from doing both, a little every day. [Get LangX](https://get.langx.io) on iOS, Android or the web.
