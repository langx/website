Sea is *mar* in Spanish, θάλασσα in Greek, 바다 in Korean and *deniz* in Turkish. Mountain is *Berg* in German, کوه in Persian and 산 in Korean, and river is *río* in Spanish, नदी in Hindi and *sông* in Vietnamese. Below are 16 nature words, from sea and sky to tree and forest, in more than 30 languages and a dozen scripts.

Every word comes from real usage data, not a phrasebook, and each section links to the full list on our free [Say it in any language](/tools/say) tool.

## Where these words come from

Each [/tools/say](/tools/say) page lists an English word in up to 53 languages. The words are ranked by how often they appear in film and TV subtitles (the OpenSubtitles 2018 frequency lists) and matched to English through Wiktionary, so every page shows where the word ranks in its language.

Two things to know:

- **Frequency lists count spellings, not meanings.** If a word for "sea" is spelled like an everyday verb, it ranks very high for the wrong reason. We only print rows whose meaning matches, and we say where the full page's "most common" claim is one of these.
- **The lists are lowercase.** We capitalize German nouns (*Berg*, *Wasser*) the way you'd see them in a sentence.

## Why Tagalog keeps coming out on top

Tagalog has the most common real match in seven of these sections: *dagat* (sea), *bundok* (mountain), *ilog* (river), *araw* (sun), *tubig* (water, one place ahead of Malayalam), *puno* (tree) and *kalikasan* (nature). Take that as a quirk of the data rather than proof that Filipinos talk about nature more. Each language's list comes from a different set of films and shows, and *araw* also means "day". If you're learning Tagalog, though, it means these words are worth learning early. The [Tagalog word list](/tools/most-common-words/tagalog) shows what else is near the top.

## Learn nature words by talking about where you live

Nature words stick when you attach them to real places: the river in your town, the mountains you can see, the rain this week. Weather and places are easy [language exchange conversation topics](/language-exchange-conversation-topics), and there are environment questions for when you want to go deeper.

- **Start with the weather.** Rain, sun and wind come up in almost every chat, because everyone can say what it's like outside today.
- **Name the places near you.** The river, the sea or the hills you can see from home are easier to remember than any list.
- **Ask about the double meanings.** Does your partner's word for moon also mean month? Are sky and heaven one word for them? A table can't tell you how those feel to a native speaker.

## FAQ

### How do you say "sea" in different languages?

Spanish *mar*, French *mer*, German *Meer*, Greek θάλασσα, Russian море, Arabic بحر, Turkish *deniz*, Korean 바다 and Tagalog *dagat*. The [sea page](/tools/say/sea) has 46 languages.

### How do you say "mountain" in different languages?

Spanish *montaña*, French *montagne*, German *Berg*, Greek βουνό, Arabic جبل, Persian کوه, Georgian მთა, Korean 산 and Turkish *dağ*. The [mountain page](/tools/say/mountain) has 38 languages.

### Which nature words are found in every language?

In this data, fire and water are in all 53 languages. Earth and tree are in 48, while nature itself is in only 31.

### Why do some pages list a strange word as the most common?

The ranks come from counting spellings in subtitles. When a word for "sea" or "river" is spelled like a common verb, it ranks high for the wrong reason. We left those rows out and used the most common real match instead.
