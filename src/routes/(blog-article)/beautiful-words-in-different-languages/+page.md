## Where these words come from

Each [/tools/say](/tools/say) page is built from two open sources:

- **Frequency counts from film and TV subtitles** (the OpenSubtitles 2018 lists published in the FrequencyWords project). This is how we know that a word is in everyday use, and where it ranks.
- **English meanings from Wiktionary**, which link each word back to an English one like "freedom" or "soul".

That has two consequences worth knowing before you copy anything:

1. **You sometimes get the form people say, not the dictionary form.** Subtitles are speech, so a row can be an inflected form, a verb ("I love") rather than a noun ("love"), or a form used when talking to someone directly. Where it matters, the full page shows the English meaning next to the word.
2. **The lists are lowercase.** They come from word counts, and counts ignore capital letters. German capitalizes every noun, so in the tables below we write German nouns the way they are written in a sentence: *Freiheit*, *Seele*, *Traum*.

For each word you get a short table, one line on what stands out, and a link to see it in every language we have.

## Using these words for tattoos, names, writing and gifts

People look up words like these for four reasons.

- **Tattoos.** A short word, often in a script you find beautiful. Korean, Georgian, Tamil, Hebrew and Arabic letters look good even to someone who can't read them.
- **Names.** Pets, boats, businesses, characters, sometimes children. Many languages have a word that sounds good in English too.
- **Writing and worldbuilding.** Fantasy writers and game designers borrow real words for places and spells. Words like [magic](/tools/say/magic), [sword](/tools/say/sword) and [universe](/tools/say/universe) are a good place to start.
- **Gifts.** A card, a bracelet or a print with a word in your partner's or friend's language.

Every one of those uses has the same risk. A word can be correct in a dictionary and still be wrong on your skin. It might be a verb form, a slang meaning, the wrong gender, or a word that native speakers only say in one particular context. The courage table above is a real example.

<Callout type="warning">
Before a word goes on a tattoo, a ring or a logo, have a native speaker look at it, in its script and in the exact font you'll use. Ask what it means to them, not just whether it is "correct". A <a href="/how-to-find-a-language-exchange-partner">language exchange partner</a> is ideal for this: they'll tell you honestly, and in return you help them with your language.
</Callout>

For writing a script correctly (Korean, Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic and others), the [alphabet pages](/tools/alphabet) show each letter and how it sounds. If you like how a language looks and want to go further, its [most common words list](/tools/most-common-words) is the best place to start.

## FAQ

### What is the most beautiful word in another language?

That depends on your taste, but words people choose often have a meaning English needs a phrase for. Korean 안녕 means "peace" and is also the everyday "hi" and "bye". Greek νίκη ("victory") gave us the name Nike.

### How do you say "soul" in different languages?

Spanish and Portuguese *alma*, French *âme*, Italian *anima*, German *Seele*, Greek ψυχή, Hindi आत्मा and Korean 영혼. The [soul page](/tools/say/soul) has 48 languages.

### Is it safe to get a tattoo from an online translation?

Not without a check. A word list or a translator can give you a verb form, slang, or a word with a second meaning you didn't intend. Ask at least one native speaker, and ideally two, before you commit.

### Why do some words have a different meaning on the full page?

The data comes from how often words are used in film and TV subtitles, and each word is matched to English through Wiktionary. Most matches are exact. Some are the closest common word, and those show the English meaning next to them so you can see the difference.

### Which words exist in all 53 languages?

Among the words on this page, love and light appear in all 53. Every [Say it in any language](/tools/say) page shows its count in the title, and every page has at least 30.
