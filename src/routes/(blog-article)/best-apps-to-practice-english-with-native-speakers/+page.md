The best apps to practice English with native speakers fall into three groups. **Language exchange apps** like HelloTalk, Tandem and LangX are free to start, and you pay for the practice by helping a native English speaker with your language. **Tutor services** like Cambly, Preply and italki give you a paid professional on a live video call. **AI speaking apps** like Speak and Praktika are not native speakers at all, but they let you talk out loud with no nerves before you try it with a person.

Most learners do best with one from the first group plus one from the second or third. Below: how to choose, what each app does well, and a first message native speakers actually answer.

## How to pick an app to practice English

English is one of the most studied languages in the world, so the question is less "is anyone out there" and more "what kind of practice do I need right now". Four things decide it.

1. **Do you need to speak live, or is writing and voice messages enough?** If your goal is a job interview or an exam speaking section, you need live conversation, and tutors or a call-friendly exchange app are the answer. If your goal is confidence and natural phrasing, text and voice messages give you time to think, which is often where real progress starts.
2. **What is your budget?** Exchange apps and AI apps have free tiers. Tutors do not, though most offer a trial lesson.
3. **What can you offer in return?** An exchange works because both people learn. Many native English speakers on exchange apps are learning widely taught languages such as Spanish, French, German, Japanese or Korean. If you speak one of those, partners come easily; if not, expect to send more first messages, or lean on tutors.
4. **Do you care which English?** American, British, Australian, Irish, Indian and South African English all work. If you need one accent for a specific exam or job, filter by country where the app allows it, or choose a tutor from that country.

## Language exchange apps: free practice with native English speakers

In a language exchange, you help someone with your native language and they help you with English. You get real conversation with a real native speaker, and it costs nothing but your time. If the idea is new to you, read [what a language exchange is](/what-is-a-language-exchange) first.

### HelloTalk

[HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com) is the largest exchange app and the easiest place to find English speakers quickly. Besides one-to-one chat, it has **Moments**, a public feed where you post a few sentences and native speakers correct them, and **Voicerooms**, live group audio rooms.

- **Best for:** learners who want lots of people and lots of formats, including live voice.
- **Keep in mind:** the free tier shows ads and caps translation and other extras; VIP removes them. Native English speakers on a busy app get many messages, so a specific first message matters (see below).

### Tandem

[Tandem](https://tandem.net) feels calmer than HelloTalk. New members are individually reviewed, and you browse profiles and filter by language and interests. The free plan includes text, voice messages, calls and corrections.

- **Best for:** finding one or two steady partners for regular calls.
- **Keep in mind:** the approval step can mean a short wait, and some filters and unlimited translation are in Tandem Pro.

### LangX

This is our app. [LangX](https://get.langx.io) matches in both directions: if you are learning English and speak, say, Portuguese, you only see English speakers who are learning Portuguese. That helps with the most common problem English learners hit on bigger apps: native speakers swamped with requests.

- **Best for:** writing practice with corrections at the center. Your partner can hold any message to correct it, and corrections are **unlimited on every plan**. Translation is built into the chat (20 a day on Free), and there are voice and photo messages and read-aloud for listening.
- **Keep in mind:** the community is much smaller than HelloTalk's or Tandem's. There are **no live calls**; speaking practice happens through voice messages. The Free plan allows 5 new conversations a day, with unlimited replies. There are no ads on any plan; see [plans](/pro) for the rest.

If you want to improve your written English slowly and deeply, pen-pal apps are underrated. ## Tutor apps: paid native English speakers on video

A tutor is not an exchange: you pay, and the whole lesson is about your English. That focus is worth it before an interview, an exam, or a move abroad.

- **[Cambly](https://www.cambly.com)** teaches English only, with native English-speaking tutors on video. You can often start a lesson with a tutor who is online right now, which suits irregular schedules. There is no free plan; you subscribe by minutes per week. Compare: [LangX vs Cambly](/open-source-alternative-to-cambly).
- **[Preply](https://preply.com)** is a marketplace. You filter tutors by price, reviews, specialty (business English, exam prep) and schedule, book a trial, then subscribe to regular lessons with one tutor. It suits people who want a fixed weekly rhythm. Compare: [LangX vs Preply](/open-source-alternative-to-preply).
- **[italki](https://www.italki.com)** has professional teachers and community tutors who set their own prices, plus a free community where you can post writing for correction. It no longer has the partner search it was once known for. Compare: [LangX vs italki](/open-source-alternative-to-italki).

## AI speaking apps: practice before you talk to people

AI apps cannot replace a native speaker, but if you freeze when you speak, they are a useful warm-up.

- **[Speak](https://www.speak.com)** is an AI speaking tutor with a structured course. You speak out loud from the first lesson and get instant feedback on pronunciation and phrasing. English for learners worldwide is one of its main courses. Compare: [LangX vs Speak](/open-source-alternative-to-speak).
- **[Praktika](https://praktika.ai)** lets you talk to animated AI avatar tutors, with real-time feedback on grammar, pronunciation and word choice. It started as an English app before adding other languages. Compare: [LangX vs Praktika](/open-source-alternative-to-praktika).

## Your first message to a native English speaker

Messages that get answers are short, specific, and offer something back. "Hi" on its own usually gets ignored. Try something like this:

> Hi Sam! I'm Ana from Brazil. I saw you're learning Portuguese, and I'm working on my English for my job in IT. Happy to help with your Portuguese, and I'd love corrections on my English. Did you start Portuguese for travel or for work?

It uses their profile, offers something before asking, requests corrections explicitly (many native speakers will not correct unless asked, because it feels rude) and ends with an easy question.

## Tips for English learners on exchange apps

**Split the time fairly.** Agree to alternate, for example half the chat in English and half in your language, or English on weekdays and your language at weekends. Partners leave when the exchange feels one-sided.

**Ask for the natural version, not just the correct one.** A sentence can be grammatically correct and still sound odd. Ask "How would you say this?" and you will learn the phrasal verbs and idioms textbooks skip, like "figure it out" or "run late".

**Use voice messages even if you type well.** Listen to each reply twice: once for meaning, once for how words link together. English is full of reduced forms ("gonna", "wanna", "didja") that you only learn by hearing them.

**Keep a phrase list.** Copy every correction into a notebook or flashcard app and review it a few days later. Corrections you never look at again do not stick; our post on [remembering a language for years](/stop-the-struggle-how-to-remember-your-foreign-languages-even-after-years) explains why.

**Fill vocabulary gaps between chats.** If you keep reaching for words you do not have, check your level with our free [English vocabulary test](/tools/vocabulary-test/english) and work through the [most common English words](/tools/most-common-words/english). A few hundred high-frequency words make conversations much easier to follow.

## FAQ

### What is the best free app to practice English with native speakers?

HelloTalk for the biggest community, Tandem for calmer one-to-one partners, and LangX for corrections on every message with no ads. All three are free to use; you help a native English speaker with your language in return.

### Can I practice English speaking with native speakers for free?

Yes. HelloTalk and Tandem both have free voice calls with partners, and HelloTalk's Voicerooms are free group audio. LangX supports voice messages but not live calls. Tutor services like Cambly and Preply are paid.

### Is Cambly or Preply better for English?

Cambly is English-only and good for speaking with a native tutor at short notice. Preply is better if you want to choose a specialist, such as business English or exam prep, and keep a fixed weekly schedule with one teacher.

### Why don't native English speakers reply to my messages?

On big apps they receive a lot of messages. Mention their profile, offer help with the language they are learning, and end with a question. Two-way matching, as in LangX, also helps, because everyone you see is learning your language.

### Are AI apps as good as native speakers?

No, but they are a useful warm-up. Use Speak or Praktika to build confidence, and real people for the humor, culture and natural phrasing AI tends to smooth over.
