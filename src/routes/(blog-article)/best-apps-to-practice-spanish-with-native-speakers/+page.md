The best apps to practice Spanish with native speakers are **HelloTalk** and **Tandem** for the largest pool of Spanish-speaking partners, **LangX** if you want every message open to correction and no ads, **Conversation Exchange** for meeting a partner in person, and **italki** or **Preply** if you want a paid tutor on video. Pair any of them with a listening resource like **Dreaming**, and you cover both halves of the job: understanding Spanish and producing it.

Spanish is one of the easiest languages to find partners for, because it is spoken across Spain and Latin America and many Spanish speakers are keen to practice English. The harder choices are which Spanish you want, and how much structure you need. This guide covers both.

## How to pick an app to practice Spanish

**Decide on a variety, loosely.** Spanish from Spain, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia and elsewhere is mutually intelligible, but the differences show up quickly in conversation:

- **Vosotros** (plural "you all") is used in most of Spain; Latin America uses **ustedes** for both formal and informal plural.
- **Vos** replaces tú in Argentina, Uruguay and parts of Central America, with its own verb forms (vos tenés rather than tú tienes).
- **Vocabulary** varies a lot. The word for "car", "juice" or "bus" depends on the country, and some everyday words in one country are rude in another.

If you have a reason to pick one (a move, a partner's family, a job), filter partners by country. If not, talk to several; your ear gets better from hearing the variety.

**Match the app to your level.** Beginners get more from text chat and voice messages, where you have time to look things up. Intermediate learners stuck at "I understand but can't speak" need live speaking: calls with a partner or a tutor.

**Be ready to give back.** In an exchange, your partner wants help with your language, most often English. That is good news: English-speaking learners of Spanish usually find partners quickly.

## Language exchange apps for Spanish

### HelloTalk

[HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com) is the biggest exchange community, so Spanish-speaking partners are easy to find. Its **Moments** feed is especially useful for Spanish: post three sentences about your weekend and several native speakers from different countries correct them, which shows you regional differences side by side.

- **Best for:** volume and variety, and live group audio in Voicerooms.
- **Keep in mind:** the free tier has ads and daily caps on translation; VIP removes them. Popular profiles get many messages, so write a specific opener.

Compare: [LangX vs HelloTalk](/open-source-alternative-to-hellotalk).

### Tandem

[Tandem](https://tandem.net) reviews new members and focuses on one-to-one exchange. Filtering for a partner in a particular country is a good way to practice the Spanish you actually need.

- **Best for:** finding a regular partner for weekly calls.
- **Keep in mind:** approval can take time, and some filters and unlimited translation are in Tandem Pro.

Compare: [LangX vs Tandem](/open-source-alternative-to-tandem), or read [Tandem vs HelloTalk](/tandem-vs-hellotalk).

### LangX

Our app. [LangX](https://get.langx.io) matches in both directions: set Spanish as the language you are learning and English as your native one, and you see Spanish speakers who are learning English. Your partner can hold any message to correct it, and corrections are **unlimited on every plan**. Translation is inside the chat (20 a day on Free), and read-aloud lets you hear a message spoken.

- **Best for:** writing practice where fixing mistakes is the point, with no ads.
- **Keep in mind:** the community is far smaller than HelloTalk's or Tandem's, and there are **no live calls**, only voice, photo and text messages. Free allows 5 new conversations a day; replies are unlimited. See [plans](/pro).

### Speaky

[Speaky](https://www.speaky.com) is a long-running exchange app that asks for no subscription at the time of writing, and it lists over 170 languages. It is a quick way to start chatting with Spanish speakers today, though it has fewer learning tools inside the chat. Compare: [LangX vs Speaky](/open-source-alternative-to-speaky).

### Conversation Exchange

If you live in a city with Spanish speakers, [Conversation Exchange](https://www.conversationexchange.com) is worth a look. It is a website, not an app, and its local search helps you find someone for a face-to-face coffee exchange. ## Tutors and AI for Spanish speaking practice

**[italki](https://www.italki.com)** and **[Preply](https://preply.com)** both list many Spanish tutors. You can pick a tutor from Spain, Mexico or anywhere else, which is the most direct way to practice one variety. italki has professional teachers and community tutors for conversation; Preply books a trial and then a regular subscription with one tutor. See [LangX vs italki](/open-source-alternative-to-italki) and [LangX vs Preply](/open-source-alternative-to-preply).

**[Speak](https://www.speak.com)** and **[Praktika](https://praktika.ai)** both teach Spanish with AI. Speak runs a structured speaking course with instant feedback; Praktika uses animated avatar tutors. They are good for rehearsing before you talk to people, not a replacement for them. If you came from Duolingo and want to speak more, our [Duolingo alternatives for speaking](/duolingo-alternatives-for-speaking) goes further.

## Your first message in Spanish

Keep it short, friendly, and specific to their profile. Tú is normal between exchange partners of similar age; switch to usted only if your partner is much older or the setting is formal.

> ¡Hola, Lucía! ¿Qué tal? Me llamo Sam y soy de Canadá. Estoy aprendiendo español y vi que estás aprendiendo inglés. ¿Te ayudo con el inglés y tú me corriges el español?

In English: "Hi Lucía! How's it going? My name is Sam and I'm from Canada. I'm learning Spanish and I saw you're learning English. Shall I help you with English and you correct my Spanish?"

Three more phrases you will use every week:

- **¿Cómo se dice... en español?** How do you say... in Spanish?
- **¿Suena natural?** Does it sound natural?
- **Más despacio, por favor.** More slowly, please.

For what to say after the first message, keep our [conversation topics](/language-exchange-conversation-topics) open, and see [how to do a language exchange](/how-to-do-a-language-exchange) for splitting the time fairly.

## Tips for Spanish learners on exchange apps

**Ask partners to correct your ser/estar, por/para and gender agreement.** These are the mistakes learners make constantly and rarely notice. Asking for them by name gets you better corrections.

**Set up a Spanish keyboard.** Typing ñ, á and the opening ¿ and ¡ properly takes seconds with the right keyboard, and your partner will read you more easily.

**Send voice messages early.** Spanish spelling is close to how it sounds, so your pronunciation can improve fast, but only if someone hears it. A 20-second voice message a day adds up.

**Notice the variety, do not panic about it.** If one partner says "coche" and another "carro", write both down with the country. It is the same language.

## FAQ

### What is the best free app to practice Spanish with native speakers?

HelloTalk and Tandem have the most Spanish speakers and free tiers with ads and caps. Speaky asks for no subscription. LangX has no ads and unlimited corrections, with 5 new conversations a day on Free.

### Should I learn Spanish from Spain or Latin America?

Either works, and speakers understand each other easily. Pick the one you are most likely to use. If you have no preference, practicing with partners from several countries is the best training for your ear.

### Can I practice Spanish speaking for free?

Yes. HelloTalk and Tandem include free calls with partners, and Conversation Exchange helps you meet someone in person. LangX supports voice messages but not live calls.

### Is Dreaming Spanish enough to become fluent?

It builds listening very well, but most learners also need to speak with people to become comfortable producing Spanish. Use it alongside a language exchange partner or a tutor.
