Modern Eastern Armenian also treats the ligature և (yev) as a letter, which is why our chart lists 39. It is a true alphabet with capital and small letters, written left to right, and most letters have one fixed sound, so you can learn to read it in a few weeks of steady practice.

## What kind of script is Armenian?

Armenian is an alphabet in the same sense as English: vowels and consonants are full letters, written one after another from left to right. The shapes are its own. Our alphabet data describes it as "devised in the fifth century for this language alone", and nothing else in use today looks like it.

The alphabet is credited to Mesrop Mashtots, a monk and scholar who created it around 405 AD so that the Bible and church texts could be written in Armenian. His alphabet had 36 letters. Two more, Օ (o) and Ֆ (f), were added in the Middle Ages, giving the 38 letters most references list today.

The order of the alphabet largely follows Greek, with Armenian-only letters slotted in: Ա (ayb), Բ (ben), Գ (gim), Դ (da), Ե (ech) run parallel to alpha, beta, gamma, delta, epsilon. If you know [the Greek alphabet](/greek-alphabet-guide), the first few letters come in a familiar order even though the shapes don't match. Like Greek letters, Armenian letters also served as numbers: Է was 7, Ի was 20, Լ was 30, and so on up to 9,000.

### Capital and small letters

Unlike [Georgian](/georgian-alphabet-guide), its neighbor, Armenian has upper and lower case. Every letter on our chart comes as a pair, such as Ա ա and Բ բ, and capitals are used at the start of sentences and names, as in English. The one exception is և, which has only a small form. At the start of a sentence it is written as two letters, Եվ.

Two punctuation marks catch beginners out. The Armenian full stop is ։, which looks like a colon. And the question mark, ՞, is not written at the end of the sentence but on top of the word being asked about.

## Armenian vowels

Armenian has seven vowel letters, plus one two-letter combination for the oo sound.

| Letter | Name | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ա ա | ayb | a in father |
| Ե ե | ech | ye at the start of a word, e elsewhere |
| Է է | eh | e in bet |
| Ը ը | et | u in but |
| Ի ի | ini | ee in see |
| Ո ո | vo | vo at the start of a word, o elsewhere |
| Օ օ | oh | o in more |
| ՈՒ ու | (two letters) | oo in boot |

Two pairs share a sound. ե and է are both e, and ո and օ are both o; the difference is that ե adds a y and ո adds a v at the start of a word. So երեկ (yesterday, #527) is *ye-rek*, and ոչ (no, #11) is *voch*.

The oo sound has no letter of its own: it is written with two letters, ո plus ւ, much as Greek writes it with ου. In modern Eastern spelling you will meet ւ (yiwn) only inside ու and inside the ligature և, as our chart notes, so read ու as a single oo.

## Armenian consonants

Fifteen consonants fall into five sets of three. In Eastern Armenian each set has a voiced sound, a sound with a puff of air, and a plain sound with no puff of air. English has no separate letter for that plain sound; it is close to the p in "spin" or the t in "stop".

| Sound family | Voiced | With a puff of air | Plain, no puff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| p | Բ բ ben (b) | Փ փ piwr | Պ պ peh |
| k | Գ գ gim (g) | Ք ք keh | Կ կ ken |
| t | Դ դ da (d) | Թ թ to | Տ տ tiwn |
| ts | Ձ ձ ja (dz) | Ց ց co | Ծ ծ ca |
| ch | Ջ ջ jheh (j) | Չ չ cha | Ճ ճ cheh |

The other 15 consonants:

| Letter | Name | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Զ զ | za | z |
| Ժ ժ | zhe | s in measure |
| Լ լ | liwn | l |
| Խ խ | xeh | ch in loch |
| Հ հ | ho | h |
| Ղ ղ | ghat | French r |
| Մ մ | men | m |
| Յ յ | yi | y |
| Ն ն | now | n |
| Շ շ | sha | sh |
| Ռ ռ | ra | rolled r |
| Ս ս | seh | s |
| Վ վ | vew | v |
| Ր ր | reh | r |
| Ֆ ֆ | feh | f |

Note the two r's: ռ is a strong rolled r, while ր is a lighter r. Our chart's names are romanized from the traditional ones, so you'll see other spellings elsewhere, such as *yech* for ech or *nu* for now.

## Eastern and Western Armenian

Armenian has two standard forms. Eastern Armenian is the language of Armenia; Western Armenian is spoken mainly in the diaspora. They share the alphabet, but Western Armenian reads several consonants differently, and it keeps the older, classical spelling, while Armenia uses the reformed spelling of the Soviet period.

The biggest difference is in the sound families above. Where Eastern has three sounds, Western has two, and the letters swap roles:

| Letters | Eastern | Western |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Բ բ / Պ պ | b / p | p / b |
| Գ գ / Կ կ | g / k | k / g |
| Դ դ / Տ տ | d / t | t / d |
| Ձ ձ / Ծ ծ | dz / ts | ts / dz |
| Ջ ջ / Ճ ճ | j / ch | ch / j |

So the same written word can sound different. **բարև** (hello, #345) is *barev* in Eastern and *parev* in Western. **պարոն** (gentleman, #56) is *paron* in the East and *baron* in the West. **գիշեր** (night, #78) is *gisher* in the East and *kisher* in the West.

Our chart and word list follow Eastern Armenian. If your partner speaks Western Armenian, the shapes stay the same; only some sounds change.

## Look-alike letters

Armenian's small letters are full of shapes that look like Latin letters but aren't. These are the ones beginners misread most:

| Armenian | Looks like | Actually |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ո | n | vo / o |
| ս | u | s |
| մ | u with a tail | m |
| ի | h | ee |
| ժ | d | zh (s in measure) |
| չ | z | ch with a puff of air |
| ց | g | ts with a puff of air |
| գ and զ | q (both) | g and z |

Among the capitals, Ս looks like a Latin U and Ո like an upside-down U. A few letters do match what they look like: Լ լ is an l, and Օ օ is an o.

## The ligature և

և is a joined form of ե and ւ, read *yev*. On its own it is the word "and": it is the 6th most common word on our list, right above ու (also "and", #7). Inside a word it stands for *ev* or *yev*, and our word list shows how common it is:

| Word | Rank | Meaning | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| և | 6 | and | yev |
| մինչև | 95 | until | min-chev |
| այլևս | 97 | no longer | ay-levs |
| նաև | 138 | also | na-yev |
| ևս | 323 | more, also | yevs |

A small warning about the top of the word list itself: some high-ranked entries are single letters glossed as numerals. The #1 entry, է, is glossed there as "7 in the system of Armenian numerals", but in running text it is almost always the word "is". Treat the one-letter entries with care.

## FAQ

### How many letters are in the Armenian alphabet?

38 in most references: the 36 created by Mesrop Mashtots and the two added in the Middle Ages, Օ and Ֆ. Modern Eastern Armenian also counts the ligature և as a letter, which gives 39, the number on our chart.

### Who created the Armenian alphabet?

Mesrop Mashtots, a monk and scholar, around 405 AD. It was made for the Armenian language, so that church texts could be written in it.

### Does Armenian have capital letters?

Yes. Every letter has a capital and a small form, used much as in English, at the start of sentences and names. The ligature և has only a small form.

### What is the difference between Eastern and Western Armenian pronunciation?

Mainly five pairs of consonants. Letters read b, g, d, dz and j in Eastern are read p, k, t, ts and ch in Western, and the reverse, so բարև (hello) is barev in Armenia and parev in Western Armenian.

### Is the Armenian alphabet hard to learn?

The letters are new, but most have one fixed sound, so reading is regular once you know them. The main difficulties are the look-alike small letters and the plain p, t and k sounds that English speakers tend to say with a puff of air.
