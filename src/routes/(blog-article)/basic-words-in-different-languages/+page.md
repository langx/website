Hello is *hola* in Spanish, *привет* in Russian, 你好 in Chinese and *merhaba* in Turkish. Every word here comes from real usage data, not from a phrasebook, and each section links to the full page on our free [Say it in any language](/tools/say) tool.

## Where these words come from

Each [/tools/say](/tools/say) page lists an English word in up to 53 languages. The words are ranked by how often they appear in film and TV subtitles (the OpenSubtitles 2018 frequency lists) and matched to English through Wiktionary. So these are words people actually say out loud, and every page shows where the word ranks in its language.

A few things to know before you read the tables:

- **The lists count single words.** A two-word phrase like "good night" can't appear as one entry, so some languages are missing from some tables.
- **Every row has an English meaning.** We only print rows whose meaning matches the sense of the section. When a page matched "thanks" to "thanks to", or "please" to "to please", we left that row out.
- **The lists are lowercase.** We capitalize German nouns (*Nacht*, *Wasser*) the way you'd see them in a sentence. The Chinese entries in this data are written in traditional characters.

## Why a word list is not a phrasebook

Knowing *danke* or 감사합니다 gets you surprisingly far. But a single word leaves out the parts that make it polite or natural.

- **Politeness has levels.** Korean 감사합니다 is glossed simply "thank you", while the thanks page matches 고마워, a more casual form. Turkish *affedersiniz* and Ukrainian *вибачте* are polite forms you'd use with a stranger. One English word often splits into a formal and a casual version, and a list can only show one.
- **Context changes the word.** "Excuse me" to get past someone, to get a waiter's attention and to apologize can be three different words. The same goes for "please" when you're asking for something versus begging.
- **Some words only live in phrases.** Spanish please, "good night" and "you're welcome" are usually two or more words, so a single-word list shows fragments or nothing.

The fix is to hear the words in real conversation. Our guide to [how to do a language exchange](/how-to-do-a-language-exchange) covers how to structure a session so both people practice, and greetings, thanks and small apologies come up in almost every one of the [language exchange conversation topics](/language-exchange-conversation-topics) we suggest.

If you want to go beyond survival words, the [most common words lists](/tools/most-common-words) show the top words in each language, and [how many words you need to be fluent](/how-many-words-do-you-need-to-be-fluent) puts those lists in context. If your language uses another script, the [alphabet pages](/tools/alphabet) show every letter.

## FAQ

### How do you say "hello" in different languages?

Spanish *hola*, Italian *ciao*, Portuguese *olá*, Russian привет, Greek γεια, Persian سلام, Turkish *merhaba* and Chinese 你好. The [hello page](/tools/say/hello) has 46 languages.

### How do you say "thank you" in different languages?

French *merci*, German *danke*, Portuguese *obrigado*, Russian спасибо, Polish *dziękuję*, Hindi धन्यवाद, Korean 감사합니다 and Tagalog *salamat*. See the [thank](/tools/say/thank) and [thanks](/tools/say/thanks) pages for more.

### Which basic word is the most common across languages?

In this data, yes. It is the single most used word in Macedonian (да) and in the top 20 for many others, including Albanian, Slovenian, Hebrew, Turkish and Telugu.

### Why are some languages missing from a table?

We only include rows whose English meaning matches the word in the section. If a language's closest match on the page was a different sense, or the word is normally a phrase, we left it out rather than fill the gap from memory.
