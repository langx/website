If you want an open source alternative to Babbel for speaking with real people, LangX is a free, open source (BSD-3) language exchange app where you chat one-to-one with native speakers and every message can be corrected. It does not replace Babbel's structured course, so the fair answer to "LangX vs Babbel" is that Babbel teaches you the language and LangX gives you people to use it with.

We make LangX, so we are not neutral, but we will be clear about where Babbel is the better tool. Everything about Babbel below comes from its official site, help center, press releases and app store listings at the time of writing (September 2026).

## Babbel and LangX are different kinds of app

[Babbel](https://www.babbel.com) is a **structured course app**. It was founded in Berlin in 2007, runs on a subscription, and teaches 14 languages through short lessons built around practical dialogues, with clear grammar explanations and a review tool that brings vocabulary back before you forget it. It also has podcasts, videos and, since September 2025, **Babbel Speak**, an AI conversation feature that guides you through spoken real-life scenarios.

LangX is a **language exchange app**. There are no lessons. You are matched with people who speak the language you are learning and are learning a language you speak, and you have real conversations, with corrections, translation and voice messages built into the chat.

So a "Babbel alternative" can mean two things:

1. **Another course app.** LangX is not that; Busuu is the closest like-for-like.
2. **A place to actually talk with native speakers,** which a course cannot give you on its own. That is what LangX is for.

## What happened to Babbel Live?

Babbel Live, Babbel's live online classes with human teachers, was **discontinued for individual learners in 2025**. According to Babbel's help center, the last classes could be booked until June 30, 2025, and learners kept access to their class history and materials until August 31, 2025. Live classes are still offered to companies through Babbel for Business, but they are no longer part of a personal subscription.

If you came here looking for a Babbel Live alternative, you have two routes: a paid tutor marketplace like italki for teacher-led lessons, or a free language exchange like LangX for conversation with native speakers.

## What Babbel does well

Babbel is one of the most respected course apps, and it earns that.

- **Lessons built around real dialogues.** You learn phrases in the context of a conversation, not as isolated words, which transfers well to actual speaking.
- **Grammar that is actually explained.** Short, clear notes tell you why something works, which many learners miss in more game-like apps.
- **Review that sticks.** The review tool brings back words and phrases at spaced intervals.
- **Babbel Speak.** Since September 2025, Babbel's AI conversation feature walks beginners through spoken scenarios in Spanish, German, French and Italian on the mobile app. It is a gentle bridge from silence to speaking.
- **Short and consistent.** Lessons fit into a commute, and the course always tells you what comes next.
- **Try before you pay.** The first lesson of every course is free to try without payment details, so you can judge the teaching style first.
- **A long track record.** Babbel has been teaching languages since 2007 and lists more than 50 million downloads on Google Play.

For a beginner or a returning learner who wants a clear, well-explained path, Babbel is a very reasonable choice.

## Where LangX differs from Babbel

### It is the conversation layer

A course teaches you how to ask for directions; it cannot give you a person who answers in an accent you did not expect. LangX is the missing piece: conversations with native speakers who are learning your language in return. Every profile in Discover is a two-way match, so both of you get something out of every chat.

### Human corrections, unlimited

On LangX, your partner holds any message to correct it, and corrections are unlimited on every plan, including Free. Human corrections often point out what sounds natural or old-fashioned, which automatic checking can miss. You correct them in your language too.

### Many more languages

Babbel teaches 14 languages. LangX lists 182, so if your target language is not in Babbel's catalog, a language exchange may be your main way to practice at all.

### Open source and a free plan that stays free

The LangX app and API are public on [GitHub](https://github.com/langx/langx) under the BSD-3 license and can be self-hosted. Babbel is closed source. LangX Free is a real plan, not a sample: unlimited text messages, unlimited replies and corrections, 5 new conversations and 20 translations per rolling 24 hours. See the [plans page](/pro) for what Fluent and Polyglot add.

### No ads

LangX has no ads and no advertising identifiers. It does use product analytics (PostHog on its EU cloud, including a masked screen recording on iOS and Android where all text and images are replaced by grey blocks on the device). It is on by default and switches off in Settings → Privacy → Share usage data. The [privacy policy](/privacy-policy) spells out what is sent.

### What LangX does not do

- **No lessons, grammar explanations or course path.** If you are starting from zero, begin with a course.
- **No AI conversation practice today.** LangX Copilot, private AI feedback, is planned for Polyglot but not shipped.
- **No live calls or classes,** only voice messages.
- **A smaller community,** so some language pairs have fewer people online.

## Is LangX a free Babbel alternative?

For the speaking part, yes. LangX Free has no time limit and no ads, and replies and corrections are never capped. It is not a free Babbel course, and it will not teach you grammar from scratch. If you want a free course, you will have to look at other apps; if you want free conversation practice, LangX is built for it.

## LangX vs Babbel: who should pick which?

**Pick Babbel if you:**

- are a beginner and want a clear, structured course
- like understanding the grammar behind what you say
- learn one of Babbel's 14 languages
- want AI-guided speaking before you face a real person

**Pick LangX if you:**

- know the basics and want real conversations with native speakers
- want an open source alternative to Babbel you can audit or self-host
- want unlimited human corrections without paying
- lost access to Babbel Live and want regular speaking practice

**Use both if you want steady progress.** This is what we would suggest:

1. Do a Babbel lesson to learn a new structure or set of phrases.
2. Use it that same day in a LangX conversation.
3. Let your partner correct you, and add the fixes to your notes.

The course gives you the building blocks; the exchange makes you use them. ## FAQ

### What is the best open source alternative to Babbel?

For speaking with native speakers, LangX is an open source alternative to Babbel: its app and API are BSD-3 licensed on GitHub. It is not a course, and there is no widely used open source equivalent of Babbel's full curriculum.

### Is there a free Babbel alternative?

LangX is free for conversation practice: no time limit, no ads, unlimited replies and corrections, 5 new conversations a day and 20 translations a day. Babbel itself lets you try the first lesson of each course for free, but full access needs a subscription.

### Is Babbel Live still available?

Not for individual learners. Babbel discontinued Babbel Live for private learners in 2025, with the last classes bookable until June 30, 2025. It remains available to companies through Babbel for Business.

### LangX vs Babbel: which is better for speaking?

Babbel is better for learning what to say, and Babbel Speak lets you rehearse with AI. LangX is better for real conversations with native speakers. Most learners get the best results by combining a course with an exchange.

### Are there apps like Babbel with real conversation partners?

Language exchange apps like LangX, Tandem and HelloTalk connect you with native speakers for free, and Busuu adds native-speaker corrections to a course. The [best language exchange apps](/best-language-exchange-apps) roundup compares the exchange options.
