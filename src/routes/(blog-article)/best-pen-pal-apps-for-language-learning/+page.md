## Slowly: best for slow, thoughtful letters

[Slowly](https://slowly.app) is the app most people mean when they say "pen pal app." It launched on iOS in 2017 and turns messages into letters that "travel": delivery time depends on the real distance between you and your pen pal, from about half an hour for someone nearby to around 60 hours for the other side of the world. The Android app was updated as recently as this month, so it is very much alive.

**Why it works for learners:** because you wait for every reply, people write real letters, not one-line messages. Reading a full page of natural writing from a native speaker, then answering at your own pace with a dictionary open, is excellent reading and writing practice for intermediate learners. You set your languages with a proficiency level and can filter pen pals by language, topics and region. Identity is a nickname and an avatar, which many shy learners find freeing.

**Limits:** there are no correction tools, so you have to ask for feedback in the letter itself. The delay that makes letters thoughtful also means you might exchange only a few a week. Slowly has a free tier with a friend quota, a paid Slowly Plus membership, and collectible stamps; its Google Play listing says the app contains ads.

## InterPals: best for the biggest pen-pal community

[InterPals](https://www.interpals.net) calls itself "the original pen pal site" and has run since 1998. It says it has more than 8.5 million users in 180+ countries. You can search by country, age and the languages people speak or are learning, and message them for free.

**Why it works for learners:** sheer size. For a less common language, it is one of the likeliest places to find a native speaker who wants to write, and it suits people who want friendship as much as grammar.

**Limits:** it is a general social site, not a learning tool, so many members are there to make friends rather than trade languages. You will do more filtering, and there is no built-in way to correct each other's messages. ## Conversation Exchange: best directory with a pen-pal mode

[Conversation Exchange](https://www.conversationexchange.com) is a long-running, simple website that offers three kinds of partners: face-to-face, chat, and **pen pal (correspondence)**. In pen-pal mode you search for people who want written exchanges, then write back and forth to practice reading and writing.

**Why it works for learners:** everyone on it is there for languages. Profiles list native and practice languages up front, so the matching is more focused than on a general pen-pal site.

**Limits:** it is a directory first. The site is plain and web-only, and for anything beyond messages you usually move to another app. Our [open source alternative to Conversation Exchange](/open-source-alternative-to-conversationexchange) page compares it with LangX.

## PenPal World and Global Penfriends: general pen-pal sites

Two older sites are still active and worth a look if the options above do not have your language:

- **[PenPal World](https://www.penpalworld.com)** is a general pen-pal social network. A free account lets you contact a small number of members per day, and a paid VIP membership raises that limit. It is friendship-first, so be clear in your profile that you want a language exchange.
- **[Global Penfriends](https://www.globalpenfriends.com)** has been online since the mid-1990s and still offers something rare: **real snail mail**. You can message online or exchange handwritten letters and postcards. Its interest groups include language partners.

## How to use a pen pal to actually learn a language

### Write in your target language, then let them answer in theirs

The classic setup is simple: you write in the language you are learning, your pen pal writes in the one they are learning, and each of you corrects the other. Alternatively, split each letter in half, one part in each language. Agree on this in your first letter so nobody ends up writing only in English. Our guide to [how to do a language exchange](/how-to-do-a-language-exchange) covers the ground rules in more detail.

### Ask for corrections, and say how many

Most pen pals will not correct you unless you ask, because it feels rude. Tell them what you want: "Please correct up to five mistakes per letter, the ones a native speaker would notice most." That is more useful than a letter covered in red ink. On an app with correction tools this is easier, since your partner can hold your message and fix it without writing a separate reply.

### Keep a mistake log

Copy each correction into a notebook or a note on your phone: what you wrote, what they suggested, and why. Review it before writing your next letter. Patterns show up fast, and fixing a pattern is worth more than fixing a hundred single mistakes.

### Reuse their language

When your pen pal uses a phrase you like, steal it in your next letter. Their writing is a personalized textbook. If you want a head start on vocabulary, look at the [most common words in Spanish](/tools/most-common-words/spanish) or [in French](/tools/most-common-words/french); they make up a large share of any letter you will read.

### Always leave a question

End every letter with a question or two. It makes replying easy and keeps the correspondence going. If you run dry, borrow from our [language exchange conversation topics](/language-exchange-conversation-topics).

Both work. They train different things.

## FAQ

### What is the best pen pal app for language learning?

For thoughtful written practice, Slowly is the best-known pen pal app. For the largest pool of people and languages, InterPals. For a site where everyone is there to trade languages, Conversation Exchange. If you want corrections built into the chat, use a language exchange app like LangX alongside one of them.

### Is Slowly good for learning a language?

Yes, for reading and writing, especially at intermediate level and above. You set your languages and levels and can find pen pals who write in them. It has no correction tools, so ask your pen pal to correct a few mistakes in each letter.

### Are pen pal apps free?

The ones in this guide can all be used for free, with limits on some (for example daily contacts or friend quotas), and several offer optional paid memberships. LangX has a free plan with unlimited replies and corrections.

### How do I find a pen pal who writes in my target language?

Filter by the language you are learning, write a profile that says you want a language exchange by letter, and include a line in your target language. Then write to several people; not everyone replies.

### Can I find a snail mail pen pal for a language?

Yes. Global Penfriends supports real postcards and letters, and its interest groups include language partners. Expect replies to take weeks.
