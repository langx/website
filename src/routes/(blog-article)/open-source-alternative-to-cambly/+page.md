If you want a free, open source alternative to Cambly for extra English practice, LangX is a language exchange app with public code (BSD-3) that matches you with native English speakers who are learning your language, with corrections on any message, unlimited on every plan. It does not replace a Cambly tutor: Cambly sells live video lessons with paid tutors, and LangX is for the free peer practice you do between those lessons.

This page compares LangX vs Cambly as of September 2026. We make LangX, and we will be clear about where a paid tutor is simply the better choice.

## Cambly and LangX are different kinds of service

**Cambly** is an online English tutoring service. You subscribe to a plan, then book or start video lessons with native English-speaking tutors, around the clock. It teaches English only, and it has a separate program for children, Cambly Kids.

**LangX** is a free language exchange app. There are no tutors and no lessons. You are matched with someone who speaks the language you are learning and is learning a language you speak, and you help each other in a one-to-one chat with corrections, translation and voice messages built in.

So a "Cambly alternative" can mean two things:

1. **Another tutoring service** (for example Preply or italki), if you want a professional on the other end of the call. LangX is not that.
2. **A free way to practice English with native speakers** in between lessons, or before you are ready to pay for them. That is what LangX is for.

## What Cambly does well

Cambly is popular with English learners for good reasons, and it is better than LangX at several things:

- **A native English speaker, whenever you want.** Cambly advertises lessons "anytime, anywhere", and you can often start a call with a tutor who is online right now. For shift workers and busy parents, that flexibility is rare.
- **Speaking out loud, live.** Every Cambly lesson is a video conversation. If your goal is to stop freezing when you speak English, few things beat regular live practice with someone whose job is to keep you talking.
- **Someone who is paid to focus on you.** A tutor adapts the conversation to your goals, whether that is general conversation, business English or interview practice, and does not need anything back from you.
- **Structure if you want it.** Plans range from small group lessons to private lessons, and higher tiers add recordings, transcripts, automated feedback after lessons and self-study recommendations tied to your goals. There is a separate program for kids.
- **Low friction.** You can sign up and look around before paying, and plans are sold by how many minutes a day and days a week you want, so you commit to a routine rather than to single lessons.

If you are preparing for an interview, an exam or a move abroad and can budget for it, a Cambly tutor gives you guided speaking practice that a free exchange cannot.

## Where LangX differs from Cambly

### Free peer practice between lessons

Most learners who take paid lessons can afford one or a few a week. What happens between those lessons often decides how fast they improve. LangX is built for that gap: daily conversations in writing and voice messages with someone who speaks English natively and wants help with your language in return. Our post on [why consistent practice is key](/why-consistent-practice-is-key-to-language-learning-success) explains why those small daily sessions add up.

### An open source alternative to Cambly

The LangX app and API are public on [GitHub](https://github.com/langx/langx) under the BSD-3 license. You can read the code, see what the app sends, contribute, or host your own instance. Cambly is closed source. If transparency matters to you, that is a reason to look for an open source alternative to Cambly, at least for the practice part.

### Two-way matching instead of a paid hour

On Cambly you pay for a tutor's time. On LangX nobody pays anybody: the person who corrects your English is someone you help with your native language. That also means you do half the teaching. It works best when you enjoy explaining your own language, and [how to do a language exchange](/how-to-do-a-language-exchange) shows how to split the time fairly.

One honest caveat: matching depends on who is learning your language. If you speak Spanish, Japanese or Portuguese, plenty of English speakers are learning it. If your native language is less commonly studied, you may find fewer English-speaking partners.

### Corrections you can keep

On LangX you hold any message to correct it, and corrections are unlimited on every plan, Free included. Because the conversation is written down, every correction stays in the chat for you to review later, and read-aloud lets you hear any message spoken.

### Not live, and not a teacher

LangX has no live calls. Conversations are text, voice, photo and video messages. And a partner is not a trained teacher: they can tell you what sounds natural, but they may not be able to explain why. If you need grammar explained or want live speaking under pressure, keep your tutor.

### No ads

LangX shows no ads and uses no advertising identifiers. It does have product analytics (PostHog on its EU cloud, including a masked screen recording on iOS and Android where every word and image is replaced by a grey block on the device). It is on by default and switches off in Settings → Privacy → Share usage data; the [privacy policy](/privacy-policy) lists exactly what is sent.

## Is LangX a free Cambly alternative?

For practice, yes; for lessons, no. LangX Free includes unlimited text messages, unlimited replies and corrections, 5 new conversations a day and 20 translations a day, with no ads. Fluent and Polyglot add unlimited new conversations, more translations and more languages; see the [plans page](/pro). Prices are set per region and shown in the app. What you do not get on any plan is a tutor.

**Pick Cambly if you:**

- want live video conversation with a native English speaker, on demand
- are preparing for an interview, exam or move and want guided practice

## FAQ

### Is there an open source alternative to Cambly?

We know of no open source tutoring service like Cambly. LangX is an open source alternative to Cambly for the practice part: a free language exchange app, public under the BSD-3 license, where you chat with native English speakers who are learning your language.

### What is the best free Cambly alternative for practicing English?

A language exchange app. LangX's Free plan has no ads and includes unlimited replies and corrections and 5 new conversations a day. You practice English with native speakers and help them with your language in return.

### LangX vs Cambly: can LangX replace my tutor?

No. Cambly gives you live lessons with a tutor who focuses on you; LangX gives you free written and voice-message practice with a peer. Most learners get the best results using a tutor for guidance and an exchange for daily volume.

### Does LangX have video calls like Cambly?

No. LangX has voice, photo and video messages and read-aloud, but no live calls. If live video is what you need, Cambly, Preply, italki or Tandem are better fits.

Preply and italki are the closest apps like Cambly, with paid tutors in many languages. For free practice, language exchange apps such as LangX, Tandem and HelloTalk. The [compare page](/compare) lists every LangX comparison.
