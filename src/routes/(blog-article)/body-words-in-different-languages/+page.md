Eye is *ojos* (eyes) in Spanish, چشم in Persian, 눈 in Korean and *mata* in Tagalog. Hand is *main* in French, دست in Persian, হাত in Bengali and *tay* in Vietnamese, and head is *Kopf* in German, ראש in Hebrew and 머리 in Korean. Below are 12 body words, from eye and hand to arm, leg and skin, across all 52 languages on the site besides English and more than a dozen scripts.

Every word comes from real usage data, not a phrasebook, and each section links to the full list on our free [Say it in any language](/tools/say) tool.

## Where these words come from

Each [/tools/say](/tools/say) page lists an English word in up to 53 languages. The words are ranked by how often they appear in film and TV subtitles (the OpenSubtitles 2018 frequency lists) and matched to English through Wiktionary, so every page shows where the word ranks in its language.

Two things to know:

- **Frequency lists count spellings, not meanings.** If a word for "leg" is spelled like the everyday word for "morning", it ranks very high for the wrong reason. We only print rows whose meaning matches, and we say where a page's "most common" claim is one of these.
- **The lists keep the form people used.** Body parts often come in pairs, so many rows are plurals (*ojos*, *yeux*, глаза), and some are case forms. We mark the plurals and capitalize German nouns (*Augen*, *Kopf*).

## Mix-ups to watch for

Frequency data is honest about what people say, but it can't tell a body part from another word spelled the same way. The ones worth knowing:

- **Another everyday word.** Tamil காலை (morning) on the leg page, Urdu وجہ (reason) and Italian *volta* (time) on the face page, Polish *ok* on the eye page.
- **Phrases from film dialogue.** "On the other hand", "to hand over", "shut up" and "to keep an eye on" all put rows on these pages.
- **Weapons.** English "arms" means weapons too, so eight rows on the arm page are words for "weapon".
- **Corpses.** Crime shows talk about "the body", so several rows on the body page mean a dead one.
- **A data error.** Georgian თმა is listed as the most common word in Georgian on the hair page.
- **Plurals.** Eyes, hands, legs and ears come in pairs, so many European rows are plurals.

If you're reading a new script to check a word like this, the [Persian](/tools/alphabet/persian), [Bengali](/tools/alphabet/bengali), [Georgian](/tools/alphabet/georgian) and [Korean](/tools/alphabet/korean) alphabet pages show every letter.

## Learn body words by talking about your day

Body words come up whenever you talk about how you feel: a headache, tired eyes, a sore leg after a run. Health, sport and daily routines are easy [language exchange conversation topics](/language-exchange-conversation-topics), and the vocabulary sticks because it is about you.

- **Say what hurts.** "My head hurts" or "my eyes are tired" is one of the most useful sentences in any language, and your partner can tell you how people actually say it.
- **Describe someone.** Hair, eyes and face are how you describe a friend, a family member or a character in a film.
- **Ask about idioms.** Body words carry a lot of idioms. Ask your partner what "on the other hand" or "shut up" sounds like in their language, and how rude it is.

## FAQ

### How do you say "skin" in different languages?

Spanish *piel*, French *peau*, Italian *pelle*, Greek δέρμα, Hebrew עור, Persian پوست, Hindi त्वचा, Indonesian *kulit*, Turkish *ten* and Vietnamese *da*. The [skin page](/tools/say/skin) has 41 languages, but check it for false matches.

### How do you say "hand" in different languages?

French *main*, Italian *mano*, Dutch *hand*, Ukrainian рука, Georgian ხელი, Persian دست, Hindi हाथ, Bengali হাত, Turkish *el* and Vietnamese *tay*. The [hand page](/tools/say/hand) has 51 languages.

### How do you say "eye" in different languages?

Spanish *ojos* (eyes), French *yeux*, German *Augen*, Hebrew עין, Persian چشم, Chinese 眼睛, Korean 눈, Turkish *göz* and Tagalog *mata*. The [eye page](/tools/say/eye) has all 53 languages.

### Why do some languages use one word for hand and arm?

Many languages name the whole limb rather than its parts. In this data, Armenian, Latvian, Lithuanian, Slovak and Slovenian have one word glossed "hand, arm", and Estonian, Finnish, Hebrew, Latvian and Malay have one for "foot, leg". Context tells you which part is meant.

### Which body word is the most common?

Among real matches, Persian دست (hand) ranks highest, at #50. Georgian თავს (head) at #83 is next, then Persian گوش (ear) and Vietnamese *đầu* (head), both at #100.
