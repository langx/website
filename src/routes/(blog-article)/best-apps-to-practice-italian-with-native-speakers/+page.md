## How to pick an app to practice Italian

**Expect standard Italian, with an accent.** Almost everyone you meet online writes standard Italian, but Italy is a country of strong regional identities. Many Italians grew up hearing a local language, such as Neapolitan, Sicilian or Venetian, alongside Italian, and their accent and a few words will tell you where they are from. You do not need to learn any of these; just ask your partner where they are from.

**Italian is not only spoken in Italy.** It is also official in Switzerland (mainly Ticino), San Marino and Vatican City. If you want Italian for a specific place, filter by country.

**Look for correction tools.** Italian mistakes are often a single letter: *caro* for *carro*, *e* (and) for *è* (is), a wrong ending on a verb in the past tense. A tool that marks up your exact sentence shows you that letter; a partner who retypes the whole line often hides it.

**Match the format to your level.** Text chat gives beginners time to work out verb endings. Once you can write a paragraph, voice messages and calls train the speed and melody that Italian conversation runs on.

**Be ready to give back.** Many Italians are learning English. Agree on how you split the time so the exchange stays fair.

## Tu or Lei in an Italian language exchange

- **Tu is normal between exchange partners,** especially online and among people of similar age. Most Italians on an app will use it from the first message.
- **Lei is the formal "you".** It uses the same verb form as "she", so *Lei è italiana?* can mean "Are you Italian?" or "Is she Italian?", and context decides. It is sometimes capitalized in formal writing.
- **Use Lei with older people, shopkeepers and officials,** and with a tutor until they suggest otherwise.
- **If in doubt, ask.** *Possiamo darci del tu?* ("Can we use tu with each other?") is the standard question.
- **Voi is the plural "you".** Writing to two people at once, use voi.

## Your first message in Italian

Keep it short, friendly and specific to their profile.

> Ciao Giulia! Mi chiamo Sam e sono canadese. Sto imparando l'italiano e ho visto che studi l'inglese. Ti va di fare uno scambio? Io ti aiuto con l'inglese e tu mi correggi l'italiano.

In English: "Hi Giulia! My name is Sam and I'm Canadian. I'm learning Italian and saw that you study English. Do you fancy doing an exchange? I help you with English and you correct my Italian." Notice *canadese* has no capital letter: Italian does not capitalize nationalities, languages, days or months. That is a small thing your partner will notice if you get it right.

## Tips for Italian learners on exchange apps

**Hear the double consonants.** *Pala* is a shovel and *palla* is a ball; *nono* means ninth and *nonno* grandfather. Send a voice message with a few of these pairs and ask your partner whether they can hear the difference in your speech. Spelling is easy in Italian, but these pairs are where learners sound foreign.

**Ask for corrections by name.** Tell your partner you want help with the past tense (*passato prossimo* vs *imperfetto*), with *essere* or *avere* as the helper verb (*sono andato*, not *ho andato*), and with the subjunctive once you start using *penso che*. Specific requests get specific corrections.

**Type the accents.** On most phone keyboards you hold the vowel to get *è*, *à* or *ù*. Leaving them out changes meaning (*e* and *è*, *la* and *là*), so it is worth the extra second.

**Learn every noun with its article.** *Il libro*, *lo zaino*, *l'amica*. Italian has several forms of "the", and they depend on the sound that follows, so copy the article along with the word.

## FAQ

### What is the best app to practice Italian with native speakers?

HelloTalk has the largest community and Tandem is strong for a steady one-to-one partner. LangX suits learners who want unlimited corrections and no ads. For a paid teacher, italki and Preply both have many Italian tutors.

### Can I practice Italian speaking for free?

Yes. HelloTalk and Tandem include free calls with partners, and Conversation Exchange helps you meet someone in person. LangX supports voice messages but not live calls.

### Should I use tu or Lei with a language exchange partner?

Tu is normal between exchange partners. Use Lei with someone much older or in a formal setting, and if you are unsure, ask "Possiamo darci del tu?"

### Do I need to learn an Italian dialect?

No. Standard Italian is understood everywhere in Italy. Local languages like Neapolitan or Sicilian are a fun extra later, best learned from a partner from that region.

### Is Italian easy if I already speak Spanish?

It is easier, because much of the vocabulary and grammar overlaps. The risk is mixing the two, so ask your partner to flag any Spanish words that slip into your Italian.
