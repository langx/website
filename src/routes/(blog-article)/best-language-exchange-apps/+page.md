The best language exchange apps in 2026 are **HelloTalk** for the biggest, busiest community, **Tandem** for focused one-to-one partner search, **Speaky** for a no-subscription start, **Lingbe** for instant voice calls, and **LangX** if you want an open-source app with no ads and unlimited corrections on the free plan. Which one is "best" depends on how you like to practice: text or voice, one partner or many, free or paid.

## How we chose

A language exchange app, for this list, is one where the main thing you do is **practice with another real person** who speaks the language you are learning. That rules out pure course apps and AI chatbots, though we include two apps (Busuu and italki) whose community features are good enough that people use them for exchange.

For each app we looked at:

- **Who you can find**: community size, how matching works, and whether it has partners for less common languages.
- **What the conversation offers**: corrections, translation, voice, calls and group rooms.
- **What is free**: whether a learner can practice seriously without paying, and what the paid tier adds. We do not quote prices, because they vary by country and change often.
- **Safety and moderation**: reporting, blocking, profile review.
- **Whether it is still alive**: recent updates in the app stores. One app from older lists, Bilingua, did not survive this check (see below).

If you are new to the idea itself, start with [what a language exchange is](/what-is-a-language-exchange) and come back.

## 1. HelloTalk

[HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com) is the largest language exchange app and the one most people have heard of. It mixes one-to-one chat with a social layer: **Moments**, a public feed where native speakers correct your posts; **Voicerooms**, live group voice chats on set topics; and **HelloTalk Live**, where hosts stream and you can join the stage.

- **Best for:** learners who want lots of people, lots of formats, and fast replies in popular languages.
- **Free vs paid:** HelloTalk says most of its features are free, including text and voice chat, Moments and Voicerooms. Translation, AI correction and voice-to-text have daily caps on the free tier. **VIP** lifts those caps, adds location and gender search, and removes ads.
- **Platforms:** iOS and Android, plus a web version you log in to by scanning a QR code with your phone.
- **Standout features:** in-chat correction and transliteration tools, Moments for quick feedback from several native speakers at once, and 260+ languages listed.
- **Drawbacks:** the social-network feel is not for everyone, and the free tier shows ads. Like every large open community, you will get some messages from people who are not there to learn languages, so the block button matters.

Compare it in detail: [LangX vs HelloTalk](/open-source-alternative-to-hellotalk).

## 2. Tandem

[Tandem](https://tandem.net) is the other big name, and it feels more focused than HelloTalk. New members are **individually reviewed**, and Tandem says there is occasionally a waitlist in some countries to keep languages balanced. Once in, you browse a community of profiles and filter by language, interests and more.

- **Best for:** people who want one or two steady partners and a cleaner, less social-media experience.
- **Free vs paid:** the free plan covers text chat, corrections, voice messages, calls, and up to an hour a day in Language Parties. **Tandem Pro** adds unlimited translations (Tandem's own overview lists three free a day), an AI toolkit for grammar checks and correction explanations, Nearby and Travel filters, more daily conversations, and no ads.
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android and Tandem Web.
- **Standout features:** over 300 languages including 12 sign languages, **Language Parties** (live audio rooms) and **Language Clubs** for topic groups.
- **Drawbacks:** the approval step can mean a wait before your first conversation, and several useful filters sit behind Pro.

Compare it in detail: [LangX vs Tandem](/open-source-alternative-to-tandem). Torn between the two giants? Read our [Tandem vs HelloTalk](/tandem-vs-hellotalk) head-to-head.

## 3. LangX

This is our app, so read this entry with that in mind. [LangX](https://get.langx.io) matches in **both directions**: you only see people who speak what you are learning *and* are learning what you speak, so every conversation has something in it for both sides.

- **Best for:** learners who want corrections at the center of the conversation, no ads, and an app whose code they can read.
- **Free vs paid:** the Free plan is a real plan, not a trial. Replies and corrections are **unlimited on every plan**, and you get 5 new conversations and 20 in-chat translations per rolling 24 hours. **Fluent** and **Polyglot** add unlimited new conversations, more translations (300 and 1,000 a day), more languages and extra filters. Details are on the [plans page](/pro).
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android and the web at app.langx.io.
- **Standout features:** tap any message to correct it, translation inside the chat, voice and photo messages, read-aloud, daily streaks, leaderboards and [LangX Tokens](/tokens), an in-app point you earn by talking and teaching (not money, and it cannot be bought or sold). The whole app is [open source under BSD-3](https://github.com/langx/langx) and can be self-hosted. No ads.
- **Drawbacks:** the community is **far smaller** than HelloTalk's or Tandem's, so for less common language pairs you may wait longer for a match. There are **no live voice or video calls** and no group audio rooms yet; practice happens through text and voice messages. AI feedback (LangX Copilot) is planned for Polyglot but not shipped.

## 4. Speaky

[Speaky](https://www.speaky.com) is one of the oldest language exchange apps and, at the time of writing, still one of the simplest ways to start for free. Its App Store listing now names **Altissia**, a language-learning company, as the seller, and the app has been updated regularly through 2026.

- **Best for:** learners who want to start chatting with native speakers today without a subscription.
- **Free vs paid:** free to use; there is no subscription listed on the App Store. The Google Play listing does flag ads and in-app purchases, so expect some on Android.
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android, and web.speaky.com.
- **Standout features:** 170+ languages, a 2026 update that added a **community map** and Nearby view, identity verification steps, AI practice partners for when nobody is online, and a vocabulary word game called Mojiku.
- **Drawbacks:** fewer learning tools inside the chat than HelloTalk or Tandem, and some store reviews, many of them from before the recent updates, mention bugs and connection problems. Quality varies a lot by language pair.

Compare it in detail: [LangX vs Speaky](/open-source-alternative-to-speaky).

## 5. Lingbe

[Lingbe](https://www.lingbe.com) skips profile browsing entirely. You press a call button and it connects you with an available native speaker for a live voice conversation. After the call you rate each other, and if you both liked it you can chat again later.

- **Best for:** speaking and listening practice when you have ten spare minutes and do not want to plan a call.
- **Free vs paid:** you start with free practice minutes and earn more credit ("lingos") by helping people learn your language. **Lingbe Pro** and lingo packs are sold in the app.
- **Platforms:** iOS and Android.
- **Standout features:** the one-tap call, feedback on grammar, pronunciation and fluency after each call, and the give-to-get credit system that keeps exchanges balanced.
- **Drawbacks:** random calls can feel abrupt if you are shy or a beginner. Updates have slowed: at the time of writing the Android app was last updated in September 2025, and the newest iOS version dates from 2024.

Compare it in detail: [LangX vs Lingbe](/open-source-alternative-to-lingbe).

## 6. ConversationExchange.com

[Conversation Exchange](https://www.conversationexchange.com) is a long-running website rather than an app, and it does something the apps mostly do not: it helps you find a partner **in your own city** for face-to-face conversation. It also supports pen-pal correspondence and online chat through its own ChitChat tool or whatever call app you already use.

- **Best for:** meeting a language partner in person, or old-fashioned email-style correspondence.
- **Free vs paid:** free to use, supported by ads. An optional **NOADS membership** removes ads and messaging limits.
- **Platforms:** web, including a mobile website. There is no official app.
- **Standout features:** the local meetup search, plus free resources like conversation topics and survival phrases.
- **Drawbacks:** the site looks dated, there are no built-in corrections or translation, and response rates depend heavily on your city.

Compare it in detail: [LangX vs ConversationExchange](/open-source-alternative-to-conversationexchange).

## 7. Busuu (community corrections)

[Busuu](https://www.busuu.com) is a course app first, but it has a genuinely useful exchange element: when you finish a writing or speaking exercise, it goes to the **Busuu community**, where native speakers correct it. You in turn correct exercises in your native language.

- **Best for:** beginners who want a structured course and native-speaker feedback without having to hold a free conversation yet.
- **Free vs paid:** community corrections are available on the free plan, but Busuu says free members can send a limited number of exercises and may wait longer. **Premium** unlocks the full course, unlimited submissions and priority in the correction queue.
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android and web.
- **Standout features:** corrections tied to lessons you just studied, and a clear course path.
- **Drawbacks:** it is not a real conversation. You get feedback on exercises, not a partner to chat with.

Compare it in detail: [LangX vs Busuu](/open-source-alternative-to-busuu).

## 8. italki (tutors and community)

[italki](https://www.italki.com) is best known as a marketplace for paid one-to-one lessons with professional teachers and community tutors. It also has a free **Community** area with posts, questions and written exercises that native speakers correct.

- **Best for:** learners ready to pay for structured speaking practice, or who want both a tutor and free written feedback.
- **Free vs paid:** the Community is free; lessons are paid per session, with prices set by each teacher.
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android and web.
- **Standout features:** a huge choice of tutors in many languages, including less common ones.
- **Drawbacks:** italki removed its free language-partner search a few years ago, so it is **no longer a true language exchange**. Many long-time users still search for "italki language partner" and are surprised it is gone.

Compare it in detail: [LangX vs italki](/open-source-alternative-to-italki).

## 9. InterPals

[InterPals](https://www.interpals.net) has connected pen pals since 1998. It is a social site first, but a large share of its members are there to practice languages, and it suits people who enjoy longer, slower messages over rapid chat.

- **Best for:** writing practice, cultural exchange and long-term pen-pal friendships.
- **Free vs paid:** InterPals says creating a profile, searching and messaging are free.
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android and web.
- **Standout features:** search by location, age, language and interests; a long-established community in many countries.
- **Drawbacks:** it is not built around learning, so there are no correction or translation tools, and you will need to say clearly that you want a language exchange.

## Apps that closed or changed

- **Bilingua**, which matched partners by interests and personality, is gone. Its Google Play page no longer exists (it was unpublished in 2023) and the iOS app is no longer available. If you used it, see [LangX vs Bilingua](/open-source-alternative-to-bilingua) for alternatives that work in a similar way.
- **italki** is still very much alive, but its free language-partner search is not, as noted above.
- **Tandem** ran a paid tutoring service, Tandem Tutors, which it closed in 2020 to focus on community exchange.

## How to pick the right language exchange app

Start from how you want to practice, not from the feature list.

- **You want the most people and the fastest replies:** HelloTalk. For popular pairs like English and Spanish, Japanese or Korean, a big community is the single biggest advantage an app can have.
- **You want one steady partner:** Tandem or LangX. Both are built around one-to-one conversations rather than feeds.
- **You want to speak out loud, today:** Lingbe for instant calls, or Tandem and HelloTalk for scheduled calls with someone you already chat with.
- **You are a beginner who freezes in conversation:** start with Busuu's corrected exercises, or text chat with voice messages in LangX, HelloTalk or Tandem, where you have time to think.
- **You want to pay nothing at all:** Speaky, Conversation Exchange and InterPals need no subscription. LangX's free plan has no ads and never limits replies or corrections, but it does cap new conversations at 5 a day.
- **You care about privacy and code you can check:** LangX is the only open-source app on this list.
- **You want someone to meet in person:** Conversation Exchange's local search, or the location filters in Tandem Pro, HelloTalk VIP, Speaky's map and LangX Polyglot's Nearby.

Many learners use two apps: a big one to find people, and a quieter one for the partners they actually stick with. Once you have picked, our guides on [how to find a language exchange partner](/how-to-find-a-language-exchange-partner) and [how to run a language exchange session](/how-to-do-a-language-exchange) will help you turn a first message into a habit. If you run out of things to say, keep a list of [conversation topics](/language-exchange-conversation-topics) handy.

## Safety tips for language exchange apps

Language exchange apps put you in touch with strangers, and every app on this list has some people who are there for other reasons. A few habits keep it pleasant:

1. **Keep the conversation in the app** until you trust someone. In-app chat means you can report and block, and your phone number stays private.
2. **Do not share personal details early**: home address, workplace, daily routine or financial information.
3. **Use block and report freely.** Tandem, HelloTalk, Speaky, LangX and the others all have them, and moderators rely on reports.
4. **Be wary of anyone who moves quickly** to romance, asks for money, or pushes you to another platform or an investment "opportunity". Those are classic scam patterns.
5. **Meet in public** if you meet in person, tell a friend where you are going, and leave if something feels off.
6. **Say what you want up front.** A profile line like "Here for language exchange only" filters out a lot of noise.

## FAQ

### What is the best free language exchange app?

It depends on what "free" means to you. Speaky, Conversation Exchange and InterPals need no subscription at all. HelloTalk and Tandem have generous free tiers with ads and daily caps on extras like translation. LangX's free plan has no ads and unlimited replies and corrections, with a limit of 5 new conversations a day.

### Is there a free language exchange app with no ads?

Yes. LangX has no ads on any plan, including Free. Most of the larger apps, including HelloTalk, Tandem and Speaky on Android, show ads on their free tiers and remove them with a paid plan.

### What is the best app to practice speaking a language with native speakers?

For live speaking, Lingbe is the quickest: one tap starts a voice call with a native speaker. HelloTalk (Voicerooms and calls) and Tandem (Language Parties and calls) are better if you want to build a relationship with a regular partner first. If you prefer voice messages to live calls, LangX, HelloTalk and Tandem all support them.

### Is HelloTalk or Tandem better?

HelloTalk is better if you want the biggest community and a social feed; Tandem is better if you want a more focused, reviewed community and one-to-one exchange. Our [Tandem vs HelloTalk comparison](/tandem-vs-hellotalk) goes through it feature by feature.

### Are language exchange apps safe?

They are about as safe as any social app: generally fine if you keep chats inside the app, share little personal information early, and block and report anyone who makes you uncomfortable. Apps with profile review, like Tandem, or identity verification, like Speaky, add an extra layer.

### Do language exchange apps actually work?

They work best as practice alongside other study, not as a complete course. Conversation with native speakers builds listening, confidence and natural phrasing that apps and textbooks struggle to teach. Pair it with vocabulary work, for example the [most common words](/tools/most-common-words) in your target language, and see our list of [Duolingo alternatives for speaking](/duolingo-alternatives-for-speaking) for more ways to get talking.
