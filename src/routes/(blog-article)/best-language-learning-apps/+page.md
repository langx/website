There is no single best language learning app, because apps do different jobs. The best picks in 2026, by need: **Duolingo** for a free daily habit, **Babbel** or **Busuu** for a structured course, **Pimsleur** for speaking out loud from audio, **Anki** or **Memrise** for vocabulary, **italki** or **Preply** for a tutor, **Speak** or **Praktika** for AI speaking practice, **LangX**, **HelloTalk** or **Tandem** for conversation with native speakers, and **Dreaming** for listening.

Most learners do best with two apps: one that teaches and one that makes you use what you learned. This guide tells you which to pair.

## Start from the job, not the app

Before you download anything, name the skill that is holding you back. Almost every learner is stuck on one of these:

- **You never start, or never keep going.** You need a habit, which is what Duolingo sells best.
- **You do not understand how the language works.** You need explanations: Babbel or Busuu.
- **You understand but cannot say anything.** You need production: Pimsleur, an AI speaking app, a tutor, or an exchange partner.
- **You keep forgetting words.** You need spaced repetition: Anki or Memrise. Our guide on [how many words you need](/how-many-words-do-you-need-to-be-fluent) explains why vocabulary is the bottleneck for so many people.
- **People talk too fast.** You need hours of listening: comprehensible input such as Dreaming.

One app rarely fixes two of these at once. That is not a flaw in the apps; it is why they are different products.

## For a structured course: Duolingo, Babbel and Busuu

### Duolingo

[Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com) teaches 40+ languages through short, game-like lessons, and every course is free. Streaks, leagues and reminders make it the best app on this list at getting you to open it every day. In April 2026 Duolingo said nine of its most popular courses (English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Chinese) now teach through **B2** on the CEFR scale, and made that advanced content free.

The free version shows ads and, on mobile, limits how much you can do in a row through a system Duolingo calls Energy. **Super** removes ads and the limit; **Max** adds AI speaking features, Video Call with Lily and Roleplay.

### Babbel

[Babbel](https://www.babbel.com) teaches 14 languages through lessons built around realistic dialogues, with short grammar explanations and a review tool. If Duolingo left you able to pass exercises without knowing why, Babbel is the natural next step. It has added **Babbel Speak**, AI conversation practice for some languages. The first lesson of every course is free when you sign up with an email; the full course needs a subscription. See [LangX vs Babbel](/open-source-alternative-to-babbel).

### Busuu

[Busuu](https://www.busuu.com), owned by Chegg, teaches 14 languages and adds something the other course apps lack: when you finish a writing or speaking exercise, **native speakers in the Busuu community correct it**. Busuu's help center says you can study for free by watching a short ad before each lesson; Premium removes ads and unlocks the rest, including unlimited submissions for correction. See [LangX vs Busuu](/open-source-alternative-to-busuu).

**Which course?** Duolingo if you need the habit and want to pay nothing, Babbel if you want the clearest explanations, Busuu if you want human feedback built into the course.

## For speaking out loud on your commute: Pimsleur

[Pimsleur](https://www.pimsleur.com) is an audio course: 30-minute lessons in which a recorded voice prompts you, you answer out loud, and then you hear the right answer. It covers 51 languages, needs no screen, and works while you walk or drive. You can try the first lesson free on its website, and the app has a 7-day free trial.

## For vocabulary that sticks: Anki and Memrise

[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net) is a flashcard app that uses spaced repetition: it shows each card just before you would forget it. It is plain and takes a little setup, but nothing else is as reliable for keeping thousands of words. The desktop app, the Android app (AnkiDroid) and the AnkiWeb sync service are free; the iPhone app is a paid purchase that funds development. Anki is **open source**, and in February 2026 its creator, Damien Elmes, handed stewardship to the team behind AnkiHub, who committed to keeping it open source.

[Memrise](https://www.memrise.com) is the friendlier option: vocabulary lessons with short **videos of native speakers**, plus AI chat partners. Its App Store listing says the free plan has limited lessons, videos and conversations, with ads; Memrise Pro unlocks everything. See [LangX vs Memrise](/open-source-alternative-to-memrise).

## For a teacher: italki and Preply

If you want someone whose job is to make you talk, and who will plan around your mistakes, pay a tutor. It is the fastest route for an exam, a job interview or a move abroad.

- **[italki](https://www.italki.com)** is a marketplace of professional teachers and less formal community tutors in a very wide range of languages. You pay per lesson with no subscription, and teachers set their own rates. There is also a free Community area for written questions and corrections. See [LangX vs italki](/open-source-alternative-to-italki).
- **[Preply](https://preply.com)** is also a marketplace, but you book a trial lesson and then subscribe to a tutor, with lessons renewed every 28 days. That structure suits people who want a fixed weekly slot. See [LangX vs Preply](/open-source-alternative-to-preply).

## For speaking without a person: Speak and Praktika

AI speaking apps let you talk out loud at any hour, repeat a sentence twenty times, and get instant feedback without anyone watching. For nervous beginners that is valuable.

- **[Speak](https://www.speak.com)** combines a structured course with an AI tutor you talk to in lessons and roleplays. At the time of writing its App Store listing offers Spanish, Korean, Japanese, Chinese, English, French and Italian. It has a free trial, then a subscription. See [LangX vs Speak](/open-source-alternative-to-speak).
- **[Praktika](https://praktika.ai)** puts you face to face with animated AI avatar tutors with different personalities and accents, in nine languages. It also has a free trial and then a subscription. See [LangX vs Praktika](/open-source-alternative-to-praktika).

Both are rehearsal spaces. An AI will not tell you what people actually say in Madrid or Seoul, and it will not become a friend you want to keep talking to. We weigh the trade-off in [AI conversation partner vs language exchange](/ai-conversation-partner-vs-language-exchange).

## For real conversation: LangX, HelloTalk and Tandem

A language exchange pairs you with someone who speaks the language you are learning and is learning yours. You help each other, so it costs nothing but time, and it is the one category on this list where you talk with real native speakers without paying. If the idea is new, read [what a language exchange is](/what-is-a-language-exchange).

- **[HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com)** has the largest community, a public feed called Moments where natives correct your posts, and live Voicerooms. Chat and calls are free; translation, AI correction and voice-to-text have daily caps unless you pay for VIP.
- **[Tandem](https://tandem.net)** is more focused on one-to-one exchange, with reviewed profiles and audio and video calls. Its free plan allows 10 new conversations and 3 translations a day; Tandem Pro raises both and removes ads.
- **[LangX](https://get.langx.io)** is ours. Matching runs both ways: you only see people who speak what you learn and learn what you speak. **Hold any message to correct it**, and corrections are unlimited on every plan, as are replies. Translation is built into the chat (20 a day on Free), with voice and photo messages, read-aloud and daily streaks. Echo, its spaced-repetition tab, ships eighteen free packs of phrases in six languages and turns any corrected message into a card. No ads, and the whole app is [open source (BSD-3)](https://github.com/langx/langx). The honest limits: the community is much smaller than HelloTalk's or Tandem's, the Free plan starts 5 new conversations a day, and there are **no live calls**, only voice, photo and video messages. Paid plans are on the [plans page](/pro).

## For listening: Dreaming

Comprehensible input means watching or listening to lots of material you mostly understand. [Dreaming](https://www.dreaming.com), which grew out of Dreaming Spanish and now also covers French, is the best-known app for it: videos graded by level, spoken entirely in the target language, with no exercises. Part of the library is free, including on YouTube; a premium plan unlocks the rest.

Dreaming suggests building a lot of listening before you speak. Not everyone wants to wait, but heavy listening helps every conversation, because most of what stops you in a real chat is not catching what the other person just said.

## How to combine two apps

The most common mistake is stacking three course apps that all teach the same way. Pair apps that do **different** jobs instead:

Good pairings, depending on your goal:

- **Complete beginner, no budget:** Duolingo plus a free exchange app once you can write short sentences.
- **Traveling in three months:** Pimsleur plus an exchange partner or a few tutor lessons.
- **Grammar-minded, steady learner:** Babbel plus Anki.
- **Exam or job deadline:** a tutor on italki or Preply plus Busuu or Babbel.
- **Shy about speaking:** Speak or Praktika first, then text chats with voice messages on an exchange app. Our [introvert's guide](/mastering-a-new-language-an-introverts-guide) is written for this.

## FAQ

### What is the best language learning app overall?

There is no single winner, because apps do different jobs. For most beginners, Duolingo is the best free starting point for building a habit. For real progress in speaking, add an app with people in it: an exchange app like LangX, HelloTalk or Tandem, or a tutor on italki or Preply.

### What is the best app to learn a language for free?

Duolingo's courses are free, Anki is free on desktop, Android and web, and language exchange apps like LangX, HelloTalk and Tandem have free plans for talking with native speakers. Our guide to [free language learning apps](/free-language-learning-apps) lists exactly what each free tier includes.

### Is Babbel better than Duolingo?

Babbel explains grammar more clearly and builds lessons around realistic dialogues; Duolingo is better at keeping you coming back and is free. Many learners start on Duolingo and move to Babbel when they want to understand why a sentence works.

### Which language learning app is best for speaking?

For live practice with people, an exchange app (free) or a tutor (paid). For solo practice, Pimsleur has you answer out loud from the first lesson, and Speak and Praktika let you hold conversations with an AI.

### How many language apps should I use at once?

Two is the sweet spot for most people: one that teaches and one that makes you speak. More than that usually means more switching and less practice.
