On its own, for most people, no: Duolingo can take you further than it used to, but not to conversational fluency. Since April 2026, Duolingo's nine most popular courses teach content through **B2**, the CEFR's "independent user" level, yet fluency means holding unscripted conversations with people, and a course cannot give you those. Use Duolingo for the foundation and the habit, and add regular conversation with real speakers.

That answer is fairer to Duolingo than the usual "Duolingo doesn't work" take, and less generous than its marketing. Here is what the course covers, what the research says, and what to add.

## What does "fluent" actually mean?

People use "fluent" to mean anything from "can order dinner" to "passes for a native". A more useful yardstick is the **CEFR** (Common European Framework of Reference for Languages), which has six levels from A1 to C2. The Council of Europe describes a **B2** speaker as someone who can "interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible without strain for either party."

That is what most people mean by fluent: you can talk to a native speaker without either of you struggling. Note the words *interact* and *spontaneity*. B2 is not about how many words you recognize; it is about what you can do in a live exchange.

For how long reaching that level usually takes, see [how long it takes to learn a language](/how-long-does-it-take-to-learn-a-language), and for the vocabulary side, [how many words you need to be fluent](/how-many-words-do-you-need-to-be-fluent).

## How far do Duolingo's courses go?

Duolingo measures progress with the **Duolingo Score**, out of 160, which it maps to the CEFR: roughly 30 to 59 is A2, 60 to 99 is B1, 100 to 129 is B2, and 130 and above is C1 and C2.

In April 2026, Duolingo announced that nine of its most popular courses, **English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Chinese**, now teach through B2, a Duolingo Score of 129, and made that advanced content free. Before that, Duolingo says, most learners in those courses could only reach A2 (a score of 59). The new material includes advanced Stories for reading and DuoRadio for listening.

Two caveats. First, the B2 expansion covers those nine courses; Duolingo teaches 40+ languages, and the others were not part of the announcement. Second, no Duolingo course teaches C1 or C2 content at the time of writing (September 2026).

So a learner who finishes one of the big nine has been **taught** B2 content. Whether they can **do** B2, meaning interact with a native speaker without strain, is the real question.

## What the research says

The most cited study is Jiang and colleagues (2021), published in *Foreign Language Annals* by Duolingo researchers with Luke Plonsky of Northern Arizona University. It tested 225 adults in the US who had completed the beginning-level Spanish or French course and used only Duolingo.

The learners reached **Intermediate Low in reading** and **Novice High in listening** on the ACTFL scale, comparable to US university students at the end of their fourth semester. The abstract is explicit: "No other skills were assessed." Speaking was not part of it.

That is a genuinely good result for a free app, and it matches what many learners find: Duolingo builds real reading and listening. It also shows where the evidence is thinnest. Duolingo has published later reports on speaking, but its best-known peer-reviewed evidence is about understanding, not conversation.

## What Duolingo does well

- **It gets you to show up.** Streaks, leagues and five-minute lessons make daily practice feel non-negotiable, and consistency is the hardest part of learning a language. See [why consistent practice is key](/why-consistent-practice-is-key-to-language-learning-success).
- **It gives you a real foundation.** Vocabulary, grammar patterns, reading and listening, in small steps.
- **It is free.** Every course, including the new B2 content, at the time of writing, with ads and a daily Energy limit on mobile.
- **It has started on speaking.** Duolingo Max includes Video Call with Lily, an AI character, and Roleplay scenarios with AI feedback.

## What a course cannot give you

The gap is not effort or course quality. It is structural:

- **Recognition is not production.** Choosing the right tiles, completing a sentence or translating one someone else wrote are different skills from building a sentence from nothing in two seconds while someone waits.
- **A course is designed so you are ready for the next step. A person is not.** Real conversations include speed, slang, accents, jokes and topics you never studied. Handling that is most of what fluency is.
- **AI practice helps, but it is not a person.** Video Call and Roleplay are good rehearsal: patient, always available, no embarrassment. An AI will not tell you what people actually say in Mexico City or Osaka, and it will not become a friend you want to keep talking to, which is what keeps people practicing for years. We compare the two in [AI conversation partner vs language exchange](/ai-conversation-partner-vs-language-exchange).

Duolingo is not hiding this. It is a course app, and courses teach. Conversation is a different activity, and you need people for it.

## When should you start talking to people?

Earlier than feels comfortable. Many learners plan to finish the course first and then start speaking, and a finished B2 course makes that plan more tempting than ever. The trouble is that speaking is a skill of its own, and it improves only when you do it. A learner who has chatted twice a week since A2 will usually handle a real conversation far better than one who finished the whole course in silence.

Signs you are ready: you can write a few simple sentences about yourself, ask basic questions, and read a short reply with a little help. Text chat is the gentlest start, because you get time to think; voice messages come next. If nerves are the problem, our [introvert's guide to learning a language](/mastering-a-new-language-an-introverts-guide) is written for you.

You do not need to quit Duolingo. Add a place to use it. The two main options:

- **A language exchange partner (free).** Someone who speaks the language you are learning and is learning yours. You chat, they correct you, you correct them. ## So, can you become fluent with Duolingo?

You can become fluent **with** Duolingo, if it is one part of your routine. You are unlikely to become fluent **from** Duolingo alone, because fluency is built in conversation. The good news is that Duolingo's courses now take you further than ever before you need anything else, and the thing you need to add, a conversation partner, can be free.

## FAQ

### What level can you reach with Duolingo?

In its nine most popular courses (English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Chinese), Duolingo now teaches through B2 on the CEFR scale, a Duolingo Score of 129. Other courses are shorter. No course teaches C1 or C2 content at the time of writing.

### Is Duolingo enough to become fluent?

For most people, no. It builds a strong foundation in vocabulary, grammar, reading and listening, and a daily habit. Fluency also needs regular, unscripted conversation with people, which a course cannot provide.

### How long does it take to become fluent with Duolingo?

Duolingo does not promise a time to fluency, and it depends on the language and how much you practice. Languages close to English take far less time than Arabic, Chinese, Japanese or Korean; our guide to [how long it takes to learn a language](/how-long-does-it-take-to-learn-a-language) has the Foreign Service Institute's figures.

### Does Duolingo teach speaking?

It includes speaking exercises, and Duolingo Max adds AI Video Call and Roleplay. The best-known peer-reviewed study of Duolingo tested reading and listening only. For live speaking, add a conversation partner or a tutor.

### What should I use with Duolingo to become fluent?

A language exchange app for free, regular conversation with native speakers, a tutor if you have a deadline, and flashcards such as Anki for vocabulary. See [the best language learning apps](/best-language-learning-apps) for how to combine them.
