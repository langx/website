## What happened to Bilingua?

Bilingua was a language exchange app made by Creatella, a Singapore-based company, and first appeared on Google Play in March 2017. At the time of writing (September 2026):

- According to the app tracker AppBrain, the Android app was **removed from Google Play on June 4, 2023**. Its last recorded update was in October 2018.
- The iOS app's App Store listing **no longer loads**.
- The bilingua.io domain now shows a **"domain for sale"** page.

We have not found an official shutdown announcement, so we cannot give you a closing date beyond the Google Play removal. But with no app in either store, no updates since 2018 and the website gone, Bilingua is effectively closed.

<Callout type="info">
If you still have an old copy of Bilingua installed, do not rely on it. An app with no updates since 2018 is unlikely to be maintained, and your matches are almost certainly inactive. Move your practice somewhere that is still being developed.
</Callout>

## What Bilingua did well

Bilingua had a thoughtful idea at its core, and it is worth saying what people liked about it:

- **Matching on personality and interests.** Instead of dropping you into a long list of profiles, Bilingua tried to pair you with people you would actually enjoy talking to. That is a real problem in language exchange: many conversations die after "hi, where are you from?"
- **A conversation helper.** Its built-in bot, Shiro, suggested topics and questions based on your shared interests, and could suggest translations and corrections. That took some of the awkwardness out of the first few messages.
- **Light learning tools.** A vocabulary list, a news feed, progress stats, games and quizzes made it feel more like a learning app than a chat app.
- **Free to use.** Bilingua positioned itself as fun, free and effective, with languages like English, Chinese, Spanish, French and Japanese.

## Where LangX fits for former Bilingua users

LangX is not a copy of Bilingua, and it does not have a chatbot. What it does have is a focus on making each conversation teach you something.

### Every match is a real exchange

On LangX you only see people who speak the language you are learning *and* are learning a language you speak. That means the person you write to has a reason to write back. Bilingua's interest matching tackled the same problem from a different angle: both try to avoid dead-end conversations.

### Corrections from real people, unlimited

Instead of an assistant suggesting fixes, on LangX your partner holds any message and corrects it. Corrections are unlimited on every plan, including Free, because they are the part of an exchange that actually teaches.

### Translation inside the chat

If you leaned on Shiro's translations, LangX has translation built into the chat, so you can check a word without leaving the conversation. The Free plan includes 20 translations per rolling 24 hours; Fluent has 300 and Polyglot 1,000.

### Voice messages and read-aloud

Bilingua was text-first. On LangX you can send voice messages to practice pronunciation, send photos to show what you mean, and have any message read aloud.

### Streaks, tokens and leaderboards

Bilingua's games kept people coming back. LangX uses daily streaks, [LangX Tokens](/tokens) (an in-app point for talking and correcting, not money) and leaderboards to do a similar job.

### Open source, so it cannot quietly vanish

This is the biggest lesson from Bilingua. When a closed app shuts down, it is gone. LangX's app and API are open source under BSD-3 on [GitHub](https://github.com/langx/langx), and anyone can host their own instance. That does not guarantee LangX will last forever, but the code cannot disappear with a company. For people looking for an open source alternative to Bilingua, this is the point.

### Honest limits

- **No live calls** on LangX, only voice messages.
- **No conversation bot.** LangX Copilot, private AI feedback on your own messages, is coming later for Polyglot, but it is not available yet.
- **A smaller community** than the biggest exchange apps, so some language pairs have fewer people.
- **The free plan caps new conversations** at 5 per rolling 24 hours. Replies and corrections are never capped. The [plans page](/plans) has the details.

## Who should pick which?

Since Bilingua is no longer available, the real question is which Bilingua alternative suits you:

- **You liked Bilingua's calm, one-to-one chats and want corrections and translation built in:** try LangX. It is a free Bilingua alternative with no ads.
- **You want live voice or video calls:** Tandem or Lingbe will suit you better.
- **You liked the social, feed-style side:** HelloTalk has a public feed alongside chat.
- **You want structured lessons plus some native-speaker feedback:** a course app like Busuu fits better than any exchange app.

## FAQ

### Is Bilingua still available?

No, not as far as we can tell. At the time of writing (September 2026) Bilingua is not listed on Google Play or the App Store, and its website domain is for sale. AppBrain records its removal from Google Play on June 4, 2023, and its last update was in October 2018.

### What is the best open source alternative to Bilingua?

LangX is an open source alternative to Bilingua. Its app and API are BSD-3 licensed on GitHub, and it offers two-way matching, unlimited corrections and in-chat translation on iOS, Android and the web.

### Is there a free Bilingua alternative?

Yes. LangX has a free plan with unlimited replies and corrections, 5 new conversations and 20 translations per rolling 24 hours, and no ads. Tandem, HelloTalk and Speaky also have free tiers.

### LangX vs Bilingua: does LangX have a chatbot like Shiro?

No. LangX focuses on corrections and translation inside real conversations. LangX Copilot, private AI feedback, is planned for the Polyglot plan but is not available yet.

### What are the best apps like Bilingua for voice practice?

For live calls, Lingbe and Tandem. For voice messages you can send and replay at your own pace, LangX and HelloTalk.
