The best language exchange apps for beginners are the ones that let you start in writing, with help on hand: **HelloTalk** for built-in translation, romanization and voice-to-text, **LangX** for calm text-first chats with unlimited corrections and no ads, and **Busuu** if you want a course that sends your exercises to native speakers before you ever hold a conversation. **Tandem** is great once you are ready for a steady partner, and AI apps like **Speak** or **Praktika** are a safe place to rehearse first.

You do not need to be "good enough" to start a language exchange. You need about fifty useful words, a short introduction you have written in advance, and an app that gives you time to think. This guide shows which apps do that best, then gives you a two-week plan and first messages you can copy.

## Can a complete beginner do a language exchange?

Yes. The Council of Europe's CEFR describes an **A1** learner as someone who can introduce themselves, ask and answer simple questions about personal details, and interact simply if the other person talks slowly and is ready to help. That is exactly what a first exchange looks like.

What worries most beginners is not grammar. It is:

- **"I won't understand the reply."** Translation and read-aloud inside the chat solve most of this.
- **"I'll freeze if they call me."** Then do not take calls yet. Text and voice messages give you all the time you need.
- **"I'm wasting their time."** You are not. Half of every exchange is in your language, where you are the expert.
- **"They'll judge my mistakes."** Your partner is a learner too, and is making mistakes in your language at the same moment.

## What makes a language exchange app beginner-friendly?

Look for help **inside the chat**: translation, transliteration (romanization) for languages in another script, voice-to-text for voice messages, read-aloud for written ones, and a correction tool so a partner can fix your sentence in place. Then look for a **gentle format** (chat, posts or letters rather than a live call on day one) and, ideally, somewhere to rehearse first, whether an AI partner or a course.

## The best apps for beginners, and why

### HelloTalk: the most help built into the chat

[HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com) has the fullest beginner toolkit of any exchange app. Translation, transliteration and voice-to-text all sit inside the chat, and **Moments** lets you post one short sentence and get it corrected by several native speakers, a low-stakes way to start before you commit to a conversation.

The catch: on the free tier each of those tools has a **daily cap**, and there are ads. VIP lifts both. The busy, social-feed atmosphere can also feel like a lot in week one. More in [LangX vs HelloTalk](/open-source-alternative-to-hellotalk) and [is HelloTalk free?](/is-hellotalk-free).

Our app. [LangX](https://get.langx.io) matches in both directions, so every person you see speaks the language you are learning *and* is learning yours. ### Busuu: a course first, native speakers second

[Busuu](https://www.busuu.com) is not a pure exchange app, which is why it suits absolute beginners. You work through lessons, and your written and spoken exercises go to the **Busuu community**, where native speakers correct them. Free members can send only a limited number of exercises; Premium removes the limit. See [LangX vs Busuu](/open-source-alternative-to-busuu).

### Tandem: when you are ready for one steady partner

[Tandem](https://tandem.net) reviews new members and centers on one-to-one chats, which many beginners find calmer than a feed. The free plan includes chat, corrections, voice messages and calls, but only **3 translations a day**, and that is the limit beginners hit first. Tandem Pro adds unlimited translation, audio transcription and an AI toolkit that can check your grammar and explain a partner's corrections. See [LangX vs Tandem](/open-source-alternative-to-tandem).

### Speaky: free, with AI partners when nobody is online

[Speaky](https://www.speaky.com) needs no subscription and, since a 2026 update, offers AI practice partners for when no human is around. Its store listings do not describe translation or correction tools in the chat, so keep a dictionary open. See [LangX vs Speaky](/open-source-alternative-to-speaky).

### Speak and Praktika: rehearse with AI first

[Speak](https://www.speak.com) and [Praktika](https://praktika.ai) are not exchange apps: you talk to an AI tutor that gives instant feedback. For a beginner who freezes, a week of rehearsing an introduction with AI can make the first real conversation far easier. Our comparison of [AI partners and language exchange](/ai-conversation-partner-vs-language-exchange) explains when to switch.

## First-message scripts for A1 learners

Keep it to four or five short sentences: hello, name, where you are from, that you are a beginner, and one question.

**Spanish:**

> ¡Hola! Me llamo Sam. Soy de Canadá. Estoy aprendiendo español. Soy principiante. ¿De dónde eres?

**French:**

> Bonjour ! Je m'appelle Sam. Je viens du Canada. J'apprends le français. Je suis débutant. Tu habites où ?

(Use *débutante* if you are a woman.)

**German:**

> Hallo! Ich heiße Sam. Ich komme aus Kanada. Ich lerne Deutsch. Ich bin Anfänger. Woher kommst du?

(Use *Anfängerin* if you are a woman.)

Then add a line in **your own language**, the one your partner is learning: "I can help with your English. Please correct my mistakes, and write short sentences if you can." It tells them what you offer and what you need.

Three beginner lifelines worth learning in your target language, in whatever form your partner uses:

- "Sorry, I don't understand."
- "Can you write that more simply?"
- "How do you say this?"

## Beginner mistakes to avoid

- **Opening with just "hi".** Ask about something in their profile instead; more in [how to find a language exchange partner](/how-to-find-a-language-exchange-partner).
- **Translating every message.** Save translation for whole sentences and look up single words in a dictionary. You will remember more and stay under free caps.
- **Chatting only in your own language.** Agree on a split, even if your half is a few simple sentences.
- **Taking a live call too early.** Voice messages build many of the same skills without the pressure.
- **Sharing personal details quickly.** Keep chats in the app until you trust someone, and block anyone who makes you uncomfortable.

## FAQ

### What is the best language exchange app for beginners?

HelloTalk has the most beginner tools inside the chat (translation, romanization, voice-to-text), with daily caps on the free tier. LangX is a calm, text-first option with unlimited corrections and no ads. Busuu suits absolute beginners who want a course with native-speaker feedback before real conversations.

### How many words do I need before my first language exchange?

Around 50–100 of the most common words and a prepared introduction are enough to start in writing. You will learn the rest from your partner. Our post on [how many words you need](/how-many-words-do-you-need-to-be-fluent) goes further.

### Should beginners practice with AI before real people?

It helps if you freeze in conversation. Rehearse with an AI app such as Speak or Praktika for a week or two, then use what you rehearsed in a real exchange. Human partners give you the culture, slang and real reactions AI cannot.

### Is it rude to use translation in a language exchange?

No. Partners expect beginners to use it. Use it to understand, then try to reply in your target language yourself, even with mistakes. That is what your partner is there to correct.

### Which language exchange apps are free for beginners?

HelloTalk, Tandem, Speaky and LangX all have free versions. The differences are in the caps: see our breakdown of [free language exchange apps](/free-language-exchange-apps).
