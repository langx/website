Mother is *madre* in Spanish, *μητέρα* in Greek, *어머니* in Korean and *माँ* in Hindi. Aunt is *tante* in French and German and *zia* in Italian, but in Turkish the most common word is *hala*, which means a father's sister. That is the fun part of family words. Many languages have no single word for "aunt", "uncle" or "grandmother". They have one for each side of the family.

Below are 15 family words in a selection of languages with different scripts. Each one links to its full page on our free [Say it in any language](/tools/say) tool.

## Where the words come from

Each [/tools/say](/tools/say) page lists an English word in up to 53 languages. The words come from how often they appear in film and TV subtitles (the OpenSubtitles 2018 frequency lists), matched to English through Wiktionary. So every word is one people actually say, and the page shows where it ranks in that language.

Two things to keep in mind:

- The lists are lowercase because they come from word counts. German capitalizes nouns, so we write *Mutter*, *Vater* and *Tante* here the way you'd see them in a sentence.
- When a language splits a word by side of the family, age or gender, the full page shows the English meaning next to the word. That's where every maternal and paternal note in this post comes from.

## Why Vietnamese family words rank so high

In Vietnamese, family words also work as pronouns. You call an older woman *bà* ("grandmother"), and she may call herself *bà* too. The data shows this directly. The English meanings for these words start with "I/me" or "you":

| Vietnamese | Meaning shown | Rank |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cô | I/me, your paternal aunt | #17 |
| ông | you, my grandfather | #29 |
| cậu | I/me, your mother's brother | #34 |
| mẹ | I/me, your mother | #90 |
| bà | you, my grandmother | #110 |
| bố | I/me, your father | #116 |
| chị | I/me, your big sister | #197 |

Getting these right is a big part of sounding natural in Vietnamese. The [Vietnamese word list](/tools/most-common-words/vietnamese) shows how many of them are in the top few hundred.

## How to learn family words so they stick

A list like this is a start. Family words stick when you use them about real people.

- **Draw your own family tree** and label it in your target language, including the maternal and paternal words if your language has them.
- **Describe your family to someone.** "My mother's sister lives in…" makes you choose the right word. Family is one of the easiest [language exchange conversation topics](/language-exchange-conversation-topics) because everyone has something to say.
- **Learn the script, not only the sound.** If your language uses one, the [alphabet pages](/tools/alphabet) show every letter.

## FAQ

### How do you say "aunt" in different languages?

French and German *tante*, Italian *zia*, Portuguese *tia*, Hebrew דודה and Indonesian *bibi*. Chinese 阿姨, Korean 이모 and Persian خاله are maternal aunts, and Turkish *hala* and Tamil அத்தை are paternal aunts. The [aunt page](/tools/say/aunt) has 37 languages.

### Which languages have different words for maternal and paternal relatives?

In our data, the aunt, uncle and grandparent pages show a split for Chinese, Korean, Persian, Turkish, Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Albanian, Swedish and several Slavic languages. Plenty of other languages make the distinction too.

### What is the word for "husband" in Chinese?

丈夫, which ranks #138 in Chinese. Wife is 妻子 (#125).

### Why do some family words have strange meanings on the full pages?

The words come from film and TV subtitles, matched to English through Wiktionary. Most matches are exact. A few pick up a pronoun, a nickname or an insult that contains the family word, and the full page always shows the English meaning next to it so you can tell.
