## What makes a language app "social"?

Plenty of apps have social *features*: friends lists, leaderboards, shared streaks. That is not the same as a social language learning app. For this list, an app counts as social if real people help you learn the language itself, in at least one of four ways:

1. **Language exchange.** You are paired with a native speaker of the language you are learning who wants to learn yours, and you chat. See [what a language exchange is](/what-is-a-language-exchange).
2. **Community corrections.** You write or record something, and native speakers correct it.
3. **Pen pals.** Longer, slower letters with people abroad, often in their language and yours.
4. **Group voice.** Live audio rooms where you listen and speak with several people at once.

By that test, Duolingo is a social app for motivation but not for conversation, which is why it gets its own section below rather than a place in the ranking.

## 1. HelloTalk: the biggest social language app

[HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com) is the app most people picture when they hear "social language app", because it looks like one. Alongside one-to-one chat with native speakers, it has **Moments**, a public feed where natives correct your posts, **Voicerooms** for live group voice chat, and **HelloTalk Live** streams.

- **Why it is social:** every format at once, exchange, community corrections and group voice.
- **Good for:** lots of people and fast replies in popular languages.
- **Watch out for:** it feels like social media, with the noise that comes with it, and the free tier shows ads and caps extras like translation. VIP lifts those.

## 2. Tandem: focused one-to-one exchange

[Tandem](https://tandem.net) reviews new members before they join and is built around finding one or two steady partners. You get text chat with corrections, voice messages, audio and video calls, **Language Parties** (live audio rooms) and topic-based **Language Clubs**. It lists over 300 languages, including sign languages.

- **Why it is social:** real partners, calls, and group rooms.
- **Good for:** people who want a calmer, less feed-like experience and in-app calls.
- **Watch out for:** a possible wait while your profile is reviewed, and several filters and translations sit behind Tandem Pro.

## 3. LangX: a social language app built around corrections

[LangX](https://get.langx.io) is our app. It matches in **both directions**: you only see people who speak the language you are learning and are learning one you speak, so every conversation is useful to both of you. Hold any message to correct it, or post a sentence to the Feed for the community to correct or ask for help with pronunciation. Corrections and replies are **unlimited on every plan**.

- **Why it is social:** every "lesson" is a person. Around the chat there is translation, voice, photo and video messages, read-aloud, daily streaks, leaderboards (week, month, year and all time) and the [LangX Token](/tokens), an in-app point you earn by talking and correcting. It is not money and cannot be bought or sold.
- **Good for:** learners who want feedback at the center of the conversation, no ads, and code they can read: the app is [open source under BSD-3](https://github.com/langx/langx).
- **Watch out for:** the community is much smaller than HelloTalk's or Tandem's, so rarer language pairs can take longer. There are no live calls or group voice rooms; practice is by text and voice messages. Free lets you start 5 new conversations and use 20 translations a day; [Fluent and Polyglot](/pro) raise those.

## 4. Speaky: open, free exchange

[Speaky](https://www.speaky.com) is one of the oldest exchange apps and still one of the simplest. You search the community and message people directly, with no approval step. At the time of writing its App Store listing names Altissia as the seller and describes a community map for finding partners nearby or worldwide, AI practice partners for when no one is online, and identity verification. It lists 170+ languages and also runs on the web.

- **Why it is social:** direct contact with native speakers, fast.
- **Good for:** starting today, for free; the stores list no subscription.
- **Watch out for:** fewer learning tools inside the chat, and Google Play marks the Android app as containing ads.

Compare: [LangX vs Speaky](/open-source-alternative-to-speaky).

## 5. Busuu: community corrections on a course

[Busuu](https://www.busuu.com) is a course app first, with CEFR-aligned lessons in 14 languages, but its **Community Corrections** make it genuinely social. When you finish a writing or speaking exercise, native speakers in the community correct it, and you correct exercises in your own language.

- **Why it is social:** real people give feedback on what you produce.
- **Good for:** beginners who want structure plus human feedback, before they are ready for free conversation.
- **Watch out for:** it is feedback on exercises, not an ongoing conversation. Free members can send a limited number of exercises; Premium is unlimited.

Compare: [LangX vs Busuu](/open-source-alternative-to-busuu).

## 6. Slowly: pen pals, slowly

[Slowly](https://slowly.app) is a pen-pal app where each letter takes time to arrive, depending on the real distance between you, from under an hour to a couple of days. You use a nickname and avatar, and photos or audio are only shared when both people agree. Letters are free and unlimited; Slowly Plus raises quotas.

- **Why it is social:** long, thoughtful letters from real people, often in the language you are learning.
- **Good for:** intermediate and advanced learners who enjoy writing, and introverts who find chat apps stressful.
- **Watch out for:** it is not a learning app. There is no correction tool, and the delay means slow feedback.

Compare: [LangX vs Slowly](/open-source-alternative-to-slowly).

## 7. InterPals: the veteran pen-pal community

[InterPals](https://www.interpals.net) has connected pen pals since 1998 and says it has 8.5 million members. You search by country, city, age and the languages someone speaks or is learning. Profiles, search and messaging are free, and the site is funded by advertising.

- **Why it is social:** a huge, long-established community for friendship and cultural exchange, with language practice as a common reason to join.
- **Good for:** long-term correspondence and meeting people from many countries.
- **Watch out for:** it is not built around learning, so there is no correction tool, and you should say clearly that you want a language exchange.

Compare: [LangX vs InterPals](/open-source-alternative-to-interpals).

## 8. Lingbe: social in real time

[Lingbe](https://www.lingbe.com) skips profiles: press a button and it connects you to a live voice call with a native speaker. You earn call time by helping people learn your language, which keeps things balanced.

- **Why it is social:** it is the most "live" app here, a real voice on the line within seconds.
- **Good for:** speaking and listening practice in spare minutes.
- **Watch out for:** random calls can feel abrupt for beginners, and updates have slowed (the newest iOS version dates from 2024 at the time of writing).

Compare: [LangX vs Lingbe](/open-source-alternative-to-lingbe).

## Also worth knowing

- **ConversationExchange.com** is a long-running website for finding a partner in your own city for face-to-face conversation, or a pen pal. It is dated and has no correction tools, but nothing else does local meetups as simply. See [LangX vs ConversationExchange](/open-source-alternative-to-conversationexchange).
- **italki** is a tutor marketplace with a free Community area where natives correct your posts. It removed its free language-partner search a few years ago, so it is social for feedback but no longer for exchange. See [LangX vs italki](/open-source-alternative-to-italki).
- **Hilokal** centers on live audio rooms and community-led sessions, plus an AI tutor, and is listed on the App Store and Google Play at the time of writing. It is worth a look if group voice is what you want.

## Is Duolingo a social language learning app?

Partly, and it is worth being fair. At the time of writing, Duolingo has real social features: you can follow friends, share a **Friend Streak** with up to five friends, team up on weekly **Friends Quests**, nudge and high-five each other, and compete in weekly **leagues**. For motivation, that is excellent, and Duolingo is very good at getting people to show up.

What it does not have is a person to talk to. There is no messaging between learners, and its old sentence discussion forums no longer take new comments. Speaking practice is with AI characters, such as Video Call with Lily on its paid plans. So Duolingo is social around your progress, not in your practice. Most learners get the best of both by keeping Duolingo for lessons and adding one app from this list for conversation; see [LangX vs Duolingo](/open-source-alternative-to-duolingo) and our guide to [Duolingo alternatives for speaking](/duolingo-alternatives-for-speaking).

## How to choose a social language learning app

- **You want the most people:** HelloTalk.
- **You want one steady partner and in-app calls:** Tandem.
- **You want corrections at the center, no ads, and open source:** LangX.
- **You want to start free, with no subscription at all:** Speaky, InterPals or ConversationExchange.
- **You are a beginner who wants structure plus human feedback:** Busuu.
- **You love writing and hate pressure:** Slowly.
- **You want to speak out loud right now:** Lingbe, or HelloTalk and Tandem's rooms.

Many people use two: a big app to meet people and a quieter one for the partners they stick with. For a wider, exchange-only ranking, see the [best language exchange apps](/best-language-exchange-apps).

## FAQ

### What is a social language learning app?

An app where real people help you learn: language exchange partners, native speakers who correct your writing, pen pals or live voice rooms. Leaderboards and friend lists alone do not make an app social in this sense.

### What is the best social language app?

For community size, HelloTalk. For focused one-to-one exchange with calls, Tandem. For unlimited corrections, no ads and open source code, LangX. The right one depends on whether you prefer chat, voice or letters.

### Is Duolingo a social app?

It has social features (friends, Friend Streaks, Friends Quests and leagues) but no way to chat with other learners. For practice with real people, pair it with a language exchange app.

### Are social language learning apps free?

Most have a free tier. Speaky, InterPals and Slowly are free at their core; HelloTalk and Tandem are free with ads and daily caps; LangX is free with no ads, unlimited replies and corrections, and 5 new conversations a day.

### Are social language apps safe?

About as safe as any social app. Keep chats inside the app until you trust someone, share few personal details early, and block and report freely. Apps with profile review or identity checks, like Tandem and Speaky, add a layer of protection.
