Good language exchange conversation topics are ones you can talk about from your own life: yourself, food, daily routine, travel, work, culture and your plans. Below are 200 ready-to-use questions, grouped by level (beginner, intermediate, advanced) and by theme, plus role-plays, conversation games, the phrases that keep a chat moving and a session plan you can copy, so you never hit that "so... what do you want to talk about?" silence again.

## Beginner conversation questions

These use simple present and past tenses and everyday vocabulary. If you are just starting, it helps to know the [most common words](/tools/most-common-words) in your target language first; the first few hundred cover a surprising amount of daily conversation.

### About you

1. Where are you from, and where do you live now?
2. Do you work or study? What do you do?
3. How many languages do you speak?
4. Why are you learning my language?
5. What is your favorite word in your own language?
6. When is your [birthday](/tools/say/birthday), and how do you usually celebrate it?
7. What does your name mean, and who chose it?
8. What three words describe you best?

### Family and friends

9. Do you have brothers or sisters?
10. Who in your family are you closest to?
11. Do you have any pets? What are their names?
12. How did you meet your best [friend](/tools/say/friend)?
13. Does your family live near you?
14. What do you usually do with your friends on the weekend?
15. Who is the funniest person in your family?

Family vocabulary varies more than you might expect between languages; we collected examples in [family words in different languages](/family-words-in-different-languages).

### Food and drink

16. What did you eat for [breakfast](/tools/say/breakfast) today?
17. What is a typical dish from your country?
18. Do you prefer [coffee](/tools/say/coffee) or tea?
19. What food do you not like at all?
20. Can you cook? What is your best dish?
21. What should I order if I visit a restaurant in your city?
22. What is your favorite snack?
23. What time do people usually eat [dinner](/tools/say/dinner) where you live?

### Daily life

24. What time do you usually wake up?
25. What is the first thing you do in the [morning](/tools/say/morning)?
26. What does a normal day look like for you?
27. How do you get to work or school?
28. What do you usually eat for [lunch](/tools/say/lunch)?
29. Are you a morning person or a night person?
30. What did you do last weekend?
31. What are you doing after this conversation?

### Home and neighborhood

32. Do you live in a house or an apartment?
33. Who do you live with?
34. What is your favorite room in your home?
35. What can you see from your [window](/tools/say/window)?
36. Is there a good [park](/tools/say/park) or café near your home?
37. What is your city famous for?

### Weather and seasons

38. What is the weather like where you are right now?
39. What is your favorite [season](/tools/say/season), and why?
40. Does it snow where you live?
41. What do you do when it [rains](/tools/say/rain)?
42. How hot does it get in [summer](/tools/say/summer)?
43. What is the best month to visit your country?

### Free time

44. What do you do in your free time?
45. What kind of [music](/tools/say/music) do you listen to?
46. What is your favorite [movie](/tools/say/movie)?
47. Do you play any sports?
48. What is the last [book](/tools/say/book) you read?
49. Do you like the beach or the mountains more?
50. Do you play an instrument, or would you like to?
51. Which app do you use most on your phone?

### Shopping and clothes

52. Where do you usually buy food?
53. Do you like shopping, or do you find it boring?
54. What is the last thing you bought?
55. Do you prefer shopping online or in a [shop](/tools/say/shop)?
56. What do people wear to work in your country?
57. What is your favorite [color](/tools/say/color) to wear?

### Animals and nature

58. What is your favorite [animal](/tools/say/animal)?
59. Are there any animals that scare you?
60. Do you prefer [cats](/tools/say/cat) or [dogs](/tools/say/dog)?
61. Is there a [river](/tools/say/river), a lake or the sea near where you live?
62. Do you have a [garden](/tools/say/garden) or any plants at home?

Pets and places are easy to show as well as tell. For the words themselves, see [animal words](/animal-words-in-different-languages) and [nature words](/nature-words-in-different-languages) in different languages.

### This or that

Quick questions for warming up or filling a silence. The rule: every answer comes with a reason.

63. Sweet or salty?
64. City or countryside?
65. Books or films?
66. Call or text?
67. Cooking at home or eating out?
68. Plane, train or car?
69. A big party or a quiet dinner with friends?
70. Sunrise or sunset?

## Intermediate conversation questions

These invite stories, comparisons and opinions, which means more past tenses, conditionals and connecting words ("because," "although," "so").

### Travel

71. What is the best trip you have ever taken?
72. If you could [travel](/tools/say/travel) anywhere next month, where would you go?
73. What should a tourist never do in your country?
74. Have you ever had a problem while traveling? What happened?
75. Which place in your country do tourists miss but shouldn't?
76. Do you plan every detail of a trip, or do you just go?
77. What is the strangest food you have tried abroad?
78. What three things do you always pack?

### Work and study

79. What was your favorite subject at [school](/tools/say/school), and why?
80. What is the hardest part of your job or studies?
81. What did you want to be when you were a child?
82. What does a good boss or teacher do differently from a bad one?
83. Would you rather [work](/tools/say/work) from home or in an office?
84. What was your first job, and what did you learn from it?
85. How is a job interview in your country different from one in mine?
86. What skill do you use every day that school never taught you?

### Culture and traditions

87. What is the most important holiday in your country, and how do people celebrate it?
88. What do people in your country do that surprises foreigners?
89. Is there a gesture in your culture that means something different elsewhere?
90. What superstitions are common where you are from?
91. How do people greet each other where you live: a handshake, a kiss, a bow?
92. What song or story does every child in your country know?
93. What happens at a typical [wedding](/tools/say/wedding) in your country?
94. Is it rude to be late in your culture? How late is "late"?

### Food culture

95. What dish reminds you of your childhood?
96. Who taught you to cook, if anyone did?
97. What dish from your country do foreigners usually not like?
98. Is there a food rule in your country that visitors often break?
99. How do people split the bill at a restaurant where you live?
100. What food from another country has become popular where you live?

### Language learning

101. What is the most difficult thing about learning my language?
102. Which word in my language do you find funny or strange?
103. Is there a word in your language that has no good translation?
104. What mistakes do learners of your language usually make?
105. How do you practice when you do not have a partner?
106. What slang do young people in your country use right now?
107. Is there a sound in my language that you find hard to pronounce?
108. What was the first sentence you ever learned in a foreign language?

Question 103 is a favorite; if you want to get it started, share one from our list of [beautiful words in different languages](/beautiful-words-in-different-languages).

### Media and entertainment

109. What series are you watching at the moment?
110. Can you recommend a singer or band from your country?
111. Which movie from your country should everyone see?
112. Do you follow any creators or podcasts? Who?
113. What video game, board game or card game do you like?
114. Do you watch foreign films with subtitles or dubbed?
115. Which famous person from your country would you most like to meet?

### Memories and experiences

116. What is your happiest childhood memory?
117. What is the bravest thing you have ever done?
118. Tell me about a time you got lost.
119. What is the best gift you have ever received?
120. What was the last thing that made you laugh out loud?
121. Tell me about a teacher who made a difference to you.
122. What is the funniest mistake you have made in a foreign language?
123. What is the best day you remember from the last year?

### Health and sport

124. What do you do to stay healthy?
125. Do you support a sports team? How did you choose it?
126. What do people in your country do when they catch a cold?
127. How many hours do you sleep, and is it enough?
128. Have you ever tried a sport that was harder than it looked?
129. What [healthy](/tools/say/healthy) habit did you start and actually keep?

### Friendship and relationships

130. How do adults usually make new friends where you live?
131. What makes someone a good friend?
132. Do you still keep in touch with friends from school?
133. Is it normal to visit a friend's home without an invitation where you live?
134. Have you ever made a friend while traveling?

### Plans and dreams

135. What is one thing you want to learn this year?
136. Where do you see yourself living in ten years?
137. If you had a free year and enough [money](/tools/say/money), what would you do?
138. What is your biggest [dream](/tools/say/dream)?
139. What have you always wanted to try but never have?
140. What would you like to be able to say in my language by the end of the year?

## Advanced conversation questions

These ask for opinions, arguments and hypotheticals. They are where you practice disagreeing politely, hedging ("it depends," "to some extent") and explaining abstract ideas.

### Society and change

141. How has your city changed in the last ten years?
142. What does your generation do differently from your parents' generation?
143. Is it easier or harder to be young today than it was 30 years ago?
144. If you could fix one problem in your country, which would it be?
145. How do people in your country feel about tourism?
146. What does "success" mean in your culture? Do you agree with it?
147. Is it better to live in a big city or a small town, and does the answer change with age?
148. Which tradition in your country do you think will disappear, and should it?

### Technology

149. Has social media brought people closer together or pushed them apart?
150. Would you trust a self-driving car?
151. How do you think AI will change the way people learn languages?
152. What technology could you not live without, and what could you easily give up?
153. Should children have smartphones? From what age?
154. Should there be stricter limits on what companies can do with personal data?
155. If machine translation became perfect, would people still learn languages?

### Work, money and life

156. Is it better to have a job you love or a job that pays well?
157. Would you take a four-day week for less pay?
158. Would you ever move abroad for work?
159. What does a good work-life balance look like to you?
160. Do people in your country talk about money openly, or not at all?
161. Is it a good idea to work with friends or family?

### Education

162. What would you change about the school system in your country?
163. Should university be free?
164. Is homework useful, or a waste of time?
165. What is something important that school never taught you?
166. Should schools teach people how to manage money: taxes, saving, debt?
167. Is it better to learn a language in a classroom or by living abroad?

### Environment

168. What does your country do well for the environment, and what does it do badly?
169. What small change have you made for the environment, and was it worth it?
170. Would you give up flying to reduce your carbon footprint?
171. Who should do more for the environment: governments, companies or individuals?
172. How has the weather where you live changed since you were a child?
173. Would you pay more for things that are made to last?

### Ethics and dilemmas

174. If you found a wallet full of cash, what would you do?
175. Is it ever right to lie to protect someone's feelings?
176. Would you tell your boss about a serious mistake made by a colleague you like?
177. Should zoos exist?
178. Do famous people deserve privacy?
179. Is it wrong to download films or music without paying?
180. Should you always follow a rule, even when it seems pointless?

### Ideas and hypotheticals

181. If you could live in any period of history, which would you choose?
182. If you could instantly master one more [language](/tools/say/language), which would it be and why?
183. Would you rather know the future or change the past?
184. What is a common belief that you think is wrong?
185. What is the best piece of advice you have ever received?
186. If you could have dinner with anyone, living or dead, who would it be?
187. If you had to move to another country for the rest of your life, which would you choose?

### Language and identity

188. Do you feel like a different person when you speak another language?
189. Should a country have one official language or several?
190. Are regional dialects and accents disappearing where you live?
191. What part of your culture is hardest to explain to outsiders?
192. What is a stereotype about your country that is partly true, and one that is completely false?
193. Is it good for the world that English has become a common language?
194. Has your language borrowed any words from mine? Which ones?

### Big questions

195. What have you changed your mind about in the last few years?
196. What makes a place feel like [home](/tools/say/home)?
197. Is happiness something you find or something you make?
198. Which matters more to you: [freedom](/tools/say/freedom) or security?
199. What would you like people to remember about you?
200. What have you learned about your own language from teaching it to me?

## Role-play scenarios

Questions practice talking about your life. Role-plays practice the situations you will actually be in when you use the language: ordering, complaining, explaining a problem. Let the native speaker play the local role, use the register a real person would use (formal "you" with a receptionist, informal with a friend), then swap.

| Scenario | Level | What it practices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordering at a café or restaurant | Beginner | Food words, polite requests |
| Asking for directions | Beginner | Places, left and right, "how far?" |
| Checking into a hotel | Beginner | Dates, numbers, spelling your name |
| Buying a train ticket | Beginner | Times, prices, "one way or return?" |
| Returning something to a shop | Intermediate | Explaining a problem, past tense |
| Seeing a doctor | Intermediate | Body words, describing symptoms |
| Calling about a late delivery | Intermediate | Complaining politely, phone language |
| Dinner with your partner's family | Intermediate | Compliments, small talk, table manners |
| A job interview | Advanced | Talking about yourself formally |
| Negotiating rent with a landlord | Advanced | Persuading, conditions, compromise |
| Asking a neighbor to keep the noise down | Advanced | Polite disagreement, staying calm |
| Giving a toast at a wedding | Advanced | Storytelling, formal register, humor |

Five minutes is enough for most scenarios. Afterwards, ask your partner what a real person would have said differently; the answer is usually a phrase no textbook taught you.

## Conversation games

Games are useful when energy drops, at meetups with more than two people, and for beginners who cannot yet hold a long conversation.

- **Two truths and a lie.** Say three things about yourself; your partner asks questions to find the lie. It forces questions and answers in the past tense.
- **Describe it without saying it.** Pick a word and describe it until your partner guesses. It is the best practice there is for the moment you forget a word in real life.
- **Twenty questions.** Think of a person, an animal or an object; your partner has twenty yes-or-no questions to find it.
- **Story chain.** Take turns adding one sentence to a story. Agree on the first line, then see where it goes.
- **Photo tour.** Open the last photo on your phone and describe it. Your partner asks five questions about it.
- **Untranslatable words.** Each of you explains a word from your own language that has no direct translation. Our list of [beautiful words in different languages](/beautiful-words-in-different-languages) has plenty to start with.

## Phrases that keep a conversation going

Most conversations do not stop because you run out of topics. They stop when you do not understand something, cannot find a word or need a second to think. Learn these in your target language before your first session, or ask your partner to write them down for you.

| When you want to… | Learn how to say… |
| --- | --- |
| Hear something again | "Could you say that again, more slowly?" |
| Find a missing word | "How do you say … ?" or "What is the word for … ?" |
| Buy time to think | "Let me think…" or "How can I put this…" |
| Check your sentence | "Did I say that correctly?" |
| Sound more natural | "How would a native speaker say that?" |
| Disagree politely | "I see what you mean, but…" |
| Ask for an example | "Can you give me an example?" |
| Change the subject | "That reminds me…" or "By the way…" |
| End the session warmly | "I have to go soon, but this was really fun." |

## Topics to handle with care

Some topics, such as politics, religion, money, relationships and appearance, can be great conversations with the right partner and uncomfortable with the wrong one. What counts as sensitive also changes between cultures: asking about someone's salary is ordinary in some countries and rude in others.

<Callout type="info">
It is completely fine to say early on which subjects you would rather skip, and to ask before starting one: "Is it OK if we talk about politics?" A partner who respects the answer is a partner worth keeping.
</Callout>

## Tips for keeping the conversation going

A list of questions gets you started. These habits keep you going:

- **Go deeper, not wider.** Three follow-ups on one question beat ten questions answered in one line each. "Why?", "Can you give me an example?" and "What would you do differently?" work on almost anything.
- **Share, do not just ask.** An exchange is not an interview. Answer the question yourself, with a detail or a story.
- **Use photos and voice notes.** A picture of your breakfast answers question 16 better than a paragraph, and a voice note turns any question into speaking practice.
- **Save the words you needed.** Every topic shows you a handful of words you did not know. Write them down, ask your partner to check them, and use them in the next session.
- **Revisit topics as you improve.** Answering the same question three months later, in more detail and with fewer mistakes, is one of the most satisfying ways to see your progress.

## Where to use these topics

Any exchange format works, and each suits the questions a little differently:

- **Text chat.** One question per message, a long answer, and time to correct it properly. Best for intermediate and advanced questions, where you want to stretch your grammar.
- **Voice notes.** Answer a question in about a minute without a script. It is speaking practice without the pressure of a live call.
- **Video calls.** Questions, role-plays and games all work, and you can follow the one-hour plan above.
- **Meetups.** Games and "this or that" questions work best in groups. Our guide to [language exchange meetups](/language-exchange-meetups) has more ideas.

## FAQ

### What should I talk about in my first language exchange?

Start with the "About you" and "Food and drink" questions. They are easy to answer at any level, and they give you plenty to ask follow-up questions about.

### How many topics should I prepare for a language exchange?

Two or three per session is enough. Most good conversations drift away from the plan, which is a sign it is working.

### What are good conversation topics for beginners?

Yourself, your family, food, your daily routine, your city and your hobbies. They use high-frequency words and simple tenses, and you can prepare the vocabulary in advance. Quick "this or that" questions are also good, because a one-word answer plus a reason is already a conversation.

### How do I practice advanced conversation in a language exchange?

Choose opinion and hypothetical questions, such as the technology, education, ethics and "would you rather" topics above, and ask your partner to correct not just mistakes but phrasing that sounds unnatural. Role-plays like a job interview or a rent negotiation practice the formal register most learners never get to use.

### What topics should I avoid in a language exchange?

None are off limits, but politics, religion, money and relationships are best saved until you know your partner, and only if you both want to. Ask before you start one, and say early on if there is something you would rather skip.

### What can I do when the conversation runs out?

Switch format instead of searching for another question: a role-play, a quick game such as two truths and a lie, or a few "this or that" questions will restart it. Going back to an earlier answer with "Why?" or "What happened next?" works too.

### Can I use these questions in any language?

Yes. They are written in English, but every question works in any language. Translate the ones you want to use before the session and ask your partner to check your version.
