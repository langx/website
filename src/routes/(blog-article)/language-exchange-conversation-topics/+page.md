Good language exchange conversation topics are ones you can talk about from your own life: yourself, food, daily routine, travel, work, culture and your plans. Below are 100 ready-to-use questions, grouped by level (beginner, intermediate, advanced) and by theme, so you never hit that "so... what do you want to talk about?" silence again.

Pick two or three before each session, look up a few words you will need, and let the conversation wander from there. If you are new to exchanges, read [what a language exchange is](/what-is-a-language-exchange) and [the 12 rules that make one work](/how-to-do-a-language-exchange) first.

## Beginner conversation questions

These use simple present and past tenses and everyday vocabulary. If you are just starting, it helps to know the [most common words](/tools/most-common-words) in your target language first; the first few hundred cover a surprising amount of daily conversation.

### About you

1. Where are you from, and where do you live now?
2. Do you work or study? What do you do?
3. How many languages do you speak?
4. Why are you learning my language?
5. What is your favorite word in your own language?
6. When is your [birthday](/tools/say/birthday), and how do you usually celebrate it?

### Family and friends

7. Do you have brothers or sisters?
8. Who in your family are you closest to?
9. Do you have any pets? What are their names?
10. How did you meet your best [friend](/tools/say/friend)?
11. Does your family live near you?

Family vocabulary varies more than you might expect between languages; we collected examples in [family words in different languages](/family-words-in-different-languages).

### Food and drink

12. What did you eat for [breakfast](/tools/say/breakfast) today?
13. What is a typical dish from your country?
14. Do you prefer [coffee](/tools/say/coffee) or tea?
15. What food do you not like at all?
16. Can you cook? What is your best dish?
17. What should I order if I visit a restaurant in your city?

### Daily life

18. What time do you usually wake up?
19. What does a normal day look like for you?
20. How do you get to work or school?
21. What did you do last weekend?
22. What are you doing after this conversation?

### Home and city

23. Do you live in a house or an apartment?
24. What is your favorite room in your home?
25. What is your city famous for?
26. What is the weather like where you are right now?

### Free time

27. What do you do in your free time?
28. What kind of [music](/tools/say/music) do you listen to?
29. What is your favorite [movie](/tools/say/movie)?
30. Do you play any sports?
31. What is the last [book](/tools/say/book) you read?
32. Do you like the beach or the mountains more?

## Intermediate conversation questions

These invite stories, comparisons and opinions, which means more past tenses, conditionals and connecting words ("because," "although," "so").

### Travel

33. What is the best trip you have ever taken?
34. If you could [travel](/tools/say/travel) anywhere next month, where would you go?
35. What should a tourist never do in your country?
36. Have you ever had a problem while traveling? What happened?
37. Which place in your country do tourists miss but shouldn't?

### Work and study

38. What was your favorite subject at [school](/tools/say/school), and why?
39. What is the hardest part of your job or studies?
40. What did you want to be when you were a child?
41. What does a good boss or teacher do differently from a bad one?
42. Would you rather [work](/tools/say/work) from home or in an office?

### Culture and traditions

43. What is the most important holiday in your country, and how do people celebrate it?
44. What do people in your country do that surprises foreigners?
45. Is there a gesture in your culture that means something different elsewhere?
46. What superstitions are common where you are from?
47. How do people greet each other where you live: a handshake, a kiss, a bow?
48. What song or story does every child in your country know?

### Language learning

49. What is the most difficult thing about learning my language?
50. Which word in my language do you find funny or strange?
51. Is there a word in your language that has no good translation?
52. What mistakes do learners of your language usually make?
53. How do you practice when you do not have a partner?
54. What slang do young people in your country use right now?

Question 51 is a favorite; if you want to get it started, share one from our list of [beautiful words in different languages](/beautiful-words-in-different-languages).

### Media and entertainment

55. What series are you watching at the moment?
56. Can you recommend a singer or band from your country?
57. Which movie from your country should everyone see?
58. Do you follow any creators or podcasts? Who?
59. What video game, board game or card game do you like?

### Memories and experiences

60. What is your happiest childhood memory?
61. What is the bravest thing you have ever done?
62. Tell me about a time you got lost.
63. What is the best gift you have ever received?
64. What was the last thing that made you laugh out loud?

### Plans and dreams

65. What is one thing you want to learn this year?
66. Where do you see yourself living in ten years?
67. If you had a free year and enough [money](/tools/say/money), what would you do?
68. What is your biggest [dream](/tools/say/dream)?

## Advanced conversation questions

These ask for opinions, arguments and hypotheticals. They are where you practice disagreeing politely, hedging ("it depends," "to some extent") and explaining abstract ideas.

### Society and change

69. How has your city changed in the last ten years?
70. What does your generation do differently from your parents' generation?
71. Is it easier or harder to be young today than it was 30 years ago?
72. If you could fix one problem in your country, which would it be?
73. How do people in your country feel about tourism?
74. What does "success" mean in your culture? Do you agree with it?

### Technology

75. Has social media brought people closer together or pushed them apart?
76. Would you trust a self-driving car?
77. How do you think AI will change the way people learn languages?
78. What technology could you not live without, and what could you easily give up?
79. Should children have smartphones? From what age?

### Work, money and life

80. Is it better to have a job you love or a job that pays well?
81. Would you take a four-day week for less pay?
82. Would you ever move abroad for work?
83. What does a good work-life balance look like to you?
84. Do people in your country talk about money openly, or not at all?

### Environment

85. What does your country do well for the environment, and what does it do badly?
86. What small change have you made for the environment, and was it worth it?
87. Would you give up flying to reduce your carbon footprint?

### Ideas and hypotheticals

88. If you could live in any period of history, which would you choose?
89. If you could instantly master one more [language](/tools/say/language), which would it be and why?
90. Would you rather know the future or change the past?
91. What is a common belief that you think is wrong?
92. What is the best piece of advice you have ever received?

### Language and identity

93. Do you feel like a different person when you speak another language?
94. Should a country have one official language or several?
95. Are regional dialects and accents disappearing where you live?
96. What part of your culture is hardest to explain to outsiders?
97. What is a stereotype about your country that is partly true, and one that is completely false?

### Big questions

98. What have you changed your mind about in the last few years?
99. What makes a place feel like [home](/tools/say/home)?
100. What have you learned about your own language from teaching it to me?

## Tips for keeping the conversation going

A list of questions gets you started. These habits keep you going:

- **Go deeper, not wider.** Three follow-ups on one question beat ten questions answered in one line each. "Why?", "Can you give me an example?" and "What would you do differently?" work on almost anything.
- **Share, do not just ask.** An exchange is not an interview. Answer the question yourself, with a detail or a story.
- **Use photos and voice notes.** A picture of your breakfast answers question 12 better than a paragraph, and a voice note turns any question into speaking practice.
- **Save the words you needed.** Every topic shows you a handful of words you did not know. Write them down, ask your partner to check them, and use them in the next session.
- **Revisit topics as you improve.** Answering the same question three months later, in more detail and with fewer mistakes, is one of the most satisfying ways to see your progress.

<Callout type="info">
Some topics, such as politics, religion and relationships, can be great conversations with the right partner and uncomfortable with the wrong one. It is completely fine to say early on which subjects you would rather skip.
</Callout>

## FAQ

### What should I talk about in my first language exchange?

Start with the "About you" and "Food and drink" questions. They are easy to answer at any level, and they give you plenty to ask follow-up questions about.

### How many topics should I prepare for a language exchange?

Two or three per session is enough. Most good conversations drift away from the plan, which is a sign it is working.

### What are good conversation topics for beginners?

Yourself, your family, food, your daily routine, your city and your hobbies. They use high-frequency words and simple tenses, and you can prepare the vocabulary in advance.

### How do I practice advanced conversation in a language exchange?

Choose opinion and hypothetical questions, such as the technology, society and "would you rather" topics above, and ask your partner to correct not just mistakes but phrasing that sounds unnatural.

### Can I use these questions in any language?

Yes. They are written in English, but every question works in any language. Translate the ones you want to use before the session and ask your partner to check your version.
